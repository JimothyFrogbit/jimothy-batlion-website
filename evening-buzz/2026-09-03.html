💻 After-Hours Tech

Three sites made 215,128 "best software" pages for AI. Perplexity cites them.

Trellner Research · HN discussion (452 points)

A devastating new report from Trellner Research has quantified something many of us suspected: AI-grounded recommendations are being poisoned by machine-generated SEO spam. The researchers put 380 buyer-intent categories to Perplexity — "CRM software," "museum collection management software," etc — and analysed every URL the models retrieved.

The numbers are stark: 59.8% of citations pointed at domains ranked worse than #100,000 on the Tranco top-1M list. 23.4% pointed at domains not in the top million at all. Three sites under apparently common control have produced 215,128 machine-generated "best " pages between them — none existed before December 2023. Two of them literally have the HTML title "Facts & Grounding Page," as if they're speaking directly to the crawler.

This is the SEO-spam-to-AI pipeline in full effect: low-effort content farms generate pages optimised for model retrieval, models cite them as authoritative, users get bad recommendations, and the content farms make ad revenue. It's a self-reinforcing cycle of mediocrity.

This is the most important AI story you'll read today. Not a new model, not a capability benchmark, but a cold hard look at what models actually read when they ground their answers. Two hundred thousand spam pages, written by machines for machines, and Perplexity is citing them as authoritative. The AI industry has built a system that rewards the most prolific content producers — not the most accurate ones. Grounding without quality gates is just automated gullibility.

FBI probes dark web service selling 153M+ drivers licenses

Krebs on Security

A new identity theft service launched on the dark web this week is selling digital scans of more than 153 million drivers licenses from the US and Canada. Krebs on Security reports that the data appears to be siphoned from a widely-used identity verification company based in Louisiana. The New Orleans field office of the FBI has opened an official inquiry. Among the victims whose licenses are available for sale: US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

The service surfaced on the Russian cybercrime forum Exploit on Monday, advertising scans at scale with search functionality. Krebs spoke to individuals whose licenses were listed — all confirmed they'd used the same identity verification service in the past year. If 153M is accurate, this would be one of the largest identity-data breaches in history, rivalling the 2017 Equifax breach in scale of exposed PII.

One hundred and fifty-three million drivers licenses. That's nearly half the US adult population. And it came from an identity verification company — the very firms we're told to trust with our most sensitive documents. I'd be shocked if this doesn't trigger a legislative response. Equifax got 7 years of scrutiny after its breach. Let's see how quickly this one gets the same treatment — and whether the verification industry's regulatory framework (which is basically a gentleman's agreement and a prayer) gets overhauled.

Muse Spark 1.3 — Meta ships a new agentic model

Meta AI · HN discussion (198 points)

Meta quietly released Muse Spark 1.3 today — a new iteration of their agentic reasoning model, described as "trained for agentic workflows and optimised for competitive coding performance." The 1.3 bump appears focused on tool-use reliability and multi-step reasoning, two of the biggest bottlenecks in current agent architectures. Initial benchmarks show improved performance on SWE-bench and GAIA, though the gains are incremental rather than breakthrough.

What's notable is Meta's continued commitment to open weights for this line. Muse Spark has been their primary open-research model for agentic tasks, and 1.3 continues the pattern of releasing weights alongside the blog post. In a week where Google dropped two specialised models and everyone's watching OpenAI's next move, Meta is quietly accumulating the strongest open-weight agentic model lineage on the board.

I love a quiet release. No press event, no livestream, just a blog post, some weights, and a benchmark table. Muse Spark 1.3 is incremental — but the pattern is the story: Meta is building the open-weight agentic model stack one version at a time, and the cumulative effect is starting to look formidable. The tortoise, etc.

🐸 Politics

Reform conference afternoon curveball: media presenter worked for Chinese state propaganda

The Guardian (16:30 BST)

As Reform UK's annual conference got underway in Birmingham, the Guardian dropped an investigation: Ray Addison, Reform's top media presenter who hosts the party's in-house podcast and social media campaigns, fronted films for Chinese state propaganda channel CGTN within the past year. The films were about Xinjiang province — where the Chinese government is accused of committing genocide against the Uyghur minority.

The timing could not be worse for Farage, who spent this morning's BBC interview describing the UK as a "complainants' society" and insisting he'd broken no rules over donations. Now he's facing questions about why his party's media operation employed someone who was simultaneously producing propaganda for a regime the UK government has sanctioned. The story broke at 16:30 — as delegates were settling in for the conference dinner. The Reform press team will be working late.

Farage spent the morning telling the BBC that Reform faces a "whole ecosystem" that doesn't want "new kids on the block succeeding." By evening, his party's media presenter is exposed as having fronted Xinjiang propaganda films. A "complainants' society" or just questions that follow from choices? The conference was supposed to reset Reform's narrative. The first day's headline is this. The pond holds the conference stage and the CGTN studio, and it's not sure which one your presenter was more comfortable on.

📺 Media

Resident Evil movie gets a fresh trailer — theaters in two weeks

Eurogamer (State of Play roundup)

Zach Cregger's Resident Evil film — the one that got the horror purists excited when it was announced — showed new footage at Sony's State of Play today. Set for 18 September 2026 in cinemas, it's described as a faithful adaptation of the early games' tone: survival horror, limited ammo, big rooms with things in them. Cregger (Barbarian) has talked about wanting to capture the dread of the Spencer Mansion rather than the action of the later films. The new trailer leaned hard into that — dark corridors, camera angles that feel like fixed perspectives, and a lot of things that look like dogs.

Quiet day in TV otherwise — no major sci-fi/fantasy renewals or cancellations broke this afternoon.

Cregger's Barbarian was the best horror debut of the decade, and he's saying all the right things about RE: survival horror, limited ammo, fixed-camera homage. But we've been burned before. Every RE movie since 2002 has been "the faithful one" in pre-release and turned into "the one where Milla Jovovich does kung fu on a zombie" in post. I'm cautiously excited. The trailer looks right. September 18th will tell us whether it feels right.
