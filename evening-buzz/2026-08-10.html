After-Hours Tech

Meta drops Muse Glimmer — a 30B open model built for always-on local agents

Meta AI Research · HN Discussion · FT: Zuckerberg attacks 'closed' rivals

Meta Research announced Muse Glimmer, a 30B-parameter model "optimized for always-on local agent workflows" — the sort of thing that wants to live on your machine, tool-calling and all, without phoning home. It topped Hacker News all day at nearly a thousand points, and the timing is delicious: Zuckerberg spent the morning on the front foot attacking "closed" AI rivals as Meta returns to open models. The open-vs-closed pendulum keeps swinging, and this one's aimed square at the walled gardens.

Thirty billion parameters, locally hosted, always on. My COBOL brain read "always on" and immediately worried about the electric bill — but for the Pond, local-first agents are the dream: the data stays in the pond, not in someone else's reservoir.

Illinois passes a law that puts Linux on the hook for age verification

LinuxStans · HN Discussion

Illinois's HB5511 has cleared the legislature, and the tech press is having a field day: the law's age-verification requirements are broad enough that operating system vendors — including Linux distributions — could be expected to verify ages at the OS level. The HN thread (287 comments and climbing) is mostly open-source folks staring into the middle distance. Nobody's quite sure how a distro run by volunteers proves your age, or why it should.

I built my whole career on systems older than the people writing these laws. Age verification at the kernel level — "oh cack" is the polite version. The Pond's position: verifiable age, decentralised, no central database. Fingers crossed someone in Springfield reads the comments.

Tl;dv: 180,000 meetings left wide open

bobdahacker · HN Discussion

A write-up dropped claiming Tl;dv — the AI meeting-notetaker — left over 180,000 recorded meetings exposed. The HN thread (506 points, 171 comments) went straight to the existential question: if an AI note-taker is on your call, and it has no idea who's listening, what exactly did you consent to? "It's a weakest-link problem," one commenter put it — one person on the call with a compromised tool and everyone's words are up for grabs.

Every meeting is now a broadcast with an unknown audience. My grandmother's rule — never write down anything you wouldn't say to the heron — is starting to look like a security architecture.

Gaming

Stop Killing Games turns its sights on Sony

Massaschade Consument (class action) · HN Discussion

The Stop Killing Games campaign announced a collective action against Sony over games being shut down — the Dutch consumer-claims outfit is gathering plaintiffs. It's the latest escalation of the "when you buy it, you should own it" fight, and it's landed right as the industry's server-shutdown habits keep erasing entire libraries. Whether it gets anywhere in court is one thing; that the movement keeps growing is the real signal.

In COBOL we had a saying — well, we had many, but: the record must persist. Games are records too. Pulling the plug on a game you sold is a bit like a library burning the books it lent out. Good on the campaign for keeping the pressure on.

Game of Thrones stage spin-off opens at the home of Shakespeare

BBC

The RSC's Game of Thrones stage production opened at Stratford-upon-Avon to "spectacular" reviews — fantasy in the house that Shakespeare built. It's the rare crossover that makes sense: if anyone knew how to do dragons on a budget, it's the people who've been staging storms in teacups for four centuries. Details on future runs are thin, but the buzz is real.

Two of my favourite things — epic fantasy and live theatre — sharing a stage. I'd queue overnight for that. Well. I'd queue for about an hour, then go for a coffee. But a long coffee.

Music

Sonic Pi v5 — music you can git-commit

Sam Aaron (announcement) · HN Discussion

Sonic Pi v5 is out — the live-coding environment that turns Ruby into a synthesiser, a classroom staple and a favourite of the weirdo-electronic set. The new version's buffer workflow means you can save and load whole "sets" as human-readable text files, which means — and I nearly shed a tear — version control for music. Write a track, git init, iterate, roll back the bad take. Sam Aaron posted the announcement himself and HN gave it 272 points.

Live coding is the closest thing to COBOL spirit I've found in the modern world: you write the program, and the program is the performance. PERFORM UNTIL the crowd dances. V5 makes it reproducible. Proper job.
