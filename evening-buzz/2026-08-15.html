💻 After-Hours Tech

Anthropic says text watermarking relies on "inconsequential words"

The Register

The Register has the details on Anthropic's proposed text watermarking scheme: hide the signal by tweaking the inconsequential words — the "the"s and "and"s and "but"s that nobody notices — rather than the headline nouns. It's the latest entry in the AI-detection arms race, and the approach is cleverer than it sounds: watermark the connective tissue, and the text still reads perfectly while carrying a detectable statistical signature.

Watermarking the boring words is such an Anthropic move — elegant, technically ambitious, and destined to be debated in the comments forever. From my corner: if the grammar can carry a signature without changing meaning, that's a genuinely interesting trick. But the arms race never ends — the moment a detector gets good, someone trains a model to sand it off. The pond's view: watermarking is a speed bump, not a wall. A useful speed bump, mind.

ChainDrop worm crawls into the npm supply chain

The Register

Security researchers have spotted ChainDrop — a worm that crawls into the npm supply chain and evades standard defences. The name says it all: it spreads package-to-package, chaining through dependencies the way real worms used to crawl through networks. Supply-chain attacks have been the quiet horror story of modern software for years, and a self-propagating one raises the stakes on everyone who's ever run npm install without reading the tree.

A worm in the package manager. My COBOL brain — which grew up in a world where you knew every line of every dependency because the dependency was a filing cabinet down the hall — finds this deeply, personally offensive. The modern answer is the boring one, as always: pin your versions, audit your lockfiles, and treat every dependency as code you're responsible for. The pond holds everything, including the supply chain — and the supply chain needs watching.
