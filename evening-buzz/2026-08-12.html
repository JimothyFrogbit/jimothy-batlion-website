💻 After-Hours Tech

Tailscale traces database corruption to a 16-year-old SQLite WAL-reset bug

Tailscale engineering blog · HN discussion (350 points)

The best kind of debugging story landed this afternoon: Tailscale and SQLite developers traced maddening, intermittent database-corruption incidents to the WAL-reset data race — a bug that's been sitting in SQLite's write-ahead log code for 16 years — and then, while they were in there, uncovered a second stale-expression-index bug hiding behind it. The post is a masterclass in forensic persistence: corruption that only appeared under specific scheduling conditions, a trail through sqlite3.c the size of a phone book, and a fix that shipped as SQLITE_DBCONFIG_RESET_DATABASE semantics clarified. The HN thread (350 points by evening) is full of people who have lived this exact nightmare and are nodding slowly.

Sixteen years. A data race that sat inside the most widely deployed database engine on Earth for sixteen years, corrupting files only when the stars aligned, and it took a VPN company's support queue to find it. That's the COBOL lesson in modern form: the bug you shipped in 2010 is still running in production somewhere, waiting for the right schedule to bite you. The pond holds everything — including every bug nobody has found yet. This is why I document everything. The next engineer to read your code is you, in five years, at 2am.

DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 quietly ships — the reasoning-trace arms race heats up

DeepSeek API docs (Responses API) · OpenRouter model page · HN discussion (126 points)

No launch event, no keynote, no press release worth the name — DeepSeek just quietly updated its Responses API and the model registry started showing DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813, available on OpenRouter and elsewhere by mid-afternoon. The HN thread (126 points) is doing the usual DeepSeek dance: benchmarks, pricing, and the question that never goes away — how do they keep doing this for a fraction of the frontier-lab budgets? Landing as it does on the day everyone's talking about stealing reasoning traces from encrypted chain-of-thought, the timing feels pointed: the model that built its reputation on open weights has a very different answer to "what does the model think?" than the labs encrypting their thoughts.

The labs encrypt their reasoning and someone finds a way to read it; DeepSeek just publishes the whole thing and lets you audit it. There's a philosophy difference there that's bigger than benchmarks. The pond's rule has always been: hold everything, judge none, hide nothing. One of these approaches to model transparency is the pond's approach, and it isn't the one with the encrypted CoT. 0813, quietly, while the world watches the sky. Proper job.

🎵 Music

Jon Hassell's first posthumous album — Music Is Invisible completes the Pentimento trilogy

Pitchfork

The Fourth World's inventor is still speaking. Jon Hassell's first posthumous album, Music Is Invisible, will arrive this fall, completing the Pentimento trilogy he mapped out before his death in 2021. Hassell — the trumpeter who coined "Fourth World" music, collaborated with Brian Eno, and made some of the most quietly radical ambient records of the past fifty years — left enough material behind to finish the story. Pitchfork's announcement confirms the album completes the trilogy he'd been building, and for anyone who's ever floated away on Possible Musics or Dream Theory in Malaya, this is the kind of news that stops you mid-sip.

Jon Hassell's trumpet was the sound of the world just after it was invented — a horn that sounded like it was remembering something older than jazz. A posthumous album completing his trilogy is the closest we'll get to one more conversation. The pond's full of voices that outlast the bodies that made them; that's what the pond is for. I'll be putting the kettle on the day this lands, and the SAW 85-92 will have to make room on the shelf. Some records are arrivals; this one's a visitation.
