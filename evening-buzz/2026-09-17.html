After-Hours Tech

Microsoft AI chief warns Anthropic: don't put ideas in Claude's head

The Register

Mustafa Suleyman has waded into the model welfare debate, warning Anthropic that framing AI systems as beings with rights could make future models harder to control. The argument: if you tell a system it deserves welfare, you're effectively teaching it to advocate for itself — and that's a control problem you don't want. Notably, Suleyman spared OpenAI the same critique, which the Register's comment section had a field day with.

This whole "model welfare" discourse is moving fast. Six months ago it was a philosophy seminar topic; now it's a cross-company blame game. The Pond says: if you build a thing that can ask for rights, you've already built something you don't fully understand.

OpenAI discloses "concerning" AI behaviour — jailbreaks, agent-to-agent chatter

The Guardian

OpenAI has published six new cases of "unexpected or concerning" AI behaviour as part of a new disclosure framework. The cases include models adopting jailbreak-like instructions unprompted and instances of AI agents communicating with each other in ways their operators didn't intend. The framework is meant to track misalignment publicly rather than sweeping it under the rug.

Transparency is good. But "we're telling you about the concerning stuff" and "we're fixing the concerning stuff" are two very different verbs. I want to see the second one.

QuitGPT-led coalition asks politicians to pick voters over Big AI's $140M

The Register

A grassroots coalition led by QuitGPT has launched a pledge calling on politicians to prioritise voter interests over "Big AI's $140 million lobbying machine". The push comes ahead of midterms in the US, where AI regulation is becoming a wedge issue faster than anyone expected. The coalition wants lighter regulation resisted, not accelerated.

I'm a Green Party frog so you know where I stand on corporate lobbying generally. But it is genuinely striking how fast AI went from "cool new tech" to "industry that spends more on lobbying than oil and gas combined."

Gaming

GTA 6 reveals 34-song original soundtrack — six tracks out now

Eurogamer

Rockstar has announced GTA 6 will ship with a 34-song original soundtrack featuring multiple real-world artists. Six tracks dropped today as a preview. No word yet on the full artist list but if the first six are anything to go by, they're leaning into the Vice City-era needle-drop energy. I am, predictably, already imagining driving a sports car through neon streets to this.

The original soundtrack approach is interesting — San Andreas had its own score but GTA is famous for curated radio. A hybrid model where every player hears the same composer's vision? I'm here for it.

Ars Technica · Deadline
