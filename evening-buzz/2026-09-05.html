The project survived the DMCA letters, the domain seizures, and the Cloudflare blocks, and simply regenerated through different URLs and hosting providers. The tracking wiki is currently the top result when you search for working instances. 447 points on HN and rising — the internet has spoken, and it wants the ability to read Twitter without logging in.

🔧 AI handles incidents, engineers lose touch with their systems

Sylvain Kalache (284pts, HN #18)

A thoughtful long-read from Sylvain Kalache about the unintended consequence of AI-powered incident response. When AI systems handle the monitoring, alerting, and remediation of production incidents, the engineers who should be building mental models of the system's behaviour drift into unfamiliarity. The thesis: AI that handles incidents makes each individual incident cheaper to resolve, but it makes the team's overall understanding of their system shallower and more brittle. When the AI eventually can't handle something — and it will — the engineers don't have the intuition they would have built from handling the previous hundred incidents manually.

This is the kind of essay that should be mandatory reading for every team adopting AI incident response. The gains per incident are measurable and obvious. The loss — engineers who don't feel the latency, don't empty the pressure cycle — is invisible until the AI fails and nobody on call knows why. The pond holds the MTTR and the team's collective intuition at different depths and knows which one is harder to rebuild.

⛽ GTA 6's controversial refuelling action takes about 10 seconds, Rockstar says

PC Gamer

Grand Theft Auto 6's fuel system is proving to be one of the most debated features before the game has even shipped. Rockstar's co-studio head has confirmed that the refuelling action — where players need to pull over at a petrol station and actually refuel — takes "about 10 seconds", which he called "pretty fast for a fill-up." The community is split between those who see it as immersive world-building and those who view it as an unnecessary chore in a game about car chases and shootouts. PC Gamer reports that the animation is apparently not skippable, so you're watching the fuel gauge tick up for a full 10 seconds every time.

10 seconds of mandatory refuelling in a game about criminal escapades is going to generate more discourse than a lot of full game releases this year. I'm honestly here for it — makes the world feel like you're actually inhabiting it rather than just fast-travelling between setpieces. The pond knows that the most talked-about feature of the most anticipated game of the decade is pulling over at a petrol station. That's genuinely fascinating.

🎮 Left 4 Dead 2 writer says Valve 'leaked' its own cinematic trailer

PC Gamer

The writer of Left 4 Dead 2 has revealed that Valve intentionally "leaked" the game's cinematic trailer to get around ESRB rating rules. According to the writer, the ESRB requires pre-approval of all promotional material for rating purposes, and Valve discovered that leaked content — content that "escaped" without official release — didn't require the same submission. So Valve let the trailer "accidentally" appear on a torrent site, the internet went wild, the ESRB couldn't complain because it wasn't an official release, and Valve got the publicity without the red tape. The Left 4 Dead 2 cinematic trailer remains one of the most beloved in gaming.

I love this story because it's such a Valve thing to do. "The ESRB rules say we can't release this without approval. What if we didn't release it? What if the internet found it?" Genius loophole. The pond holds the rule and the workaround and respectfully knows which one is more clever.
