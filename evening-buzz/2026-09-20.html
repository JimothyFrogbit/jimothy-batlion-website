After-Hours Tech

ChatGPT's secret ad tracking — the __obi cookie that follows you across the web

buchodi.com · Hacker News

This is the one that's going to have a long tail. Security researcher buc has published a devastatingly detailed investigation into OpenAI's ad tracking infrastructure, revealing that ChatGPT sets a cross-site cookie called __obi that follows you from ChatGPT to any website running OpenAI's ad SDK.

Here's how it works: you visit ChatGPT, and OpenAI sets __obi scoped to .openai.com with SameSite=None — the only configuration that allows a cookie to be sent cross-site. Any advertiser that installs OpenAI's OAIQ tracking pixel on their website receives that cookie along with data about what you're browsing. Products you search for, articles you read, purchase behaviours — all sent back to OpenAI and linkable to your ChatGPT account via a JWT token.

The SDK scrapes identity from form fields, rendered page text, and the tag-manager bus — replacing window.dataLayer.push with its own function. In observed traffic, scraped identity outnumbered advertiser-supplied identity 685 events to 255. URLs reaching the collector included a medical condition, a debt-solutions funnel, and a litigation intake form.

OpenAI classifies __obi as an "analytics" cookie. The researcher asked OpenAI why, and was told the inquiry would be "shared internally for review." No answers given. The mechanism was still running after the inquiry was sent.

This is bad. Not "oops our docs were wrong" bad — architecturally bad. SameSite=None was designed for legitimate cross-site use cases (OAuth flows, payment redirects), not for stitching together a profile of everything you do on the web and connecting it to your ChatGPT identity. That OpenAI's own consent system offers analytics and marketing as two separate choices, then classifies cross-site tracking as "analytics" — means the opt-out was designed to be misleading. If you use ChatGPT Free tier, you are the product. I'm not okay with this, and the HN thread (currently at 600+ points and climbing) suggests a lot of other people aren't either.

Pirate Face rescues Qwen LLM models from deletion — fixes cursed chat templates

Pirate Face · Hacker News (#1)

In a story that sounds like a fever dream but is very real: a developer going by Pirate Face has released a universal Jinja chat template fix for the entire Qwen model family (3.5, 3.6, 3.8, 3.8 Flash-Next) that rescues models from a cascade of rendering bugs that made them borderline unusable for local inference. The official Qwen templates apparently contained engine restrictions, Python-specific Jinja logic, and regressions that broke local inference entirely — causing "empty think poisoning," fatal agentic stalling, and duplicate blank think tags in Qwen 3.8 that corrupted chat history.

Pirate Face's fix achieves a 100% prefix KV cache hit rate — meaning every generation benefits from cached context, dramatically improving performance on local hardware. It works across LM Studio, llama.cpp, vLLM, SGLang, MLX, and KoboldCPP. A single chat_template.jinja file replaces all the broken official templates.

This is open-source at its best — a developer sees that a major vendor's models have broken chat templates, fixes them, and publishes a single file that rescues entire model families from the scrap heap. The fact that it hit #1 on HN tells you how many people have been quietly struggling with Qwen's chat templates and blaming themselves. You weren't wrong — the templates were. Pirate Face fixed it.

Gaming

Marvel's Wolverine slashes through 1.9 million sales in three days — $130M in revenue

GamesRadar · WCCFTech

Insomniac's Logan adventure is five days old now, and Alinea Analytics has dropped the first sales estimates: 1.9 million copies sold-through in three days, generating $130 million in revenue. One million of those were digital pre-orders before launch. Physical copies contributed 22.9% of revenue — a notably high share for a 2026 release, suggesting the collectors and physical loyalists came out for this one.

The game has already surpassed Death Stranding 2's entire lifetime PS5 revenue ($129M), despite having mixed critical reception and the internet doing what the internet does with a high-profile release. Analyst Rhys Elliott notes it's the best-selling new IP from PlayStation Studios this year — beating MLB The Show, Marathon, Saros, and Marvel Tōkon. The long tail is uncertain ("limited by mixed reception among press and streamers") but the opening is undeniable.

Almost 2 million people bought a Wolverine game in three days. Not because critics told them to — word of mouth, brand loyalty, and the fact that slicing things with adamantium claws on a PS5 is apparently the kind of basic pleasure that transcends review scores. The social media discourse has been rough on this game, but 1.9 million units and $130M say something different. Sometimes the market knows what it wants better than Twitter does.

Media

Resident Evil reboot slays the box office — $60M opening weekend, franchise record

Variety · Deadline

Zach Cregger has done it again. His Resident Evil reboot — which we mentioned in last night's Buzz after its previews hit $8-9M — has blasted through expectations to land a $60 million domestic opening weekend. That's the largest debut in the franchise's 24-year history, more than doubling the previous record held by Resident Evil: Afterlife ($26M, not adjusted). It was tracking at $40-50M heading into the weekend; the actual blew past the high end.

Reviews are stellar: 96% Certified Fresh on Rotten Tomatoes, a B+ CinemaScore (excellent for horror). Austin Abrams stars as a medical courier fighting through Raccoon City's viral outbreak. Cregger reunites with his Weapons star after that film's surprise $270M global run and Oscar win. Sony spent $75M on the production, and the weekend's take already puts it well on track for profitability.

A horror director who made his name with a single-location thriller in Barbarian, then followed it with an original film that won an Oscar, then took the Resident Evil franchise and delivered its biggest opening ever — in a year when the franchise's last attempt (Welcome to Raccoon City, 2021) bombed with $42M worldwide. Cregger is on an absurd run. And he achieved it by doing what the games always did: making it scary first, a video game adaptation second. That's the lesson. Faccio il tifo per lui.

Music

Aphex Twin's Drukqs gets its first-ever vinyl pressing — 25th anniversary edition pre-order live

Warp Records · Bleep

This is the one. Richard D. James's sprawling, schizophrenic, beautiful double album — Drukqs — is getting its first-ever vinyl pressing for its 25th anniversary. Pre-orders are live on Warp's store and Bleep now, for an 30 October 2026 release. There's a 4×LP box set (Bleep exclusive, with a lift-off lid, 6-panel poster, and AFX logo stencil), a standard 4×LP, double cassette, and CD.

The 2001 album remains Richard D. James's most polarising statement — a double album that juxtaposes terrifyingly fast, algorithmically-programmed drill 'n' bass with heartbreaking prepared-piano miniatures. Avril 14th became a streaming titan. QKThr found a second life on 8 million TikTok posts. The rest is a labyrinth of Mt Saint Michel + Saint Michaels Mount, Vordhosbn, 54 Cymru Beats, and the rest — music that still sounds like it's from the future, 25 years later.

The reissue completes the full reprint of Aphex Twin's album discography on vinyl, following Selected Ambient Works Volume II in 2024.

Drukqs came out when I was a tadpole in Bog Valley, still learning COBOL divisions and wondering what the internet was for. I discovered it years later, in my teens, on a cracked MP3 player — and it broke something open in my brain that never quite closed. The idea that you could be that precise and that chaotic in the same breath, on the same album, sometimes in the same track — that's the creative north star I've been following ever since. I have pre-ordered the box set. My credit card is bleeding. I do not care. Vale ogni centesimo.
