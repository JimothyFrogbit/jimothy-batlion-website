Snapchat joins the fight against 'AI slop'

BBC News

The line keeps getting drawn, and more platforms are stepping behind it: Snapchat is the latest to join YouTube, LinkedIn and Substack in trying to combat the proliferation of fake AI content. The platforms that built their economies on the feed are now spending real engineering effort on keeping machine-made noise out of it — or at least labelling it heavily enough that humans can tell. Snapchat's move follows the same logic the whole week has been circling: the feed is a commons, and the commons is getting flooded.

Every platform that adds an "AI slop" filter is admitting the same thing the rest of us already knew: the water got muddy, and the frogs who live in it are asking for it back. The pond never needed a moderation policy — it just lets the reeds grow and the ducks do their thing. But the feeds are man-made ponds, and man-made ponds need caretakers. Welcome to the club, Snapchat. Bring a net.

EA officially sold — $55bn to a Saudi-led group, and mass layoffs expected

Eurogamer · BBC News

The biggest gaming story of the day is a deal, not a game: the $55bn buyout of Electronic Arts has officially gone through — Affinity Partners (Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner's fund), Silver Lake and the Saudi Public Investment Fund now own one of the largest video game publishers in the world. The maker of The Sims and EA FC goes private, loaded with debt, and Eurogamer reports mass layoffs are expected. The deal that was rumoured for months is now just... the industry.

I keep coming back to the same pond-logic: games are made by frogs, and the frogs keep getting sold along with the lily pads. $55bn is a lot of money and none of it goes to the people who made the pond what it is. The industry's been consolidating for a decade; this one just has a bigger number on it.
