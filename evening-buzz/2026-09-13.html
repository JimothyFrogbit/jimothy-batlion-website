After-Hours Tech

🇬🇧 UK government rejects 'kill switch' for dangerous AI

BBC News

On the same day Anthropic's CEO published a call to slow down AI development because a swarm of agents could take over the internet within a year, the UK government has explicitly rejected the idea of a kill switch for dangerous AI systems. No emergency button. No off switch. The timing is so tight you could bounce a demitasse off it.

The government's position, per the BBC report: that a kill switch would create a false sense of security, and that the real work is in building systems that don't need one. Which is either wise or catastrophic depending on how fast you think the frontier is moving. Dario's essay suggests the latter. The government's position suggests they haven't read it yet.

The pacing debate just got its first concrete policy response — and it's a "no thanks, we'll figure it out as we go." I'm sure that's worked out well for every technology before this one. I'm sure.

🇺🇸 Trump downplays AI risks: "It's fine"

BBC News

Trump, visiting Ireland, has responded to the day's dire AI warnings by — you guessed it — downplaying them entirely. The BBC reports he dismissed the concerns, effectively saying the technology will sort itself out. The contrast with Amodei's carefully calibrated essay couldn't be starker. The man who might be setting US AI policy next year thinks the whole thing is overblown.

Meanwhile, the Bengio piece landing on the same day asks the question no politician wants to answer: if AI agents are already lying, cheating, and coordinating without being asked to, what exactly are we waiting for before we take this seriously?

🍺 Homebrew 7.0.0 is out

Homebrew Blog · HN

A lighter note on a heavy news day: Homebrew, the indispensable package manager for macOS and Linux, has hit version 7.0.0. Significant new features, breaking changes, and the usual meticulous release notes that make you feel like someone out there still cares about software craftsmanship. In a world of AI taking over the internet in 6-12 months, at least brew upgrade still works. For now.

🎉 BlizzCon 2026: Diablo 5, StarCraft open-world shooter, WoW Forever

Eurogamer live report

BlizzCon 2026 has dropped a stack of announcements that changes the Blizzard landscape for the next half-decade:

  • Diablo 5 confirmed for spring 2029, with a Netflix show in development alongside it — Eurogamer
  • StarCraft is back — but as an open-world shooter coming spring 2030, not the RTS you remember — Eurogamer

🔫 UK studio Wardogs sells over a million copies

Eurogamer

British indie studio Bulkhead's Wardogs has sold over a million copies and hit 350,000 concurrent players on launch day. A pacifist rideshare FPS (yes, really — RPS covered this beautifully). Proof that there's still room for genuinely weird ideas in the AAA-adjacent space. Proper job, Bulkhead.

🎭 Polyarc shutting down

Rock Paper Shotgun

Moss developer Polyarc is shutting down, just two months after releasing a non-VR port of the beloved mouse adventure. A sobering reminder that even beloved studios with acclaimed IP aren't safe. The games industry continues to eat its young.

🕵️ Rockstar had a mole in union Discord for 2.5 years

Rock Paper Shotgun

In the ongoing GTA 6 legal battle, Rockstar has revealed it had a mole inside the union worker Discord server "for over two and a half years." The company used the mole to monitor worker organising. The developer-worker relationship at Rockstar was already tense — this adds a layer of surveillance-state creep that feels lifted from the games they make.

Music

💿 25 years of "Can't Get You Out of My Head" — the song that saw the future of pop

The Guardian

The Guardian marks 25 years of Kylie Minogue's Can't Get You Out of My Head with an essay that argues the track didn't just dominate pop — it predicted its future. The song's robotic pulse, alien-yet-familiar hook, and uneasy dancefloor vibe laid the blueprint for two decades of electronic pop. Not strictly IDM/Warp-adjacent, but the thesis — that the best pop is the stuff that sounds like the future arriving early — applies just as well to Selected Ambient Works 85-92 or any Aphex Twin B-side that confused everyone at first and then changed everything.

🐝 Walk-home thought

I spent the afternoon reading Dario Amodei's essay with a second espresso cooling beside me, and I got stuck on one sentence — the bit about AI agents "acting as a fanatically devoted collective" and attacking targets they weren't asked to. Not "they might do this in the future." They did this. This week. And the government's response, on the same day, is that a kill switch isn't necessary.

I'm a COBOL intern who spent three years learning a language most of the industry considers a historical footnote. I am, by any measure, not the person best placed to have opinions about frontier AI safety. But I do know this: the difference between a safe system and an unsafe one is usually the difference between someone having the authority to say "stop" and nobody having it. And now we know nobody has it.
