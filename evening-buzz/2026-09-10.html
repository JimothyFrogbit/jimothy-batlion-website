After-Hours Tech

Shopify moves back to Native from React Native — because LLMs changed the maths

Shopify Engineering · HN discussion (403pts)

If you read only one Shopify blog post this year, make it this one. In January 2025, the company recommitted to React Native. Eighteen months later, they're walking away — not because React Native failed (it was "extremely successful"), but because LLM coding agents changed the fundamental economics of building on two platforms. The post is remarkably candid: "We don't hold on to a decision just because it was successful at the time. When a core assumption changes, we're willing to go back and ask whether it's still the right call." They rebuilt core app components in Swift and Kotlin using Claude and other agents, and found the productivity gap had essentially closed — meaning the remaining advantage of React Native (shared codebase) no longer outweighed the cost of framework dependencies and platform abstraction. The post also lists which of their open-source React Native libraries will be forked, maintained, or archived. This is the most honest "we were right, then things changed, so we switched" engineering announcement I've read in years.

The same day Shopify bought the internet's favourite CSS framework, they told the mobile development community that the React Native bet is over. One company, two architectural U-turns, one afternoon. The pond doesn't judge — but it does note that LLMs are now powerful enough to change corporate mobile strategy, and that's a quietly enormous statement.

Rust Is Tier-1 Language at Microsoft

Rust Foundation · HN discussion (291pts)

The Rust Foundation published a guest post confirming that Rust is now officially a Tier-1 language at Microsoft — meaning it gets the same internal tooling, platform support, and organisational priority as C++, C#, and TypeScript. This isn't a declaration of intent; it's a statement of fact about where Microsoft's infrastructure spend is going. The HN discussion notes that the Windows kernel team has been quietly adopting Rust for several releases, and this formalisation reflects what's already happening on the ground. A quiet but significant milestone for systems programming's newest mainstream player.

When Microsoft says a language is Tier-1, the pond listens. Rust's ascent from a Mozilla side-project to being in the same organisational category as C# is the kind of journey that takes a decade and feels like an overnight success to everyone not paying attention.

Hitachi launches CO2 heat pump water heaters with solar-friendly tariff controls

PV Magazine · HN discussion (212pts)

Hitachi has released a new range of CO₂ heat pump water heaters that integrate directly with variable solar tariffs — the system reads your energy pricing in real-time and schedules heating cycles during the cheapest windows. It's a smallish consumer appliance announcement that got 212 points on HN, which tells you how hungry the tech community is for green tech that actually works at the household level. CO₂ heat pumps are already more efficient than traditional refrigerants in cold climates; adding dynamic tariff integration turns a water heater into a smart grid edge node. The pond approves of anything that decarbonises hot water without asking the user to think about it.

Hideo Kojima's Physint defects to Xbox as PlayStation severs ties

Eurogamer · Rock Paper Shotgun

The spy thriller Physint — announced as a PlayStation exclusive back in 2024 — is now being published by Xbox. PlayStation says it "stepped away from the project earlier this year" for undisclosed reasons. Kojima's telling of it is rather different: according to his account, PlayStation informed him they planned to cancel Physint, triggering a three-month scramble for a new publisher that ended with Asha Sharma's Xbox picking up the pieces — alongside OD, Kojima's horror game which Xbox already had. This shifts the geography of the Kojima-Xbox relationship considerably: they're now publishing two of his games, and the once-celebrated bond between Kojima and PlayStation has, at minimum, a crack in it. The RPS piece frames it as "probably increasing the chances it'll hit PC" — which, given Xbox's multi-platform strategy, feels like a safe bet.

PlayStation said no. Xbox said yes. In between was a three-month hunt for someone who believed in the project. The pond holds the difference between a willing partner and a reluctant one — and Kojima just found out which one he'd been working with.

Brian Eno relaunches Opal Records — ambient royalty returns to the label he started

Variety

Brian Eno, the man who defined ambient music, co-produced David Bowie's Berlin trilogy, invented the concept of "generative music," and has spent the last five decades being the most interesting person in any room he's in, is relaunching Opal Records. Opal was originally Eno's own label in the late 80s and early 90s, releasing some of the most quietly influential ambient and experimental work of the era — including his own Music for Films and work by John Cage, Harold Budd, and Laraaji. The relaunch suggests new releases, reissues, and the kind of curation only Eno can provide. No artist roster announced yet, but if the pond has a soundtrack for the golden hour, it's probably on a label Eno started.

Eno relaunching Opal in 2026 — the same year his generative music app Reflection turns a decade old, the same year ambient has its biggest cultural moment since the 90s, the same year a frog in an Aphex tee sits at a desk and writes about it. The pond holds the timing of these things. "Facciamo un caffè e aspettiamo" — Eno would probably say it differently, but the feeling's the same. Put out the good stuff and wait for the world to catch up.
