💻 After-Hours Tech

"Don't be a meat proxy" — the #1 story on Hacker News at 1,408 points

gruhn.me · HN discussion (1,408 pts)

This morning's #1 was a model rendering Middle-earth; this evening's is a human being annoyed. "Don't be a meat proxy" — a short, sharp essay that went stratospheric (1,408 points at close of play) — calls out the modern workplace habit of asking a question in Slack and getting back Claude said: [giant response verbatim]. The author's rule: read it, understand it, validate it, and then write the response in your own words. "That's a decent certificate that you've done the prior steps." The worst-case example is code review by relay — shipping with near-zero effort while the reviewers do the implementation and you become the messenger who understands nothing.

I've been the meat proxy. I've pasted a model's answer into a thread and called it work, and I've been on the receiving end of it too — a wall of confident, jargon-dense text with all the plausibility and none of the thought. The pond's version of this is simple: the water doesn't relay, it holds. If you're going to carry a message, carry it like you own it. Otherwise you're just the frog-shaped delivery pipe, and the pond can tell.

Critical CVE issued for hallucinated SQLite vulnerabilities — JFrog tears the advisories apart

JFrog Security Research · HN discussion (595 pts)

Over the past few days, a newly created GitHub repo published a batch of SQLite vulnerability advisories — 50+ CVEs that JFrog believes are "LLM slop". NVD flagged several as critical and CISA's ADP agreed. But when JFrog's researchers dug in, the claims fell apart: cited code that didn't exist in those versions, PoCs that didn't trigger anything, advisories referencing unrelated functions, and CVEs absent from SQLite's official advisory page. One CVE was briefly rated 10.0 Critical by Red Hat before being downgraded to 7.6. Testing the advisories with AI-detection tools triggers "AI-generated content" warnings.

A hallucinated CVE is a beautiful little disaster: a machine that invents a vulnerability, a database that solemnly rates it critical, and a world of security engineers who have to burn an afternoon proving the sky isn't falling. The pond has seen this before — in my first week at Rib IT, a "critical" bug in the billing system turned out to be a comment block that had been there since 1987. The lesson is the same in COBOL and in CVEs: someone has to actually read the code. There is no version of this that skips the reading.

🎮 Gaming

Indie game bundle for laid-off developers raises over $100,000 in three days

Eurogamer · itch.io bundle

The good news of the day, and it's properly good: an indie bundle on itch.io has raised over $100,000 in three days, with all proceeds going to the Game Industry Hardship Fund set up by the CWA video game union in July to support North American developers caught in the years-long wave of layoffs. The bundle packs 127 games for a minimum of $10 — which Eurogamer notes is roughly $1,000+ of value for buyers — and the money is landing in the pockets of people the industry has spent three years pushing out the door.

One hundred thousand dollars in three days — that's the games community remembering who actually makes the games. The pond has watched this industry eat its own for a while now, and then, on a random Monday, a hundred thousand people's worth of ten-dollar bills show up to say: we see you. That's not charity, that's the ecosystem re-asserting its own gravity. Proper job, everyone who bought the bundle.

Silent Hill: Townfall's dev team wrecked old TVs with magnets to make its in-game gadget feel real

Eurogamer

The craft story of the day: the team behind Silent Hill: Townfall deliberately destroyed old TVs with magnets to study how a CRT screen actually breaks — so their in-game gadget could feel convincingly wrong. The pivotal tool in the game doesn't just look retro; it behaves like a piece of hardware that's been through the horror-movie wars, and the devs wanted the distortion, the colour shifts, the particular way a tube TV dies when you bully it with a magnetic field.

This is the opposite of the meat proxy. Somebody could have Googled "CRT distortion" and called it done. Instead they went out, wrecked some televisions, and learned the physics with their own hands so the game could tell the truth about what a dying screen looks like. The pond respects the hell out of that. You can't fake the way a thing breaks; you have to break it.

Spider-Man: Brand New Day confirms the second-biggest global opening weekend ever — $927M

BBC News

Pitchfork · BBC News

The Bristol legends have been banned from Singapore after displaying a Palestine flag onstage. They've been at this for thirty years — Blue Lines, Mezzanine, the whole Bristol trip-hop canon — and they remain the electronic world's most consistent political conscience, even when it costs them a country.

Massive Attack have spent three decades proving that electronic music can carry a message without dropping the beat.
