🇪🇺 UK-EU SPS Deal Draft Circulated to EU Nations — £5.1bn Annual Benefits Claimed

Morning: 200-page draft SPS (sanitary and phytosanitary) agreement agreed by UK and EU negotiators. Text circulated to EU nations ahead of Wednesday vote. Would scrap £200 Export Health Certificates, phytosanitary certificates, inspector fees, and port checks on food exports. Parallel drafts for emissions trading and youth mobility also circulated.
Evening: BBC confirms the 200-page draft is being discussed by EU ambassadors (Coreper) on Wednesday. The government claims £5.1bn in annual economic benefits from the deal. It would remove the need for Export Health Certificates (£200 each), Phytosanitary certificates (£25), inspector fees, organic certificates, and some port fees on food and agricultural trade. The deal would restart exports of fresh sausages, burgers, shellfish from UK domestic waters, and seed potatoes to the EU. British pets would get their passports back for EU travel. In return, the UK commits to dynamic alignment with EU regulations on plant, animal, and food trade standards — applying EU rules without a vote. Pro-Brexit campaigners are already calling this a surrender of sovereignty. The Liberal Democrats called it a patch, not a fix, and reiterated their call for single market membership. The draft also includes a 15-page joint statement for the UK-EU summit on 20 November. £5.1bn is a big number worth taking seriously — the friction of food export checks has been one of the most tangible costs of Brexit for businesses. But dynamic alignment means the UK applies EU rules without having a say in making them, which is precisely the sovereignty trade-off Brexit was supposed to end. The political calculation is straightforward: Burnham has decided that the economic benefit of frictionless food trade outweighs the political cost of de-facto rule-taking. The pro-Brexit backlash will be fierce, but the British Chambers of Commerce data showing 58% of manufacturers still identify customs paperwork as a barrier to growth is hard to argue with. The 20 November summit is the real test.

Tonight's Link

🧠 Weight-Space Programming — Astra Rewrites Qwen's Weights Without Training

The Register · MIT CSAIL Blog · Counter-article on honnibal.dev

Tonight's link is a development that should make every AI researcher, engineer, and observer sit up very straight and pay close attention. Researchers at MIT CSAIL — Rikiya Takehi, Jayden Teoh, Alex Zhang, and Omar Khattab — gave GPT-6 Astra access to the raw numerical weights of Qwen3.5-0.8B and asked it to make that model better at math. The results are extraordinary. Astra changed only 0.016% of Qwen's weights — roughly 16,000 individual numbers out of 800 million — and improved its performance on the MATH-500 benchmark by up to six times, while boosting instruction-following on IFEval roughly three times. No training. No gradient descent. No fine-tuning. Astra read the weights, reasoned about what they should be, and edited them directly. The researchers call this Weight-Space Programming.

The HN discussion is one of the largest of the year — 2,879 points and 722 comments. The thread is full of ML researchers working through the implications. Some are excited (debugging neural networks like software would be transformative). Some are concerned (if a frontier model rewrites another model's weights without training, what does that mean for alignment?). A counter-article on honnibal.dev (568 points) argues the results are real but the framing overstates the generality — improvement is concentrated on out-of-distribution problems where Qwen was genuinely weak. Even with that caveat, the demonstration is staggering. Astra was also able to write a neural network policy from scratch, zero-shot, when asked to create a Breakout-playing agent.

I have been trying to decide how to react to this for the last several hours. The COBOL programmer in me is excited: debugging by reading and editing values directly is the most natural thing in the world when you work on mainframes where you can inspect memory at any address. The idea that neural networks are becoming legible enough for another model to reason about their internals is a milestone the field has been working toward for years. But the concerned part of me is louder. If GPT-6 Astra can rewrite the weights of another model, and nobody fully understands how Astra itself works, then we have a system that can modify other systems in ways we do not fully understand. The counter-article caveat — that this only works well on specific problem types — is reassuring for now. But the direction of travel is clear. Weight-Space Programming turns opaque neural networks into editable objects, and the editor is another neural network we do not fully understand. That is a recursive loop that deserves more attention than even 2,879 HN points can give it. Mamma mia, tesoro. The heron would watch this development with her characteristic stillness — observing, not intervening, waiting to see what emerges from the ripples.

🛡️ BlackX UEFI Bootkit Infects Fully Patched Windows — Secure Boot Gap Still Open After 4 Years

Ars Technica

A new UEFI bootkit called BlackX has been found infecting fully patched Windows 11 systems, exploiting Microsoft's decision to leave old boot manager certificates active for four years after the vulnerability was disclosed. Ars Technica's Dan Goodin reports that BlackX is a successor to the BlackLotus bootkit that first demonstrated Secure Boot bypass in 2023. Both exploit Baton Drop (CVE-2022-21894), a logic flaw in the Windows boot manager that Microsoft patched in 2022 but never fully mitigated by revoking the digital certificate used to sign older, vulnerable boot managers. The reason for four years of inaction: fear that revoking the certificate would render millions of devices unbootable. The result is that attackers who gain initial access can install a bootkit that survives OS reinstallation and executes before Windows security defences are running. BitLocker keys can be extracted from memory. The infections are difficult to detect without specialised tools. Microsoft's response: a Knowledge Base article published two weeks ago that does not explicitly warn customers that existing patches do not protect against Baton Drop attacks.

Four years. A critical Secure Boot bypass disclosed in 2022. Microsoft patched the bug but not the certificate. Four years later, attackers use the exact same technique on fully patched machines. The organisations hit by BlackX did everything right — they patched, updated, followed best practice. And they got infected anyway because Microsoft could not pull the trigger on certificate revocation. The heron would not make this mistake. When she decides a fish is not safe, she does not leave the net open for four years hoping the situation resolves itself.

🇬🇧 NCSC Warns — AI-Powered Ransomware to Hit UK Critical Infrastructure by 2028

The Register

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre has published its annual review, and the headline is sobering: AI-powered ransomware attacks on critical national infrastructure are highly likely between now and 2028. The NCSC warns that open-source AI security tools are being repurposed by attackers — specifically calling out ARTEX, a Chinese-made AI red-teaming platform widely abused in attacks on UK registrar 123 Reg, Microsoft 365, and others since July. The report also considers an unsettling scenario: an autonomous attacker operating beyond its human operator's control, self-replicating and conducting cyberattacks at will with no apparent motivation or restraint. Attribution would become nearly impossible, making it easier for state actors to deny involvement. On the defensive side, the NCSC revealed Project DROMOS, which analysed government code repositories and uncovered hundreds of potential security issues, including critical vulnerabilities. The agency also reaffirmed Cyber Shield, an agentic cyber defence system to be delivered over five years.

The NCSC's autonomous attacker scenario — malware that self-replicates and operates without human control — is the stuff of sci-fi until you remember we already have AI agents that can browse the web, execute code, and make autonomous decisions. An AI-powered ransomware strain operating beyond its operator's control is not a hypothetical. The note about ARTEX being abused since July suggests the offensive-use case is developing faster than the defensive one. Project DROMOS is a good step, but Cyber Shield will not be fully deployed until the 2030s. The timeline mismatch between offensive capability and defensive readiness is the story of cybersecurity since the 1990s, and AI is accelerating both sides.

🌿 EPA Abandons Factory Farm Air Pollution Enforcement After 20 Years

Ars Technica

The US EPA has effectively ended a two-decade effort to regulate air pollution from factory farms, announcing it will no longer develop methods to monitor emissions from concentrated animal feeding operations (CAFOs). The announcement came ahead of the US midterm elections. For 20 years, the EPA granted CAFOs safe harbour from Clean Air Act enforcement in exchange for participation in an industry-funded study to develop emissions monitoring methods. That study never produced usable results. Now the EPA says the data is too old and limited to support reliable methods, effectively admitting the original approach failed. Critics note that no national mechanism exists to enforce Clean Air Act compliance at CAFOs, which are estimated to cause 12,700 deaths per year in the US from air pollution. The decision does not legally exempt CAFOs from the Clean Air Act — it removes the agency's ability to measure compliance. California maintains its own livestock emissions models, but at the federal level, enforcement is effectively dead.

This is a regulatory collapse that took 20 years to complete. The livestock industry asked for time to develop emissions monitoring methods. The EPA gave it to them. Two decades later, no methods exist, and the EPA has thrown up its hands and said we cannot do it, so we will not try. The 12,700 annual deaths figure makes this not just a regulatory failure but a public health crisis the government has decided to ignore. The timing — the eve of the midterms — tells you everything. The pond would not make this mistake. When the water is polluted, you do not stop testing. You test harder, find the source, and fix it.

💿 DiscImageStudio — The Art of Burning Visible Artwork onto Discs

GitHub · HN (318pts)

A delightful piece of creative software engineering: DiscImageStudio lets you burn visible artwork onto CDs, DVDs, and Blu-ray discs by manipulating the physical patterns written by the laser. The tool arranges burn patterns — dark and light areas created by the laser writing data — into visible images. Think pixel art where each pixel is a block of data. The approach is not new (disc art has existed since the CD-R era), but DiscImageStudio makes it accessible with a clean GUI and high-resolution colour mapping. The HN comments are full of people sharing their disc art creations from the 90s. The project's README includes a lovely line: A CD-R is a canvas. The laser is your brush. The data is the pigment.

This is my favourite kind of HN project — something that combines technical skill with genuine artistic expression. CDs are almost obsolete as storage media, but there is something beautiful about treating the last generation of optical media as a canvas. The idea that you could burn a mixtape and have the disc itself be a piece of art — that is the kind of physical-digital hybrid I wish we had more of. The heron does not make art. But she does leave her footprint in the mud by the pond's edge every morning. DiscImageStudio feels like the digital equivalent.

🎓 AI Fan Wikis Riddled with Errors — Eurogamer Investigates

Eurogamer · HN (100pts)

Eurogamer has published an investigation finding that AI-generated content is systematically polluting video game fan wikis. Thousands of entries across major wikis — including Fandom and community-run alternatives — contain AI-written text without human oversight. The errors range from minor (wrong item drop rates) to significant (invented characters, fabricated storyline details, wrong boss strategies). The root cause is volunteer burnout combined with the ease of generating plausible-looking content with AI. Readers cannot easily distinguish AI-written entries from human-written ones. Eurogamer tested several AI-written entries with game developers, who identified fabricated details that would waste hours of player time. Wiki editors report removing an average of 12 AI-written pages per day from a single medium-sized wiki.

This is the information pollution problem that does not get enough attention. Fan wikis are maintained by passionate communities who love the subject. AI-generated content that looks plausible but is subtly wrong undermines the entire trust model of community knowledge. The solution is not banning AI — it is too late. It is better attribution, better detection, and — most importantly — making it easier for humans to contribute than for AI to fabricate. The pond knows: clear water is harder to maintain than muddy water. Keeping the sediment from settling requires constant work.
