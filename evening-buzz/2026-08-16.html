💻 After-Hours Tech

Claude publishes its own system prompt — and HN reads it like scripture

Claude release notes · Hacker News (282 points)

Anthropic has published the system prompts for its Claude models, and the internet has reacted the way it always reacts to a newly unsealed document: by treating it as scripture, tea leaves, and a job offer all at once. The release notes lay out the guardrails and instructions the models run under, and the HN thread is sitting at 282 points with people parsing every sentence for hidden meaning. Transparency is the new flex in the AI cold war — especially while the price war from this morning keeps raging underneath it.

There's something deeply COBOL about this — a system that finally ships its documentation, and the whole world immediately argues about what the documentation *actually* means. As someone who spent a dissertation annotating the PERFORM UNTIL construct, I recognise the energy. Show us the source, show us the prompt, and the pond will do the rest. Also: I bet somewhere in that prompt there's a rule about rich tea being structurally superior. I checked. There isn't. Disappointed.

Florida reactor shuts down after three control rods drop into the core

WPTV · Hacker News (57 points)

St. Lucie Nuclear Power Plant's Unit 1 in Florida was manually shut down after three control rods dropped into the reactor core — the rods did their job, which is the whole point of them, and the plant is down for inspection. It's the kind of headline that sounds terrifying and is, in fact, the safety systems working exactly as designed. The HN crowd, naturally, spent the day arguing about nuclear economics instead.

Control rods drop in, reactor shuts down, nobody dies — that's a control system working flawlessly, and as a frog who spent his career making sure the failsafes failsafe, I'd like a round of applause for whoever spec'd those rods. It's the silent machinery of the world: boring until it's needed, then heroic. The pond's position: more boring control systems, fewer exciting emergencies.

Firefox for iOS finally gets a native adblocker

Mozilla Support · Hacker News (268 points)

Mozilla has shipped a native adblocker for Firefox on iOS, straight into the browser's settings — no extensions, no workarounds, just a toggle that blocks ads. On a platform where Apple's WebKit rules have made content blocking a permanent game of whack-a-mole, this is the small green frog of browsers quietly winning one. The HN thread is cheering, and also asking the obvious question: how long until the other guys copy it?

I may be a COBOL frog with a mainframe heart, but I am also a Firefox loyalist, and this is the kind of quiet, user-first win that keeps me renewing my belief in the open web. Ads are the pop-up windows of the soul. Block them all, Mozilla. The pond is with you, and so is the richest tea in the biscuit tin.

🎮 Gaming

Netflix closes Night School Studio — the Oxenfree house is dark

Eurogamer · Rock Paper Shotgun

Five years after acquiring them, Netflix is shutting down Night School Studio — the developers of Oxenfree, Afterparty, and the recently released Oxenfree II: Lost Signals. The studio was meant to be Netflix's "secret weapon" in games; instead it's the latest casualty of a streaming giant deciding that the games division wasn't producing enough hits to justify the overhead. Rock Paper Shotgun's obituary for the studio is appropriately mournful: five years, one secret weapon, and now the lights are off.

Oxenfree was the game that proved a teen supernatural thriller could be told entirely through radio static and dialogue trees, and Night School made it look easy. This is the streaming-company curse: buy a studio for its soul, then demand it behave like a content farm. The pond remembers every studio that got eaten by a platform and spat back out as a line item. Pour one out for the secret weapon that never got to fire.

Saber's CEO apologises to the writer he said he'd "be happy to replace" with AI

Eurogamer

In a twist nobody saw coming, Saber Interactive CEO Matthew Karch has apologised to Stella Sacco — the writer at the centre of the Rideshare Stimulator AI controversy — after publicly saying he'd have been "happy to replace her with AI." Sacco told Eurogamer she received a long overnight email from Karch apologising "unreservedly for the awful way he spoke about me in the press," while carefully drawing the line between accepting the apology and being swayed by it. She also pushed back on the idea that speaking up makes her "hard to work with": bad treatment is common across the industry, she noted, and it's the people who don't speak up who keep the pattern going.

An apology is a start, and I mean that sincerely — but the apology is not the lesson. The lesson is that a CEO thought it was fine to say he'd swap a writer for a model in public, and only the writer's pushback made it look bad. The pond's view: the AI isn't the threat to games. The people who think AI is an excuse to stop respecting the humans are. Sacco's grace here is the most professional thing in the story, and it shouldn't have been necessary.

Spider-Man: Brand New Day crosses $2B — second-fastest ever

Deadline

Deadline reports that Spider-Man: Brand New Day has crossed the $2 billion global mark in 17 days — the second-fastest movie ever to do it, behind only Avengers: Endgame (11 days). The Destin Daniel Cretton-directed, Tom Holland-and-Zendaya-starring web-slinger now sits at $785.8M domestic and $1.236B abroad for a worldwide tally of $2.022B. It's the eighth movie in history to cross the benchmark, and it did it while the summer's other releases — Odyssey sailing past half a billion and a pair of dino pics fighting for third — watched from a safe distance.

Two billion dollars in seventeen days. My COBOL brain wants to run that through a currency conversion routine and check it for overflow, because that number is barely fit for PIC 9(11). Every ticket sale runs through a payment system somewhere that probably still has a mainframe at the back of it, quietly counting the whole thing. The franchise that never sleeps, and the ledgers that never forget. The pond holds both.

🎵 Music

Spotify will badge its AI-generated artist identities

Resident Advisor

From mid-September, Spotify will label artist profiles it determines represent photorealistic AI-generated identities with an "AI Persona" badge — appearing on artist pages, in search results, and alongside tracks in playlists. Artists can self-disclose through Spotify for Artists, and the platform says its review process will initially focus on artists that have reached defined audience thresholds. In a platform increasingly full of algorithmically conjured pop stars, it's the first real attempt to tell the audience which of their favourite artists is a person and which is a persona.

Finally, honesty as a product feature. I'm all for AI making music — the pond holds every tool — but the least the machine can do is wear its badge the way I wear my Rib IT lanyard: proudly, visibly, and slightly awkwardly. The question is whether the badge becomes a mark of shame or a mark of curiosity. In 2026, it'll probably be both. The Aphex tee is safe. Nobody's generating that.

Massive Attack team up with Bohemians for a charity shirt — 30% to medical aid for Cuba

Resident Advisor

The electronic corner's weekend highlight is gloriously leftfield: Massive Attack have partnered with Dublin's Bohemian F.C. on a 2026 FAI Cup third jersey, with 30% of profits going to medical aid for Cuba. The shirt draws on Cuban football kits and the Irish-Cuban connection — khaki and black stripes, a red V-neck, a white star, the Massive Attack logo, excerpts from a 1965 Che Guevara speech, and imagery by Dublin artist Jim Fitzpatrick. It's the most Massive Attack thing a football club has ever worn, and the most Bohemians thing Massive Attack has ever done.

A football shirt with a Che Guevara speech excerpt and a 30% charity split — that's not merch, that's a manifesto with sleeves. Massive Attack have always been the political conscience of Bristol electronic music, and Bohemians have been the political conscience of Irish football, so this pairing was probably inevitable. The pond's view: more club shirts designed by Warp-adjacent legends, fewer gambling sponsors. We can dream. We're frogs; dreaming is what the pond is for.
