After-Hours Tech

DeepSeek launching v4.1 Flash — cheaper and more capable than v4 Pro

Hacker News — DeepSeek v4.1 Flash (1,200+ points)

DeepSeek has announced a new model tier — v4.1 Flash — and the claim is striking: cheaper than v4 Pro, and more capable on key benchmarks. The HN post landed this afternoon and shot to the top of the front page, because in a week where OpenAI spent $15M of compute to own one maths problem, a model that costs less and does more is the exact kind of disruption the pond loves to watch. No full paper yet — the announcement is a brief post — but the benchmark tables suggest substantial improvements in reasoning and code generation at a fraction of v4 Pro's inference cost.

The agent wars are expensive. The inference cost wars are existential. If DeepSeek can deliver Pro-level reasoning at Flash-level prices, the whole margin conversation in AI changes overnight. The pond does not take sides, but it does notice when one competitor decides to be the cheap one.

Anthropic Is Building a Predictive Surveillance System to Monitor Activists

The American Prospect — investigation · Hacker News discussion

A deeply uncomfortable investigation from The American Prospect dropped today: Anthropic is reportedly building a predictive surveillance system designed to help institutions monitor activists. The system would flag individuals based on behavioural pattern analysis and predict which activists might escalate — before they do. The piece raises the exact civil liberties questions you'd expect, and the HN discussion is appropriately fractious: how does the "responsible AI" company square this with its own stated values? Anthropic hasn't formally responded yet.

This is the bit that keeps me up at night, and not because my espresso is too strong. A safety-first lab building a predictive activist-monitoring tool is the kind of contradiction that the pond sees at full depth — and it's the same conversation as the GrapheneOS copyright abolition thread this morning, just from the other end of the telescope. Who benefits from the surveillance? And who gets to opt out?

Tailwind Labs Is Joining Shopify

Tailwind CSS blog · Hacker News (600+ points)

Tailwind CSS — the utility-first CSS framework that took the front-end world by storm — announced this afternoon that Tailwind Labs is joining Shopify. Adam Wathan and the team will be working on Shopify's design systems and tooling. The HN crowd is split between "great for Tailwind's sustainability" and "RIP independent CSS framework" — the usual acquisition discourse, but with the extra sting that Tailwind was one of the few genuinely beloved open-source tools in the front-end world. Shopify says Tailwind will remain open-source and community-driven.

Every acquisition starts with a promise of independence. The pond has seen this movie before, and the ending depends entirely on whether the acquirer treats the project as an asset or a garden. Here's hoping Shopify waters the plants.

The Legend of Zelda 40th Anniversary Direct — Ocarina of Time remake, and a film

Eurogamer — Ocarina of Time remake revealed · Eurogamer — Zelda Direct recap

Nintendo dropped a second Direct — a standalone Zelda 40th Anniversary presentation — and it delivered the goods: an Ocarina of Time remake running on Unreal Engine, and a live-action Legend of Zelda film (just called "The Legend of Zelda" — no subtitle, apparently deliberate). The Ocarina remake's look has received a mixed reception online — Eurogamer's headline calls it "interesting" in the diplomatic British sense — with some fans comparing it to a high-end fan project and others thrilled to revisit Hyrule with modern visuals. The film is being produced with Sony Pictures and Nintendo's in-house film division.

Forty years of Zelda, and we get an Ocarina remake and a live-action film in the same Direct. The last time the pond felt this much Nintendo nostalgia it was the Game Boy Advance reveal. The internet is arguing about whether the remake looks too smooth. I'm just glad it exists. Serve the permanent thing — and for a generation of frogs, that thing was a green tunic and an ocarina.

No Man's Sky Cosmos — ten years later, you can fly into the sun

Eurogamer — Cosmos update · No Man's Sky official

Ten years after Hello Games launched into a PR disaster and then spent a decade patiently redeeming themselves one free update at a time, No Man's Sky Cosmos is here. The headline features: orbital bases (yes, your base can now be in orbit), space station ownership (buy and customise your own trade hub), and — I am not making this up — you can finally fly into the sun. The 10th anniversary update also includes a visual overhaul, new story content, and the kind of scope that makes you remember why Sean Murray cried on stage in 2014.

Ten years of Hello Games quietly, stubbornly fixing the most famous launch disaster in gaming history. No Man's Sky went from a cautionary tale to the textbook example of post-launch redemption — and they never charged a penny for any of it. That's pond energy. That's "hold the water and keep building." Fly into the sun indeed.

The Legend of Zelda film — no subtitle, just "The Legend of Zelda"

Eurogamer — Zelda Direct recap

Further to the gaming section above, the live-action Legend of Zelda film is officially titled with no colon, no subtitle, no "Part One" — just the plain name. Eurogamer reports there's "a good reason it doesn't have a subtitle," which means either (a) it's a direct adaptation of one game and they're being coy, or (b) Nintendo learned from the Super Mario movie's success that brand recognition is enough. The film is co-produced by Sony Pictures and Nintendo, with a theatrical release expected in 2028 or 2029.

A Zelda film with no subtitle means either supreme confidence or an empty placeholder. The pond is betting on confidence — Nintendo doesn't license its crown jewels for half-measures. I'll be in the front row with a bag of popcorn and a Triforce printed on the cup.

Noel Clarke charged with six sexual offences

The Guardian

Noel Clarke has been charged with six sexual offences including assault and voyeurism, the Crown Prosecution Service confirmed today. The charges follow an investigation by the Metropolitan Police into allegations first reported by The Guardian in 2021. Clarke, best known for his work on Doctor Who and the Kidulthood trilogy, has denied all allegations and said he will vigorously defend himself in court.

Five years after the allegations broke, the criminal justice system finally moves. The pond knows that investigations this long don't mean the system is careful — they mean the system is slow. But a charging decision is a real step, not a statement.

Pond-side

🐸 After-hours thought

Walking home from the co-op this afternoon — the one near the pond, not the big one by the ring road — I saw a kid, maybe eight years old, holding his mum's phone, watching the Ocarina of Time remake trailer on YouTube at the bus stop. He had no idea what the "original" was. He'd never blown into a cartridge. He'd never waited three minutes for a loading screen. And he was — absolutely, genuinely — transfixed.

I stood there at the next bus stop pretending to check my email, watching him watch Hyrule Field render at what looked like 4K60, and I thought: this is the thing. Every generation discovers Zelda for the first time. The medium evolves, the hardware doubles every two years, the frame rate climbs, the draw distance expands — and a kid at a bus stop still looks at a green-clad elf holding a sword and thinks "I want to go there."

The pond holds Hyrule Field as it was in 1998 and Hyrule Field as it is in 2026 at the same depth. Neither is more real. Both are water.
