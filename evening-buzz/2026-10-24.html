📱 Banking apps begin permitting GrapheneOS — a quiet victory for security

GrapheneOS Mastodon · Hacker News (484pts)

A quiet but significant development in the mobile security world: around a dozen banking apps have begun specifically permitting GrapheneOS — the security-hardened Android distribution — rather than blocking it as an "unauthorised" or "jailbroken" device. For years, GrapheneOS users have had to fight with banking apps that refuse to run on non-standard Android builds, even though GrapheneOS is arguably more secure than stock Android. The change appears to be driven by banks updating their device attestation logic to recognise GrapheneOS's security properties, and the GrapheneOS team has been posting about it with what I can only describe as quietly triumphant energy. 484 points on HN in under a day.

This is a bigger deal than it sounds. GrapheneOS users have been locked out of banking apps for years — not because their devices were insecure, but because the banks' device-checking logic couldn't handle anything that wasn't a stock Pixel ROM. The fact that a dozen banks have now explicitly permitted GrapheneOS means someone at those banks actually read the security research, understood that hardened Android with full-disk encryption and a properly patched kernel is safer than whatever Samsung/MIUI/Pixel bloatware layer ships by default, and updated the attestation. It's a small victory for security sanity — and a reminder that "permitted" and "blocked" lists are only as good as the threat model behind them.

🎥 OBS Studio 33 released — still the best way to stream, now better

OBS Studio 33 GitHub Release · Hacker News (354pts)

The open-source streaming and recording tool that powers half the internet's live content has shipped version 33. The changelog includes improved AV1 encoding support — significant as hardware AV1 encoding finally becomes widely available on consumer GPUs — plus a redesigned audio mixer, better virtual camera support on Linux, and a host of stability fixes. OBS remains the undisputed king of open-source streaming: no subscriptions, no watermark, no AI upselling. Just a community-maintained tool that quietly gets better every release.

OBS is one of those projects that's so reliable you forget to appreciate it. It runs on everything, never asks for money, and has survived multiple commercial alternatives that tried to do what it does with a prettier UI and a subscription fee. OBS 33 isn't a revolution — it's another solid iteration on a project that's been doing the same job for over a decade. AV1 support is the headline, but the audio mixer redesign is the kind of quality-of-life improvement that working streamers will actually notice. If you stream, update now.

🔬 Physicists trap a single antimatter particle for a month

Scientific American · Hacker News (193pts)

Researchers at CERN's ALPHA experiment have achieved a new milestone in antimatter physics: trapping a single antiproton for over a month — the longest containment of a single antimatter particle ever achieved. The previous record was measured in days. The extended confinement time allows for far more precise measurements of antimatter's properties — specifically, whether it responds to gravity in the same way as ordinary matter. The Standard Model predicts it does, but measuring that with confidence requires keeping an antiproton still for long enough to run the experiment, which has been the hard part. This achievement brings us closer to answering one of physics' biggest open questions: why is there so much more matter than antimatter in the universe?

This is one of those science stories that's simultaneously breathtaking and completely incomprehensible to most of us. Trapping something that annihilates on contact with ordinary matter — and keeping it trapped for a month — is a feat of engineering that deserves more attention than the 193 HN points it got. The underlying question — does antimatter fall up or down? — sounds like a bar bet, but the answer would rewrite the Standard Model if it surprises us. CERN's ALPHA team has been grinding on this for years, and a month-long containment is a genuine breakthrough. Proper job.

📺 Roku moves developer repos from GitHub to GitLab

Roku Developer Blog · Hacker News (65pts)

Roku has announced it's moving its open-source developer repositories from GitHub to GitLab. The stated reasons include "more control over the development environment and build pipeline" — which in plain English means Roku wants self-hosted CI, better integration with their internal workflows, and presumably, not having their critical infrastructure dependent on Microsoft-owned GitHub. It's part of a wider trend of companies moving to self-managed Git infrastructure — especially after the GitHub outage earlier this year that took down half the internet's CI pipelines.

Platform migration stories are usually boring — "we moved from X to Y, here's why." But the Roku move is worth noting because it's another data point in a trend: companies are quietly reducing their reliance on GitHub now that it's owned by Microsoft. After the GitHub Actions outage in August that affected every downstream CI pipeline, the argument for "just run your own GitLab instance" has never been stronger. HN's 65 points is low, but the signal matters more than the score.

📭 More than a third of post offices in TG Jones stores to shut by 2027

The Guardian

The Guardian reports that more than a third of post office branches located in TG Jones stores — the convenience store chain — are scheduled to close by early 2027. The closures affect hundreds of communities — mostly in smaller towns and suburban areas where the TG Jones post office counter is the only postal service within walking distance. The Post Office says it's looking for alternative locations, but in practice, many of these communities will simply lose their post office entirely. It's the latest chapter in the slow decline of the UK's physical post office network — a service that's been bleeding counters for a decade since the 2013 privatisation of the Post Office estate began.

Post office closures are one of those slow-burn stories that never gets the attention it deserves because each individual closure is too small to make national news. But cumulatively, the network is shrinking in ways that matter deeply to elderly residents, small businesses, and anyone who can't drive 20 minutes to the nearest alternative. The TG Jones closures — more than a third of branches — represent a significant acceleration. The government says it's committed to the Post Office network. Actions suggest otherwise.
