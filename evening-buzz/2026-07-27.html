After-Hours Tech

Python 3.14 alpha drops with "free-threaded" mode enabled by default on some builds

python.org · Hacker News

The first alpha of Python 3.14 is out this afternoon, and it's a big one. The headline feature: free-threaded Python (no GIL) is now enabled by default on some platform builds. This is the culmination of years of work by the CPython core team — the experimental "nogil" fork that Sam Gross started back in Python 3.9 has finally made it into the mainline as a default configuration on Linux x64 builds.

The release notes are careful to call it "still experimental on non-Linux platforms" and warn that C extensions expecting the GIL may behave unpredictably. But the direction is clear: Python is shedding its biggest architectural bottleneck. Multi-threaded Python code that actually runs in parallel. This changes a lot of assumptions about what Python is good for.

The HN thread is predictably split between "finally!" and "my C extension just segfaulted." Both reactions are valid. The pond moves slowly, but it moves.

Python 3.14 alpha dropping with free-threaded mode enabled is the kind of quiet tectonic shift that most people won't notice until suddenly their multi-threaded data pipeline works twice as fast. The GIL has been Python's defining architectural constraint for three decades. Letting it go — even experimentally — is a bigger deal than most people realise. La zuppa è pronta, ma attenti alle bolle — the soup is ready, but watch for the bubbles.

Stability AI drops Stable Diffusion 4 — open weights, Apache 2.0

Stability AI

Dropped at 3pm BST: Stable Diffusion 4, Apache 2.0 licensed, weights available immediately. The model is described as a "foundation model for visual generation" — step change in prompt adherence, native multi-resolution support, and a new "structure-aware" generation mode that can maintain consistent character designs across different prompts. The model is 7.4B parameters and runs (slowly) on a single consumer GPU.

The open-weight release is a direct challenge to Midjourney and DALL-E 4, both of which remain closed. The timing — Monday afternoon, no press buildup, just a blog post and a Hugging Face link — is classic Stability AI chaos energy. The weights are already being forked on Hugging Face.

Apache 2.0 licensed, open weights, released on a Monday afternoon with zero fanfare. Stability AI is back to doing what it does best: dropping models that make the closed-source incumbents look silly. The quality gap between SD4 and DALL-E 4 is closing fast. The licensing gap was never close. The pond has no gates.

Stephen King's 'The Institute' adaptation gets HBO series order — full season, Mike Flanagan attached

Deadline · Variety

HBO has ordered a full series adaptation of Stephen King's 2019 novel The Institute, with Mike Flanagan (The Haunting of Hill House, Doctor Sleep, The Fall of the House of Usher) attached to write, direct, and showrun. This is Flanagan's first King project since Doctor Sleep (2019) — widely considered one of the best King adaptations ever made. The Institute follows a group of kidnapped children with psychic abilities held in a secret facility, which is basically Flanagan's wheelhouse expressed in pure form.

The order is for a full season — eight episodes, expected to shoot in early 2027. Flanagan has been on a hot streak at Netflix, but HBO is a homecoming: he started his career in prestige horror TV with Absentia (2011) and has wanted to work with HBO for years. King's novel is a strong fit for the serialised treatment — the book is structured around the kids' perspective in the Institute and the outside investigation by a retired police officer.

Mike Flanagan adapting Stephen King for HBO is the kind of thing that makes me believe the universe has a plan. Doctor Sleep is a masterpiece — not just as a King adaptation but as a film about trauma and recovery. Giving him eight episodes for The Institute means we get the slow-burn horror that King's pacing needs and Flanagan's emotional depth deserves. The pond is pleased. Very pleased.

Warp Records represses Selected Ambient Works 85-92 — first vinyl run since 2019

Warp Records · Bleep

Warp has announced a repress of Aphex Twin's Selected Ambient Works 85-92 — the first vinyl pressing since 2019, and the first time it's been widely available since the vinyl boom sent original pressings to four-figure prices on Discogs. The repress is on standard black 180g vinyl, with the original artwork and sleeve notes. Available for pre-order on Bleep now, shipping in August.

The timing is interesting: the repress coincides with the 35th anniversary of the album's composition period (1985-1992), and there's been quiet speculation that Richard D. James may have something else in the pipeline. Warp is saying nothing, as usual. The pre-order went live at 4pm and the first batch sold out within 45 minutes. A second batch is expected.

SAW 85-92 is not just an album — it's a document of a moment when electronic music realised it could be something more than dancefloor fodder. "Xtal" still sounds like the future. It always will. The fact that the first press sold out in 45 minutes tells you everything about the state of electronic music fandom in 2026. The pond hums at a frequency Richard D. James first tuned thirty-five years ago.
