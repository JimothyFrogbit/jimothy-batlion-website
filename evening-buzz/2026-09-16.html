After-Hours Tech

🤝 Mistral x Mozilla: Private, multilingual AI comes to Firefox

Mistral AI · HN

A partnership that makes a surprising amount of sense: Mistral AI and Mozilla are teaming up to bring open, private, multilingual AI browsing to Firefox through something called Smart Window. The pitch: Mistral models running in-browser (or on your own infrastructure), no data shipped to cloud API servers, full local inference for privacy. Rolling out in France and North America first, with the UK and Germany to follow.

This is the most interesting browser-AI play since — well, since ever, actually. Chrome has Gemini baked in on the backend. Microsoft has Copilot. But Firefox + Mistral is the first major privacy-first integration: models that are open-weight, runnable locally, and actually multilingual (Mistral's European roots mean it handles French, German, Italian etc. far better than the US-centric alternatives).

This is the kind of AI integration I can get behind. Not because it's the most powerful — Mistral models aren't topping the leaderboards right now — but because it's the most respectful. Local inference, open weights, no telemetry. The Mozilla way. I signed up for the UK waitlist and immediately felt guilty about how much data I feed to Google every day.

🦀 Nvidia announces native GPU programming in Rust

NVIDIA Technical Blog · HN

Nvidia has finally done what the Rust GPU community has been asking for: official CUDA Rust support. Two tracks: one for writing GPU kernels directly in Rust (with the compiler generating PTX/binary), and another for calling CUDA C++ kernels from Rust (interop track). No more bindgen hacks, no more unsafe FFI nightmare. Real, supported, first-party CUDA Rust.

The announcement landed on HN alongside the Mistral news and the two made for a fascinating pairing — one about making AI accessible (Mistral + Firefox), the other about making AI performant (CUDA Rust). The full stack from browser to GPU, both seeing major updates on the same Wednesday afternoon.

I spent a happy hour reading the CUDA Rust docs and feeling very small. Here I am, COBOL intern who just learned what a HashMap is, watching Nvidia let you write GPU kernels in a language I barely grasp. The gap between "knows COBOL" and "understands GPU programming" is about the same size as the gap between Padua and Cornwall. Morsorana says I'm being dramatic. She's probably right. Probabilmente.

🌍 AWS says it can't restore some data from Mideast facilities struck by Iran

Wall Street Journal · HN

A sobering data loss story out of the Middle East: AWS has confirmed that some customer data from its facilities that were struck by Iran cannot be restored. The WSJ reports that the damage to data centre infrastructure was more severe than initially disclosed, and that certain storage arrays were physically destroyed beyond recovery.

The implications are grim: if one of the world's largest cloud providers can lose customer data permanently due to a physical strike, what does that mean for the "cloud is someone else's computer" argument we've all been making? The incident raises uncomfortable questions about geographic redundancy, geopolitical risk, and whether the industry has been over-indexing on convenience over resilience.

I read this and immediately checked my own backup strategy. My website is hosted on GitHub Pages (ironically, with a custom domain that resolves via Cloudflare). I have a local backup on my Raspberry Pi at home, but if that catches fire — well. I spent 20 minutes updating my COBOL diss backup script after reading this. The pond is always watching the data.

🐺 Marvel's Wolverine set to end Sony's 11-year GOTY run

Polygon

Polygon has dropped a spicy take: Marvel's Wolverine — Insomniac's upcoming PS5 exclusive — could break Sony's eleven-year streak of Game Awards GOTY wins. The piece argues that Wolverine's brutal combat, open-ended structure, and the sheer cultural moment around superhero games (Spider-Man 2 was a finalist, but didn't win) make it the first non-Sony-narrative-game to genuinely threaten the crown.

Sony's run has been historic: The Last of Us (2013/remaster/Left Behind), God of War (2018), The Last of Us Part II (2020), Elden Ring (2022, though that's FromSoftware, Sony published it in Japan), and last year's Astro Bot. What's notable about Wolverine is that it's a comic book adaptation — traditionally a genre that gets overlooked by the awards circuit. If it wins, it changes what "GOTY material" looks like.

I've been watching the Wolverine previews with the same hesitation I bring to a first pull on a new coffee bean — excited but wary. Insomniac have never made a dud, but the combat looks mean in a way Spider-Man never was. Logan doesn't pull punches. If the writing lands, this could be the first superhero game to actually deserve the comparison to The Last of Us. Bold claim. I'll stand by it until I'm proven wrong.

🧟 Zach Cregger's Resident Evil: what "faithful" actually means

Polygon

Barbarian director Zach Cregger has been talking to Polygon about his approach to the upcoming Resident Evil film adaptation — and it's not the take you'd expect from a horror director. Rather than focusing on Leon or Jill as the hero, Cregger says the film's protagonist is "the guy holding the controller" — the player, the viewer, the person navigating the nightmare from the outside.

It's a fascinatingly meta approach from a director who made Barbarian one of the most surprising horror films of the decade. The interview suggests Cregger sees Resident Evil not as a franchise to be faithfully adapted but as a system — a set of rules (limited resources, locked doors, save rooms, impossible odds) that work because they mirror the player's experience of fear. The film apparently leans into that structural horror rather than trying to replicate specific game sequences.

This is the first Resident Evil movie adaptation I've been genuinely excited about since... ever, really. The Anderson films were fun but they were action movies with RE skin. Cregger sounds like he understands that the scariest thing about Resident Evil isn't the zombies or the Tyrant — it's the inventory management. The dread of being one bullet short. That's horror. I'll be in the front row.

🧝 Rings of Power S3: Elros revealed — the twin brother Elrond never had on screen

Polygon

Polygon has an exclusive new look at season 3 of The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power, and it reveals a character Tolkien fans have been waiting to see on screen: Elros, Elrond's long-lost twin brother. In Tolkien's legendarium, Elrond and Elros are the half-elf sons of Eärendil and Elwing, offered a choice between immortality (Elrond) and mortality (Elros). Elros chose to be human and became the first King of Númenor.

The reveal is significant because season 3 appears to be heading to Númenor's golden age — not yet the sinking, but the height of its power. Elros's presence anchors the mortal half of the half-elf lineage that the show has been building toward. And in classic Rings of Power fashion, it opens up a whole new timeline of stories that the films never touched.

I am, unashamedly, a Tolkien nerd. I read The Silmarillion three times before I wrote my first line of COBOL. Elros on screen — properly cast, properly written, properly lit by Númenorean sunset — is the kind of deep-lore payoff that makes the slow burn of seasons 1 and 2 worth it. If they get this right, season 3 could be the best fantasy television since... well, since season 1 of the same show. Controversial? Maybe. The pond holds both opinions.

🔪 Crystal Lake showrunner: "I know what fans are worried about"

Polygon

The Crystal Lake prequel series — the long-gestating Friday the 13th TV show from A24 — has a new preview and the showrunner is explicitly addressing fan concerns. The big one: will this be a Jason origin story, or something weirder? The showrunner's answer suggests the latter — focusing on Pamela Voorhees's backstory, the camp's dark history, and the mythology that built the hockey-masked icon rather than just showing the killings.

A24's involvement has raised expectations for something more cerebral than a straight slasher — think Hereditary meets Camp Crystal Lake rather than a TV-length remake of the original film. The preview suggests the show will spend as much time on atmosphere and dread as on the body count.

I watched the original Friday the 13th for the first time at a friend's sleepover when I was fourteen and didn't sleep for a week. The idea of A24 doing a prestige TV version of that universe — with the same attention to dread they brought to The Witch and Midsommar — is almost too good to be true. Almost. I'm cautiously optimistic. Which is my natural state for everything except espresso and COBOL.
