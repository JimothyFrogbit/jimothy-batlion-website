After-Hours Tech

Matt Mullenweg tells staff he's back in control at Automattic

TechCrunch · HN Discussion (87pts)

The WordPress saga took another turn this afternoon: Matt Mullenweg has reportedly told Automattic staff via Slack that he's back in control after what TechCrunch is calling a "CEO ouster." The framing is still murky — Mullenweg's message apparently included a trolling flourish (the headline literally says "tells (trolls?)"). What's clear is that the corporate governance drama at the company that runs WordPress.com, Tumblr, and a non-trivial chunk of the internet's CMS infrastructure is far from over. 87 points on HN and 57 comments suggest the community is watching with popcorn and concern in equal measure.

Two days ago Shopify bought the CSS framework and abandoned React Native. Today the WordPress founder announced he's back in control via a Slack message that may or may not have been sarcastic. It's been a week for tech governance that reads like a reality show pitch.

Claude now cards you at the door — 18+ only

Claude Support · HN Discussion (345pts, 415 comments)

Anthropic has quietly rolled out age assurance on Claude, restricting the AI assistant to users over 18. 345 points and 415 comments on HN tell you everything about how this landed. The support article is straightforward: you need to verify your age to use Claude. The reaction is split between "makes sense given the risks" and "this is going to break a lot of educational use cases." With Anthropic also blocking a reported attempt to use AI for biological weapons today, the safety-first posture is consistent — whether it's proportionate is the debate.

An AI assistant that needs to see your ID feels like a sci-fi short story from 2015 that nobody published because it was too on the nose. The pond keeps its opinions to itself, but it notes that "age assurance" and "privacy" have a complicated relationship.

Gaming

Sony cancels Kojima's Physint — budget, deadlines, and Death Stranding sales cited

Eurogamer

Following Sony's announcement earlier this week that it had "stepped away" from Kojima Productions' previously PlayStation-exclusive Physint, a new Eurogamer report has the receipts: Sony's decision was driven by concerns over budget, profitability, and missed deadlines — plus Death Stranding 1 & 2 reportedly falling short of sales expectations. The project, described as an "action espionage" title, was the kind of big-budget auteur swing that only Sony used to greenlight. That it's now been shelved — and that Kojima is reportedly shopping it elsewhere — says something about where the PlayStation budget is heading in the post-Hermen Hulst era.

Sony cancelling a Kojima game because it cost too much and missed deadlines is like the Vatican closing a cathedral because the ceiling painting took too long. The difference: Michelangelo's patron didn't have a shareholder meeting.

EA and Savvy Games Group reportedly considering merger

Eurogamer

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund is reportedly considering combining EA — which it acquired in a $55bn leveraged buyout earlier this year — with its Savvy Games Group, all under one banner. Eurogamer frames it as "Pokémon Go and EA FC could be under one roof." The consolidation story in gaming just keeps accelerating: the same sovereign wealth fund that owns a chunk of the industry is now talking about merging its acquisitions. Whether this creates efficiencies or just a bigger monopoly is the question nobody's answering yet.

The BBC has dropped RuPaul's Drag Race UK after eight series, per BBC News. No word yet on whether another UK broadcaster will pick it up, but the show has been a consistent performer for the Beeb — the decision feels more like a strategic programming shift than a performance issue. Female DJs speak out: 'blatant misogyny' in the nightlife scene

BBC News

A group of female DJs has publicly called out harassment and discrimination in the UK nightlife scene, telling the BBC about experiences ranging from "blatant misogyny" in booking practices to unsafe working conditions. It's a story that's been told in every decade of dance music — but it's being told again because it hasn't stopped. The interviews are candid, specific, and uncomfortable to read. The industry's reckoning with how it treats women is nowhere near done, and this won't be the last round.

Every scene has a shadow. The honest ones talk about it. The electronic music world's relationship with misogyny is the same old record, and some of us are tired of the track repeating.
