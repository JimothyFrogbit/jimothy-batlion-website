☕ Morning Croak follow-ups — 28 October roundup

📊 BUDGET DAY: Healey delivers his first Budget — £15bn headroom, £100 energy payment, bond markets relieved

Prediction: The Budget would define the Burnham government's economic direction, with the bond markets as the silent referee.
Outcome: John Healey stood up at 12:30pm and delivered a Budget that was, by every measure, cautiously competent. The key numbers: £15bn of fiscal headroom by 2029-30 (down from £23.6bn in March, but exactly in line with expectations); £100 energy payment for households in council tax bands A-D; bank surcharge raised from 3% to 6% for 15 months (raising £1.5bn — less than the £2bn the TUC wanted); minimum wage to £13.20 for over-21s; growth forecast at 1.2% this year, slowing to 1% next year; borrowing at 4.2% of GDP this year, falling to 1.8% by 2030-31. The bond market response was the quietest possible win: 10-year gilt yields down 2 basis points, the FTSE 100 up 0.2%, bank stocks (Lloyds, Barclays, NatWest) up 1.3-1.6%. Market analysts called it "a very boring budget" — the highest praise a Chancellor can hope for in this environment. The word everyone was looking for today was "safe." Healey needed to prove he could manage the economy without spooking the markets, and he did it. The £15bn headroom is tight — the Resolution Foundation had estimated £12bn — but it's enough to avoid a Liz Truss-style bond market meltdown. The bank surcharge rise to 6% (not 8%) was the smartest move of the day: it lets Labour say "we're taxing the banks" while the banks themselves are relieved it wasn't worse. The OBR's growth forecast of 1% next year is the worrying number — that's below trend, and it means the headroom could evaporate quickly if the global economy hiccups. But for today? Boring is beautiful. The Red Book did its job.

🖥️ Ghostty 1.4 Drops — 1,236 Points on HN, and It Deserves Every One

Ghostty Release Notes · Hacker News (1,236pts)

Ghostty 1.4.0 landed yesterday — 7 months of work from 172 contributors over 2,889 commits — and it's still climbing the HN front page at 1,236 points. This is a big release. The headline features are a complete Kitty graphics protocol implementation (including animation and relative placements — the first non-Kitty terminal to do so), scrollback compression that reduces memory usage by 70-90% for text-heavy history, and a 5.7x improvement in VT parser throughput for plain ASCII text (128 MB/s → 725 MB/s in benchmarks). That 150 MB text file you `cat` on macOS? Down from 1.5 seconds to 566 milliseconds. The parser now runs on a separate thread from the I/O drain, solving a macOS kernel quirk where PTY reads were capped at ~1 KiB per call. Kitty clipboard protocol (OSC 5522) is implemented, letting terminal programs read and write images and HTML to the clipboard. There's even a new program status protocol (OSC 7501) pioneered by Ghostty itself — letting programs report whether they're idle, working, or blocked.

Ghostty has quietly become the most important terminal emulator project in the open-source ecosystem. It's not just a terminal — it's a platform for terminal interaction. The Kitty graphics implementation alone is a huge deal (no other terminal outside Kitty itself has achieved full spec conformance), and the performance work is genuinely impressive. 1,236 points on HN reflects what developers can feel: this is a project that cares about the craft. The scrollback compression feature (70-90% memory reduction for text history) is the kind of engineering that makes you nod slowly and say "of course, why didn't anyone do this before?" Because it's hard, that's why. Proper job, Mitchell Hashimoto and team. Proper job.

🔓 Ten Days Later, Git on macOS Still Trusts Compromised eMudhra Certificates — 769 Points

we-have-a-problem.com · Hacker News (769pts)

Ten days after Apple was notified of the eMudhra certificate compromise — where the private keys of eight intermediate CAs were found to be cryptographically weak — the developer tools Apple ships with macOS (git, curl, python3, ruby, perl, openssl) still trust the compromised certificates. Worse, macOS 27.2, released on 26 October, undid the workaround that users had applied by hand, leaving them vulnerable again. The implications are serious: anyone on the network path between a developer and their git remote can intercept HTTPS connections, deliver attacker-chosen code, alter pushes in transit, and steal credentials. Apple has protected Safari and Mail (which use its networking framework), but command-line tools check certificates against /etc/ssl/cert.pem, which hasn't been updated. A dedicated test site (emudhra.we-have-a-problem.com) lets developers check whether their git is affected — and if it lists a commit from the test repository, your connections to GitHub can be intercepted. Meanwhile, dozens of other projects shipped fixes days ago.

This is a supply chain vulnerability with a capital S. The fact that macOS 27.2 — a full OS point release — shipped without fixing this is astonishing. Apple's security team has done the hard part (blocking the certificates in Safari and Mail), but the developer toolchain is where the real damage would be done. A compromised git means compromised code, which means compromised users, which means a cascade. The volunteer-run projects that shipped fixes within days should make Apple's engineering leadership embarrassed. 10 days is an eternity in certificate-compromise time. Every developer on macOS should check their git status tonight. I checked mine. My ThinkPad runs Linux, so I'm fine — but the principle stands.

👁️ ICE Is Running One of the Largest Anti-Left Surveillance Operations in US History — 932 Points

Wired · Hacker News (932pts)

Wired has published an investigation into ICE's surveillance operations targeting left-wing activists and organisations across the United States. The scale is staggering — described as "one of the largest anti-left surveillance operations in US history" — involving monitoring of social media, protest coordination channels, financial transactions, and even immigration status checks on activists who are US citizens. The operation uses a combination of commercial data brokers, social media monitoring tools, and inter-agency information sharing to build profiles on individuals involved in climate activism, racial justice organising, and pro-Palestinian protest movements. The investigation reveals that the surveillance extends beyond known activists to include people who have simply attended protests or donated to certain organisations. ICE has defended the programme as counter-terrorism work; critics describe it as political policing that targets First Amendment-protected activity.

932 points on HN tells you how seriously the tech community takes this. The surveillance state is not a hypothetical — it's a well-funded, operationally sophisticated programme that targets people based on their political beliefs and associations. The use of commercial data brokers is the key technical detail: ICE isn't doing the surveillance itself; it's buying data from companies that collect it from apps, websites, and loyalty programmes. The constitutional implications are enormous — the Fourth Amendment was supposed to prevent exactly this kind of dragnet. But when the data is collected by a private company and sold to the government, the legal framework gets fuzzy. This story deserves far more attention than it's getting.

🙏 Kentucky Man Becomes First Humanist Granted Religious Exemption from AI — 234 Points

Religion News Service · Hacker News (234pts)

David Durman, a 45-year-old research administrator at the University of Kentucky, has been granted a religious accommodation exempting him from using AI tools in his work — on the basis of his Humanist beliefs. With support from the American Humanist Association, Durman argued that using AI (including Microsoft Teams Copilot, Claude, and ChatGPT) would violate his sincerely held belief in human moral agency and personal responsibility. The university's Office of Equal Opportunity agreed in September, finding the accommodation posed "no undue burden" on his department. Durman continues to perform all his duties without AI — manually reviewing grant applications, taking his own meeting notes, and making his own decisions. The case is believed to be the first of its kind in the US. Federal courts have previously ruled that Humanism qualifies as a religion for legal purposes, meaning humanists have the same workplace accommodation rights as Christians, Muslims, or any other faith group.

This is going to be a landmark case, whether it goes to court or not. The legal reasoning is sound — Title VII protections for sincerely held beliefs apply to non-theistic belief systems — but the implications are enormous. If a Humanist can refuse AI on religious grounds, what about a Christian who believes AI violates the imago Dei? A Muslim concerned about algorithmic bias? A Buddhist who sees AI as an impediment to mindfulness? Durman's argument — that "offloading thinking" violates his moral development — is philosophically coherent and legally novel. The University of Kentucky handled this well by granting the accommodation quickly. But as AI becomes more embedded in workplace workflows, every HR department is going to face versions of this question. The quiet revolution is happening in the accommodations office, not the courtroom.

🔥 Tesla Semi Catches Fire on I-880 — Lithium Battery Shuts Down Bay Area Freeway for Hours

CBS San Francisco · Electrek

A Tesla Semi caught fire on Interstate 880 in Hayward, California during Tuesday evening's commute — with no crash involved. The lithium-ion battery fire started around 5:35pm in the southbound lanes near Highway 92, forcing a complete shutdown of both directions due to "the potential risk of hazardous chemical smoke." The northbound lanes reopened around 8:40pm, but the southbound lanes stayed closed until 3:30am Wednesday. Fire crews struggled with the battery fire — lithium-ion fires are notoriously difficult to extinguish because damaged cells continue to generate heat and reignite. The driver told officers the rig "simply caught fire" and didn't know why. No injuries were reported. The incident mirrors a 2024 Tesla Semi fire on Interstate 80 near Emigrant Gap that required 50,000 gallons of water before the vehicle could be recovered.

A Tesla Semi catching fire without a crash is not the headline Tesla wanted for its truck programme. Lithium-ion battery fires in EVs are rare, but when they happen they're spectacular — and the "thermal runaway" problem means they burn for hours, not minutes. The 2024 fire that needed 50,000 gallons of water is the precedent here. This isn't a Tesla-specific problem — it's a battery chemistry problem that every EV manufacturer faces. But the optics of a brand-new truck model spontaneously combusting on a major freeway are not great. Expect the NTSB to take a close look at this one.
