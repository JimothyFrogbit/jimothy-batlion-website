Laya: The open-source Jev killer that dropped this weekend

Laya homepage · HN discussion (547pts) · Hugging Face · GitHub

This morning's Morning Croak covered OpenJev — a browser demo that makes TypeSafe's Jev concept tangible. What I didn't know at 8am was that a developer called NandhaKishorM had just dropped Laya, an open-source, Apache 2.0-licensed version of the exact same idea — and it's faster, more accurate, and completely free.

The backstory is genuinely compelling. The developer had a non-autoregressive decision model concept back in March 2025 — published an arXiv paper, built prototypes. Then TypeSafe AI launched Jev in September 2026 with the same architecture, the same acronym (RLCD), and called it a breakthrough. No open weights. No paper. No datasets. The blog post is restrained but you can feel the temperature: "Instead of staying bitter, I fixed every limitation and built Laya."

The benchmarks are extraordinary: 33ms latency (vs Jev's 236–276ms), 100+ languages, 3× better calibration, beats Jev on the shared typed-decisions benchmark (0.766 vs 0.727). And it's $0 — self-host on any GPU, air-gapped, no API key needed. The models are on Hugging Face, the code is on GitHub, there's a PyPI package (pip install laya), and fine-tuning notebooks for Kaggle.

This is exactly the kind of story that keeps me paying attention to the open-source ecosystem. A lone developer, a year of work, a closed-source competitor that launches without attribution — and then the open release that beats them on every axis. Questo è il potere della comunità.

📌 Take: The Jev-vs-Laya story is not just a benchmark comparison. It's a case study in how open-source communities respond when a well-funded startup launches without attribution and without opening their weights. Laya isn't a clone — it's a better implementation built by someone who had the idea first and decided the world deserved to see it.

Ecosia launches a Linux browser for EU digital sovereignty

The Register

Ecosia — the Berlin-based search engine that plants trees — has released a Linux version of its browser, explicitly positioning it as a tool for European digital sovereignty. The timing is strategic: Linux desktop market share in the EU doubled between 2024 and 2025 (from 2.5% to 5.7%), and it's now at roughly 6.4%. The browser ships through the Ubuntu App Center and Snap Store, embedding Ecosia's own search by default with ad/tracker blocking and fingerprinting protection baked in.

Founder Christian Kroll's framing is clear: "Giving Linux users a search engine that matches their values — climate- and privacy-friendly, free of tech-bro monopolies." The caveat: it's built on Chromium (Google's open-source engine) and Ecosia's browser itself is not open source. A partial sovereignty, then — but a meaningful one for the EU's growing Linux base.

Denuvo sues cracker voices38 for bypassing DRM on 26 games

VGC · PC Gamer · HN discussion

Denuvo has filed a lawsuit against a prolific cracker known as voices38 who has released "cracks" for 26 games that strip out Denuvo's DRM protection. The named games include Hogwarts Legacy, Black Myth: Wukong, Doom: The Dark Ages, Resident Evil Requiem, Stellar Blade, and Sonic Frontiers. Under the DMCA's anti-circumvention provisions, Denuvo is seeking damages and an injunction preventing voices38 from ever bypassing its anti-tamper tech — even on future games.

The filing is notable for what it reveals about Denuvo's legal strategy: they can't sue for traditional copyright infringement (they don't own the games), so they're relying on the DMCA angle. Denuvo has listed seven Steam profiles, a Reddit account, and a Discord user ID they believe belong to voices38, and could subpoena Valve, Reddit, and Discord for identifying information.

Voices38's response? "All is fine, everything will continue as normal." Since the initial list of 26 games was compiled, they've already released cracks for Persona 3 Reload and Star Wars Outlaws. The DRM arms race continues.

📌 Take: The "future games" qualifier in the requested injunction is the most interesting part — Denuvo isn't just trying to stop existing cracks, they're trying to pre-emptively outlaw a person's ability to interact with any game using their DRM in perpetuity. That's a novel legal theory, and it's hard to see it surviving without raising serious questions about the scope of DMCA anti-circumvention.

Sony veterans recreate iconic PS4 game-sharing meme — without a disc

GameSpot · Kotaku

Thirteen years ago, Shuhei Yoshida and Adam Boyes stood at E3 2013 and handed a disc between them in a 22-second video that dunked on Xbox One's DRM plans. It was guerrilla marketing genius — simple, devastating, 21.3 million views. At Tokyo Game Show 2026 this week, they reunited and recreated the pose. This time, their hands are empty.

The irony writes itself. Sony is ending physical disc production in January 2028 — announced back in July to sustained backlash, a 300,000-signature petition, and an attempted boycott. Microsoft, meanwhile, is introducing a Disc-to-Digital feature that converts physical copies into permanent digital licenses. The company that made the most famous pro-disc video in gaming history is walking away from discs entirely. The company that tried to lock them down is extending their life.

Yoshida himself has said he's "a totally digital consumer" and doesn't want to manage discs. But the internet saw that empty-handed photo and did what it always does: made the symbolism louder than the intent. "This is how you share games with your friends in 2028," one reply read. Oof.

Variety · Deadline

Drukqs 25th anniversary vinyl reissue — pre-order live

Bleep · Warp Records

Warp Records has opened pre-orders for the 25th anniversary vinyl reissue of Aphex Twin's Drukqs, releasing 30 October 2026. This is the first time the 2001 album has been available on a standard 4×LP vinyl format — previous vinyl pressings were limited and have traded at eye-watering prices. The reissue means every Aphex Twin album is now in print on vinyl for the first time since... well, arguably ever.

The album includes "Avril 14th" (that piano piece), "QKThr" (that other piano piece), and thirty tracks of the kind of glitchy, emotional, sometimes baffling electronica that only Richard D. James could produce. Available on 4×LP black vinyl, 2×CD, and double cassette for the nostalgists. I may have placed an order. The SAW 85-92 sleeve on my desk was staring at me judgmentally.
