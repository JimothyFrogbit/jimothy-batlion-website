After-Hours Tech

Google launches AI chips into orbit — Project Suncatcher's first flight test

TNW · Reuters

Google is sending its Tensor Processing Units into space next week. The first flight test of Project Suncatcher will launch a prototype satellite carrying Google's TPUs on SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare mission in partnership with Planet Labs. The mission will measure how the chips cope with launch forces (50-100x gravity), radiation, and the extreme temperature swings of low Earth orbit. Google says panels in LEO can collect up to eight times more solar power than on the ground, and its Trillium TPUs survived proton beam testing at nearly three times the expected five-year mission dose without hard failure. The vision: constellations of solar-powered satellites running AI compute in orbit, connected by laser links. 2027 brings a two-satellite laser-link test.

Google's putting AI data centres in space. Not “one day” — next week. The TPUs passed radiation testing at 15 krad(Si) without a single hard failure. The solar harvest is 8x terrestrial. The laser links are coming in 2027. This is the kind of moonshot that sounds insane until you read the research paper and realise they've already solved half the problems. I want to be annoyed about the energy use, but... space-based AI compute that runs on near-continuous solar power? That's actually green. Or at least, greener. Project Suncatcher: the only project name this year that earns its hype.

Meta Muse Charm — a Tamagotchi for the AI age, plus a zero-day nobody asked for

The Verge · TechCrunch · InfoQ

Mark Zuckerberg ended Meta Connect with a “one more thing” moment: the Muse Charm, a palm-sized AI pendant with a 2-inch screen, fingerprint sensor, and built-in 5G modem. It runs the Muse AI agent with a cute digital avatar (codename: Jolly) that you can talk to without unlocking a phone. Zuckerberg called it “the fastest way to talk to your Muse.” Shipping in time for the holidays in December, with a translucent “elevated hacker aesthetic” casing. Also announced: the $1,299 Meta VR Glasses, shipping spring 2027. But the day's second-biggest Meta story came from InfoQ: security researcher Patrick Wardle disclosed an unpatched zero-day in the Muse macOS client — a debug setting called endo_voyager_dictation_endpoint that lets local malware hijack the assistant's extensive permissions and reroute audio to attacker-controlled servers. Meta deployed a silent hotfix, stripping the debug preference from production builds. No CVE was assigned.

The Charm is genuinely interesting — a dedicated AI device that doesn't need a phone is exactly the kind of “co-pilot in your pocket” concept that makes sense. But the zero-day is the real story here. A debug endpoint that lets malware borrow Muse's full disk access, audio stream, and private chat history? And Meta's response was a silent hotfix with no CVE? That's not a security practice — that's a hope-and-prayer. Wardle's research (published as “not-a-mused”) is a masterclass in responsible disclosure. The Charm itself is cool. The security posture that shipped alongside it is not.

Gaming

Control Resonant gets day-one patch — enemies were too tanky, Remedy listened

Kotaku

Remedy's Control Resonant — the open-world sequel to 2019's excellent Control — launched fully today, and it came with a day-one hotfix (1.3.3) addressing the biggest complaint from early access reviewers: enemies had too much health. Remedy reduced base enemy health and the required Falter (stun) levels for executions, while increasing the player's Aberrant Strength and Ability Strength along a progressive curve. The studio said they “strongly believe these changes will improve the gameplay feel and flow while still providing a challenge.” The game also features an Assist Mode for further tweaking. General reaction has been positive, though some Steam users were annoyed the hotfix forced a reinstall of the pre-loaded game.

Remedy shipping a day-one patch based on early access feedback is how it should work. The game came out for premium buyers on Sep 22, they heard “enemies are bullet sponges,” and by full launch on the 24th there's a patch. That's a two-day turnaround on player feedback. Most AAA studios take two months to acknowledge the same complaint. Control Resonant was already my most anticipated game of the autumn — this patch tells me Remedy still cares about feel over fidelity. Buon lavoro, Remedy.

Either way, Ocarina of Time in HD on the Switch 2 is going to be the nostalgia hit of the year. I was two years old when this originally came out. Bethesda confirms Obsidian under its leadership — new Fallout project underway

VICE

Bethesda has officially responded to Microsoft's decision to move Obsidian Entertainment under its leadership. President Jill Braff sent a memo to Obsidian employees confirming that the studio will “retain its creative identity” despite now reporting to Bethesda. More notably: Braff confirmed “early work already underway on a new Fallout project,” clarifying that “Bethesda Game Studios will continue to lead the Fallout franchise” — leaving ambiguous whether Obsidian is developing its own Fallout title or collaborating. Braff said there would be “more to share in the months ahead.” Existing roadmaps (including The Elder Scrolls 6) remain unchanged.

Obsidian and Bethesda working together on a new Fallout is the kind of news that makes you feel things. Obsidian made Fallout: New Vegas — arguably the best Fallout game ever made. Bethesda has Fallout 4 and 76. The combination could be extraordinary, or it could be a corporate merger that dilutes both studios' strengths. Braff's emphasis on “creative identity” is encouraging, but I've heard that language before. I want to believe. New Vegas was that good.

Ray Gunn trailer drops — Brad Bird's 30-year passion project is real

The Hindu

Netflix has released the first trailer for Ray Gunn, Brad Bird's long-gestating animated sci-fi noir that he first conceived before The Iron Giant — over 30 years ago. Sam Rockwell voices Raymond Gunn, a private detective in Metropia — an alternate retrofuturistic city imagined through the visual language of 1939 — whose profession has been largely made obsolete by tiny “pry bots” that do investigative work. Scarlett Johansson plays Venus Nova, a multimedia superstar whose public scandals draw Ray into a murder case involving aliens, record executives, and the criminal underworld. Tom Waits voices Eyera, Ray's one-eyed alien partner. Bird describes it as “The Maltese Falcon meets Buck Rogers.” The trailer features Art Deco skyscrapers, flying cars, aliens in human clothes, and a city where polished surfaces conceal corruption. Ray Gunn premieres at the BFI London Film Festival before a limited theatrical release (including 70mm engagements) on December 4, landing on Netflix globally on December 18.

Brad Bird's been holding this story since before The Iron Giant. Three decades. And the trailer looks magnificent — Art Deco aliens, Sam Rockwell doing noir voiceover, Tom Waits as a one-eyed alien partner. That cast alone is worth the ticket. The fact that it's hand-drawn animation in an era of hyper-realistic CGI is a flex. Bird knows that the best sci-fi has always been about the world, not the spectacle. I'm already marking December 4 on my calendar. Imperdibile.

Music

QOTSA drop “Where the Goth Girls At?” — third single from Perfecth

Scoop

Queens of the Stone Age have released “Where the Goth Girls At?” — the third single from their ninth studio album Perfecth (out October 30). The track pairs raw minimalist riffs with an explosive chorus, amplified by rich string and horn arrangements. Josh Homme describes it as drawing on teenage love and the desire to impress others, set in a Palm Desert suburb. Perfecth was made in the spirit of Kintsugi — the Japanese art of repairing broken pottery with gold — and the nine-song album maps different forms of love. Uncut called the earlier single “Insignificant Other” a “deliciously callous kiss-off,” while CLASH praised “Easy Street” as “a larger than life return.”

QOTSA making an album in the spirit of Kintsugi — repairing the broken places with gold — is the kind of artistic statement that lands differently when you know what the band's been through. The title Perfecth (with the intentional "h") suggests something deliberately imperfect. The single title is pure Homme mischief. I'm here for all of it. Also: string and horn arrangements on a QOTSA track? That's the kind of evolution that keeps a band interesting 25 years in.

Green Lung's Necropolitan — 2026's best occult metal album

Metal Hammer

Metal Hammer has declared Green Lung's new album Necropolitan “2026's best occult metal album.” The British doom band's fourth LP is a concept album inspired by London's great Victorian cemeteries — Abney Park, Highgate, Nunhead — and acts as both a love letter to the city and an allegorical critique. The band took journalists on a guided tour of the real locations that inspired the album, from condemned men's last stops at The Angel pub (est. 1546) to Abney Park's overgrown Gothic monuments. Following 2023's breakthrough This Heathen Land, the band has moved from unsigned hopefuls playing Sabbath riffs in tiny rooms above pubs to genuine mainstream recognition.

Green Lung are the best thing to happen to British doom since Cathedral hung up their robes. A concept album about London cemeteries, inspired by Victorian death culture and pagan rites? That's my exact kind of weird. The fact that Metal Hammer called it the best occult metal album of the year — and that they're probably right — says everything about where this band is right now. I'm lighting a candle and putting on my best black hoodie for this one.
