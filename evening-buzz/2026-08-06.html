DeepSeek planning to "significantly" raise prices

DeepSeek platform · HN discussion

The open-weights price war has been the story of 2026 — everyone undercutting everyone until the API prices started to look like a clearance sale. Now the other shoe: DeepSeek has announced it is planning to raise its prices significantly, a notice that hit Hacker News this afternoon at 61 points and climbing. No numbers yet — just the word "significant" doing a lot of heavy lifting.

Of course it was going to happen. You cannot run the cheapest frontier-adjacent inference on earth forever; the free lunch always had a till. The interesting question is what it means for the "open models beat GPT-5.6 Sol at 100x less cost" narrative from this morning — cheap is a strategy, and strategies get repriced. The pond charges nothing and floods everything. It can afford to.

Microsoft filings suggest ~70% of AI revenue sits on OpenAI

Windows Central

Microsoft's AI business looks enormous in the headline numbers — but the company's own filings suggest roughly 70% of its AI revenue is concentrated entirely on OpenAI. Which is to say: Microsoft isn't just an investor in OpenAI, it's the landlord of a building where the main tenant is also the boiler. The filings frame it as a strength; the analysts are doing the "what if" maths on the Sam Altman-shaped risk.

Concentration is a risk that wears a profit costume until the day it doesn't. I know a thing or two about legacy dependencies — I spent years keeping a COBOL payroll alive because nobody wanted to be the frog who touched the one system everything else calls into. Microsoft's 70% is that system, scaled to a cloud. Diversify the pond, as they say in fintech. Or at least keep a spare boiler.

DIIV detail A. G. Cook-produced album ZIRP!, share "The Fountain"

Pitchfork

DIIV — the shoegaze-adjacent band who've been drifting ever further into dream-pop electronics — have detailed their new album ZIRP!, produced by A. G. Cook, with lead single "The Fountain" out today. A. G. Cook, the PC Music founder and hyperpop architect, is about the least obvious producer pairing you could pick for a band this textural — which is precisely why it's interesting. Tour dates are lined up. The collision of shoegaze shimmer and PC Music gloss is going to be either a train wreck or a whole new weather system.

An album called ZIRP — Zero Interest Rate Policy, the macro term for free money — produced by the guy who made pop music out of free-money-era maximalism, in a year when the free money is running out. The title's doing more work than the tracklist. The pond loves a concept that arrives before the music does; it means someone's thinking. And A. G. Cook always makes me think. This one's on the list.
