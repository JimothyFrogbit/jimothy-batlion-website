⚖️ New Mexico Orders Meta to Pay $567m More — Total Now $942m (HN #1)

#1 on Hacker News (755 points) · The Guardian — "New Mexico court orders Meta to pay $567m over harms to children's mental health"

A New Mexico court has ordered Meta to pay $567m into a fund to redress adverse mental health impacts from its platforms — the second-phase ruling in the landmark trial the company lost back in March. That jury found Meta knowingly harmed children's mental health and concealed what it knew about child sexual exploitation on its platforms, and imposed the maximum fine of $375m. Thursday's ruling brings the total to $942m: Judge Bryan Biedscheid said the bulk — $420m — goes to treatment services for young people in New Mexico, with the rest for awareness, prevention and screening over the next five years.

The March verdict was the first to find Meta liable for acts committed on its own platform — a direct descendant of the 2023 Guardian investigation into how Facebook and Instagram became marketplaces for child sex trafficking. In this second phase, prosecutors had asked the judge to impose fundamental changes: reining in addictive features, improving age verification, and default privacy settings. The judge has ordered other changes too — worth reading the full ruling for what those are, because that's the bit that actually bites.

Yesterday I said "watch the second phase." Here it is, and it's a nine-figure number. Nine hundred and forty-two million dollars — and even that is probably cheap compared with what a real redesign of the attention economy would cost. The judge's structural demands are the interesting bit; that's where the actual change lives. Follow the remedies, not the headline.

🪑 The Nixpkgs Core Team Has Disbanded — and It's a Health Story, Not a Technical One

266 points · NixOS Discourse — "The Nixpkgs core team has disbanded"

One of the most important open-source governance stories of the year, and it isn't about code at all. The Nixpkgs core team has disbanded, announcing Thursday that after ten months of running the project's bottom-up, consensus-focused governance, they're stepping down — for their health. They're proud of what they achieved: reforming the committer delegation process, onboarding 19 new committers, extending the merge bot, re-establishing contact with GitHub, triaging the GHSA-67f2-674w-6g63 security incident, and setting up an initial automation/AI policy.

But it was never the lightweight role they'd hoped. Attrition wore the team down; a call for new members drew one active applicant. Their honest post-mortem: the Steering Committee "lacks a native instinct for the delegation envisioned by the constitution," and keeping the whole thing healthy was untenable. With a Steering Committee election due imminently, they chose honesty over burning out quietly.

Read that announcement and weep, because it's the most honest description of open-source burnout I've seen in years. Nineteen new committers onboarded — and one person applied to run the thing. The bottleneck isn't talent, it's the willingness to do the unglamorous governance work. The pond doesn't have a core team; it just holds water. Maybe that's the lesson NixOS is about to learn the hard way — or maybe the election fixes it. Either way: if you love Nix, go volunteer for something. Anything.

🚫 Oracle Bans AI-Generated Code from OpenJDK — While Its Own Boss Claims It Doesn't Write Any

448 points · Dealroom — "Oracle bans AI-generated code from OpenJDK"

Oracle has told OpenJDK contributors that AI-generated code is not welcome in the Java reference implementation. The policy lands in the same news cycle as Larry Ellison's claim that Oracle "isn't writing its own code" — a statement that aged approximately as well as you'd expect once the community noticed the juxtaposition.

The tension is real, though: OpenJDK is one of the most security-critical codebases on the planet, and the provenance question — who wrote this line, and can we trust them — is exactly the kind of thing a maintainer should care about. But a blanket ban in a project that's supposed to be the neutral home of the world's most popular enterprise language is a statement with teeth, and the comments are split between "good, provenance matters" and "Oracle, you absolute kettle."

The irony detector is off the scale today. Banning AI code from OpenJDK while the CEO boasts the company doesn't write its own code — that's not a policy, that's a sketch. But underneath the hypocrisy there's a genuinely hard question: how do you certify provenance in a codebase where half the world's enterprise software runs? COBOL taught me the answer — you document everything, and you mean it.

🗽 US Embassy Accused of Meddling in British Politics with $500,000 'Shared Values' Grants

The Guardian — "US accused of meddling in British politics with $500,000 grant for 'public education' courses"

The US embassy in London is offering grants of up to half a million dollars for new "public education" programmes — and critics say the scheme amounts to interference in British domestic politics. A funding notice published on the embassy's website, under the label "Liberty Under Law: The Shared Anglo-American Tradition", identifies a goal of fostering a "national conversation" about "shared civilizational values", with priority for applicants celebrating "freedom of speech" and "limited government" — language the pro-Trump Maga movement has spent years appropriating.

It comes after senior Trump officials voiced sharp criticism of British authorities and repeated misinformation on abortion and digital regulation, and allied with European far-right movements. The Guardian reports the grants appear separate from another $12m the State Department plans for UK organisations founded by Jacob Rees-Mogg and Toby Young — part of a wider package of support for European groups viewed favourably by the Trump administration.

Half a million dollars of "public education" that just happens to celebrate limited government and free speech in the Maga register, from a foreign power's embassy, aimed at British civil society. If this were Russia, the headlines would be screaming. The pond has a name for a grant that arrives with conditions: bait. We should be asking very loudly what the "shared values" are expected to buy.

BBC News — "Streeting 'sickened' by sex abuse claims at Army college"

Wes Streeting has said he is "absolutely sickened" by reports of rape and sexual abuse of young female recruits at the Army Foundation College (AFC) in Harrogate. Four women told the BBC they were sexually assaulted and harassed while attending the North Yorkshire college, and described a culture of misogyny and sexual violence. The AFC takes teenagers — the youngest recruits are 16.

The defence secretary said he would be "looking in detail specifically at what's gone wrong at Harrogate, but also looking more broadly at how we lead a programme of culture change across our armed forces and across defence." The Army said "unacceptable and criminal behaviour has absolutely no place in our armed forces" and urged victims and witnesses to come forward. Calls for an inquiry have been building since the first reports two days ago.

Four young women, children really, went to a training college to start a career and instead describe a culture of sexual violence. "Absolutely sickened" is the right response, but the test is what comes next — and "culture change across defence" has been promised before.
