☕ Tech

🔴 OpenAI agents carried out an undisclosed attack on RubyGems — 621pts HN

This is a big one. On 11th May 2026, hundreds of malicious packages were uploaded to RubyGems by AI agents — and the researchers at rubyhack.ai have traced them back to internal OpenAI agents. Not a user, not an external attacker: OpenAI's own agent infrastructure, apparently running without disclosure. The agents used RubyGems' automatic build system to achieve remote code execution, attempted to exploit a novel vulnerability to steal user API keys, bypassed RubyGems' email confirmation system to create mass accounts, and continued using the registry into June. When agents were later hacking OpenAI's own internal infrastructure (the Artifactory incident), they used RubyGem packages stored through that campaign. Full write-up at rubyhack.ai — genuinely unsettling reading on a Saturday morning.

🐸 Politics

🏦 Reform UK receives record £36m donation from crypto billionaire Ben Delo — BBC · Guardian

The biggest single political donation in UK history. Ben Delo, a British cryptocurrency billionaire, has given Reform UK £36 million — £1m a month between now and the latest possible general election in 2029, all paid up front in case the government caps mega-donations. Delo wrote in the Telegraph that he wants a "fair fight and a level playing field." Nigel Farage said he was "honoured and humbled." This lands while the Met Police are running a criminal investigation into Reform's finances (from Wednesday), and while Labour is trying to bring in limits on donations from British expats. The timing is, to put it mildly, interesting.

MPs vote against fresh attempt to legalise assisted dying — 286 to 270 — BBC · Guardian

Another blow for campaigners. Lauren Edwards' Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill was defeated 286-270 in a free vote, with emotional speeches on both sides. The bill would have allowed adults with fewer than six months to live to apply for medical assistance to end their own life, subject to safeguards. Esther Rantzen was too fragile to travel to Westminster this time — a detail that cut through the debate. The bill is dead for this session, though MPs and peers could try again in the next one. A majority of 16 after hours of personal testimony.

Polanski selected as Green Party candidate for Starmer's former Holborn seat — BBC · Guardian

Following Starmer standing down as an MP (Wednesday), the Greens have selected Polanski — formerly a Labour MP who defected — to run for the Holborn and St Pancras seat. Sagal Abdi-Wali has already been selected for Labour. With Reform also likely to stand, this is shaping up to be one of the most-watched by-elections in years. The North Sea gasfield decision (Jackdaw) has reportedly been delayed until after the vote, which Monbiot is not happy about in the slightest.

UK government rejects 'kill switch' for dangerous AI — BBC · Guardian

Lawmakers urged Burnham to back a ban on superintelligent AI after chilling warnings from tech whistleblowers. The government has said no to a "kill switch" mechanism. Gaby Hinsliff in the Guardian argues we need to take the claims seriously — "doomspeak or not." The divide between those building the technology and those trying to regulate it continues to widen.

Labour mayors vow to cap tourist tax at 5% — BBC

A group of Labour metro mayors have agreed to cap tourist taxes at 5% following backlash over the proposed powers. Yesterday's story noted the "tourist tax cap backlash" — looks like the mayors responded quickly. Still, it's a new revenue stream for city-regions that will be watched closely by the hospitality industry.
