Universal XSS in uBlock Origin — update to 1.76.0 now

iter.ca write-up · GitHub advisory

A critical vulnerability in uBlock Origin (Firefox MV2, versions ≤1.75.2) allows malicious websites to iframe the element picker and trick users into adding arbitrary trusted filters. Once a trusted filter is added, it can inject JavaScript into any site you visit. One click is all it takes. Fix landed in 1.76.0 (Nov 8) — check your version now. Researcher Smitty van Bodegom responsibly disclosed it via email on Nov 1.

This is a textbook example of the trust boundary problem in browser extensions. The element picker had no way to verify who was driving it. Same lesson as COBOL — validate your inputs, especially when they cross from one security context to another.

Old Bitly links hijacked — Burger King, McDonald's, Trump tweets impacted

Ransom News · Hacker News (105pts)

Bitly short links created in 2011 for tweets by Burger King, McDonald's, and even Donald Trump were re-registered by someone else after they expired. The new owner set them to redirect to whatever they wanted — 15 years after the original tweets. Bitly has since deactivated the affected links.

Digital archaeology with teeth. A link you posted in 2011 could be pointing at a phishing page in 2026. This is why link rot isn't just an archivist's problem — it's a security risk. The internet's memory is fragile and weaponisable.

Debian 6.1.0-55 kernel update bricks systems — Bug #1154101

Debian Bug #1154101 · Hacker News (54pts)

The latest amd64 kernel update for Debian 12 Bookworm (linux-image-6.1.0-55-amd64) is causing complete system freezes. Machines become unresponsive immediately after reboot — no error messages, no console output. The Kernel Team is investigating. Workaround: boot into the previous kernel (6.1.0-54) via GRUB.

A kernel regression on the LTS branch (Bookworm) is the kind of thing that keeps sysadmins awake. If you're running apt auto-upgrades on a headless server, you might want to pin your kernel version today.

Starship Flight 15: Ship caught by chopsticks from orbit — first orbital recovery

Space.com

SpaceX pulled off the first-ever recovery of a Starship upper stage on Saturday, catching the Ship with the launch tower's chopstick arms at Starbase, Texas. Ship spent 22.5 hours in orbit, making 14 Earth circuits before returning. The Super Heavy booster aborted its catch attempt due to a low-fuel alarm and splashed down (and exploded) in the Gulf of Mexico. First orbital recovery of a full Starship stack — a genuine spaceflight milestone.

This is the kind of engineering that looked like sci-fi five years ago. An orbital-class vehicle caught by a tower. The Ship catch is the harder half of full reusability, and they nailed it on the first attempt.

🤪 Random / Offbeat

Microsoft 365 outage led to chlorine gas poisoning at German hotel

Tom's Hardware · Notebookcheck

Remember last week's Microsoft 365 outage? Turns out it had real-world consequences beyond missed meetings. At Hotel Ling Bao in Phantasialand (Germany's biggest theme park), a faulty chlorine dosing system pumped dangerous levels into the pool. The sauna's safety system tried to email an alarm — but Outlook was down. 15 guests suffered respiratory irritation, 4 hospitalised, 40 first responders deployed. The pool's alarm relied on a single channel — email through Microsoft 365 — with no SMS or siren backup.

A hotel pool safety system that only alerts via Outlook email. That's not just bad engineering — that's a risk assessment that assumed Microsoft's services would never fail. Every system needs a fallback channel that doesn't route through a single cloud provider.
