☕ Tech

Jensen Huang resigns from Nvidia after 33 years — board approached Arm CEO Haas as successor

AP News · Hacker News (3165pts)

Jensen Huang resigned as CEO, president, and board member of Nvidia, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress steps in as interim CEO. The Nvidia board has reportedly approached Arm CEO Rene Haas as a potential successor. This comes after weeks of turmoil: the WSJ reported the board was investigating Huang over an affair with a Chinese spy, Chinese AI models were found carrying Blackwell chip fingerprints, and Commerce Secretary Lutnick has agreed to testify on Nvidia's China chip sales. An employee suggestion-box post calling for Huang's return has drawn over 1,600 comments. Separately, SemiAnalysis claims the mass smuggling of up to 1.5 million Nvidia GPUs could be behind Huang's departure.

This is a genuine seismic event for the entire AI industry. Jensen Huang has been Nvidia's public face for 33 years — he built the company that accidentally became the most important hardware supplier in the history of computing. The fact that the board has approached Arm's CEO (a former Nvidia vet) suggests they want continuity, but the Blackwell chip-forensics angle makes this feel like a national security story wearing a corporate governance hat. The employee petition tells you everything about how cult-like Nvidia's internal culture was around him. First Nvidia employees petition to keep him, then he resigns anyway — something broke that we're not seeing the full picture of.

Microsoft 365 outage resolved after 18 hours — network capacity upgrade caused cascading failure

Ars Technica · TechCrunch

Microsoft says all services are fully restored after the day-long global outage that stretched from the Americas to Europe. The cause: a network capacity upgrade that introduced an issue leaving servers unexpectedly unavailable, which then overloaded the rest of the infrastructure. The outage affected Outlook, Teams, Copilot, Defender screening, Intune admin dashboards — and cascaded into Claude's Microsoft 365 connector failing. Kentik's Doug Madory clocked it at about 18 hours. Microsoft says there's no evidence of malicious activity and a full post-incident report will follow.

Eighteen hours. For a core piece of global infrastructure. A "network capacity upgrade." This is the third major Microsoft outage in as many months. We've built the modern world's communication backbone on a single vendor's platform, and when it goes down, governments, hospitals, courts, and schools all stop. The fact that it also took down Claude's connector is a lovely reminder of our new dependency stack — AI tools depend on cloud tools depend on network infrastructure. One bad config and the whole tower wobbles.

FAA nationwide ground stop — all US departing flights grounded after ATC communications failure

Ars Technica · The Verge

A rare nationwide ground stop on all departing US flights Friday lasted several hours. The FAA says an update to the private communications network connecting the air traffic control system caused the system to reboot and crash. The ground stop has been lifted, and flights are moving again. The outage affected airports across the country during a busy travel period.

The same day Microsoft has its 18-hour outage, the FAA has its own. Friday the 13th energy was real yesterday. We are absolutely flying too close to the sun with the complexity of our interconnected systems — a network update crashes air traffic control and a cloud provider simultaneously. There is no backup plan for "the network reboots wrong."

OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet — dots agents can now pay for purchases

TechCrunch · OpenAI Blog

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday: a stored payment method that lets its "dots" AI agents (in ChatGPT Work, Codex, and dots) complete purchases on behalf of users. You add a US-issued Visa, Mastercard or AmEx card once, ask your dot to buy something, then review items, total, and card before approving with Face ID or fingerprint. The merchant never sees your card number — network payment tokens handle it. Available to US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18+. Spending limits and pre-approval by category may come later. Notably, Amazon blocks outside agents and United Airlines banned unauthorised AI booking just this week.

This is the moment agent commerce becomes real. A payment rail for AI agents is the missing infrastructure piece — without it, agents can research and recommend but never complete a transaction. The security model (user approves each purchase, tokenised payments, chargeback rights survive) is sensible. But the fact that Amazon and United are already blocking agents tells you where this is heading: a cold war between AI agents and the platforms they want to interact with. Sierra's Poppy 1.0 protocol (launched this week with Shopify default-on) is the alternative approach — standardised agent-to-business handshakes. We're watching two competing visions for how agents interact with the world emerge in real time.

🐸 Politics

Burnham's make-or-break EU summit week — "long nights ahead" as 95% deal nears

The i Paper

Andy Burnham faces a make-or-break week culminating in a Brussels summit on Friday where a historic new UK-EU deal is expected to be signed. After flying to COP31 in Antalya, Turkey for a brief appearance on Thursday, he went directly to the European Political Community summit in Dublin for last-minute talks. The biggest sticking points: EU demand for the UK to reduce tuition fees for European students, and UK demand to be part of the EU's Made in Europe scheme to protect manufacturers from Chinese imports. Burnham has signalled he will give ground on both — calling it in the UK's "interest" to have more European students, and acknowledging "Europe's issue is Britain's issue" on Chinese imports. But there's been a notable backtrack: Burnham has dampened down talk of rejoining the EU, with No 10 refusing to commit to outlining "the options" before year's end. Cameron has warned against reopening the "long and painful" Brexit debate. Labour MPs in Red Wall seats are already complaining.

The classic Burnham manoeuvre — signal ambition, draw the fire, then retreat to the centre. He promised to set out "the options" for the UK-EU relationship at conference, and now the summit "needs to go well" before any such discussion. A "95% there" deal that includes concessions on tuition fees and Made in Europe is genuinely significant — closer than any PM has got since 2020 — but the rejoining rhetoric is getting walked back faster than an espresso shot pulling into an undialled grind. Green leader Polanski has already called Burnham out on the pattern: "Signal one thing, take the praise, then do nothing or the opposite." Hard to argue.

Burnham refuses to commit to Milburn's full welfare reforms — backbench revolt brewing

The Telegraph

Asked whether the Government would implement Alan Milburn's welfare reforms in their entirety when they are published next week, Burnham refused to commit. The report is expected to recommend that under-25s should no longer be able to claim PIP or the health element of Universal Credit, moving instead to a "youth allowance" at standard UC rates. Labour backbenchers on the Left have threatened revolt over the plans. Burnham — who opposed Keir Starmer's £5bn welfare cuts last year — said: "There's lots of detailed recommendations, so I can't give you blanket assurance here tonight." The Telegraph editorial page frames it as Burnham "confronting Britain's addiction to handouts."

This is the issue that broke Starmer, and now it's Burnham's turn. The welfare bill is genuinely out of control — one in five working-age people on a health-related benefit, a 72% increase since 2019 — but "under-25s lose PIP" is a brutal headline, especially for a Labour PM who campaigned on compassion. The CSJ report found a single inactive person on Universal Credit + PIP + housing support can receive £27,400 per year tax-free. That's not sustainable. But nor is making disabled young people the scapegoat for a system the government designed. Expect a rebellion next week.
