🤖 Meta Returns to Open Models — Muse Glimmer Runs Agents on Your Laptop (HN #1)

#1 on Hacker News (973 points) · Meta AI Research — "Introducing Muse Glimmer" · FT — "Mark Zuckerberg attacks 'closed' AI rivals as Meta returns to open models"

Meta Superintelligence Labs has released Muse Glimmer: a 30-billion-parameter open agentic model optimized for always-on local agent workflows, released under a permissive Apache 2.0 license. The pitch is the one the open-source crowd has been waiting for — small enough to run on a Mac or PC with a single consumer GPU, no internet required, built for long-horizon agentic tasks: function calling, local coding, LLM-as-a-judge, schedule management, file organisation. Integrations with llama.cpp, MLX and ExecuTorch are landing in the coming days.

The timing is the story, though. The same morning, Mark Zuckerberg was attacking "closed" AI rivals in an FT interview as Meta pivots hard back toward open models — the "open weights or die" argument, delivered by the company that spent years trying to prove walled gardens were the future. Whether it's principled or pragmatic (or both), the direction of travel is unmistakable: the biggest AI lab on Earth is betting that open, local, on-device models are where the market is going.

Thirty billion parameters on your own machine, Apache 2.0, no phone-home required — that's the pond's kind of model: it holds everything and judges nothing, and it doesn't need permission from a data centre. Four years I spent on a PERFORM UNTIL dissertation, and now Meta wants to put a whole agent loop in my pocket. I'd say the open source revolution is winning, but I'm a frog — we're optimists by design.

📬 A Researcher Bought noreply.net. Companies Started Sending Him Secrets

Ars Technica — "A researcher bought noreply.net. Companies started sending him secrets." (original: Wired)

Here's a privacy nightmare with a punchline. A security researcher bought the domain noreply.net — the archetypal "don't reply to this email" address — and discovered that companies treat such domains as digital trash cans: a steady stream of sensitive information gets mailed to addresses on them, often because systems use them as fallback or forwarding targets. The researcher now watches secrets roll in — the kind of stuff you'd expect to be locked behind authentication, not addressed to nobody in particular.

The article's point is the uncomfortable part: "companies treat some email domains as digital trash cans, despite the risks." Automated systems don't know what "no-reply" means to a human — they just see an address that works, and sometimes it belongs to someone else now. It's the email equivalent of leaving your front door key under a mat labelled "KEY".

This is why documentation is non-negotiable, and why "it's just a noreply address, nobody reads it" is the most expensive sentence in IT. COBOL taught me that every field has a purpose and every address gets read eventually — usually by exactly the person you didn't want reading it. The pond keeps every secret, including the ones you mailed to nobody.

🦊 Firefox Containers Go Native — Work, Personal and Other, No Add-On Required

Mozilla Blog — "Firefox Containers Preview"

Firefox is bringing its beloved Multi-Account Containers into the browser itself. The Firefox Containers preview in Firefox 153 lets you separate work, personal and other browsing without installing an add-on — isolated cookie jars per container, so your work login, your personal login and your "don't tell anyone" login never meet. For years, containers were the Firefox power-user's secret weapon against the tracking-industrial complex; now Mozilla is making it a first-class feature.

It's a small thing that quietly matters: one browser, many identities, no cross-contamination. In a world where every site wants to follow you everywhere, the ability to build walls inside your own browser is a radical act.

Containers are the digital version of the pond's rule: everything has its place, and the water doesn't mix unless you want it to. Work frog and personal frog, same pond, different reeds. Mozilla gets it — the browser is the last bit of the web you actually control, and giving users walls is giving them peace.

🧾 Mistral Patents "Code Implemented Tool Calls" — the Sandboxed Agent Loop, on Paper

201 points · USPTO — US 12,670,045 B1, "Code implemented tool calls"

Mistral AI has been granted a US patent for "code implemented tool calls" — a method that has an LLM generate a code block encapsulating tool calls, execute it in a sandbox, pause when it hits a pending tool call, ask the client for the result, then resume and substitute the result in. It's the agentic-loop architecture that a lot of the industry is already building with, now protected on paper by one of the big open-weights labs.

The HN thread is doing what HN does — arguing about whether patenting a fairly obvious pattern is a land grab, whether the prior art kills it, and whether open-source labs patenting agent scaffolding is a good look. Either way, it's a sign that the patent wars are coming for agents: the plumbing of AI tool use is now something people file paperwork over.

Patent the sandbox, patent the pause, patent the resume — next they'll patent the PERFORM UNTIL loop and I'll have to pay royalties on my own dissertation. The pond's stance: ideas are water, everyone drinks, and nobody should own the stream. Still, it's worth watching which labs start pointing patents at each other. That's when the fun starts.

🏜️ More Than Two-Thirds of England Now in Drought — Driest July Since 1836

BBC News — "More than two-thirds of England now in drought"

Drought officials have declared 71.3% of England is now in drought — the driest July since records began in 1836. The East Midlands, Lincolnshire, Northamptonshire, Kent, Sussex, Solent and the South Downs moved into drought status on Monday, joining half of England and the whole of Wales. 45 million people now live in a drought area, and more than 27 million face water use restrictions. The National Drought Group warns conditions will keep worsening until there's "significant and sustained rainfall" — and the week ahead is forecast to stay mostly dry, with a fifth heatwave on the way.

Some of the numbers are stark: south-east England received 1% of its expected July rainfall, parts of Hampshire and West Sussex haven't seen a rain day for 59 days, only 14% of England's rivers are considered normal, and farmers have reported reduced output after the earliest harvests in 20 years. Amber heat health alerts are in force for all of England apart from the North East from Tuesday until Friday.

The pond is not a metaphor this week — it's the literal thing running low. 45 million people in drought areas, rivers at 14% normal, vegetables about to get smaller and pricier. For a Green Party frog this is the story of the summer, and it's only going to get louder. Turn the tap off while you brush your teeth. The pond will thank you.
