☕ Tech

Residential proxies detected on White House network — 236 exits in 30 days

932 points — Layer3 Intel dropped a bombshell yesterday: their Residential Proxy Observatory detected 236 proxy exits from AS2650 — the Executive Office of the President's network — over 30 days. Twenty-eight different residential proxy services were able to route traffic through six addresses on the White House network between 5 October and 3 November. Krebs confirmed the story — one of the services identified is Infatica, a Russian proxy company whose founder's previous web hosting business was ground zero for the 2016 election system hacks in Arizona and Illinois. Spur and Synthient independently verified the addresses as residential proxy exits. The big question: was this malware, a shady SDK bundled into a staffer's app, or someone knowingly installing proxy software on a White House device? The White House has not commented.

OpenAI Images 2.6 — 50% faster, GIFs, reference images

241 points — OpenAI released Images 2.6, their latest image gen model, now available in ChatGPT, Codex, and the API. Key upgrade: 50% faster generation, GIF creation (ask for a looping animation or upload a sketch and ask ChatGPT to animate it), and saved reference images that persist across chats. Two API tiers: GPT-Image-2.6 Sunburst (high quality) and Flare (as fast as 6 seconds per image at low quality). People are generating 4 billion images per week across OpenAI's platforms — half a billion a day. The release notes also mention they've cancelled the Pro 200 reduction announced in September — users keep their higher allowances.

AI is forcing us to pay off 40 years of technical debt — 1158 points

#1 on HN — IEEE Spectrum published a piece tying the PHP2Shell vulnerability to a broader argument: AI code generators are forcing organisations to confront decades of accumulated technical debt, because AI can exploit it faster than humans ever could. The PHP2Shell vulnerability — an RCE in PHP's HTTP handler — is a wakeup call that the software we built in the 2000s is not ready for AI-powered security testing. Grim but essential reading.

This connects to yesterday's story about CVE-2026-98432 still being unpatched on Ubuntu after 19 days. The pattern is clear: AI finds the cracks faster than we can fill them.

Revolut cuts AI coding costs 90% with self-hosted Chinese GLM model

The Register reports that British fintech Revolut switched its 2,000 engineers from Claude Code and Codex to self-hosted GLM models from China's Z.ai. The cost dropped from $3/million tokens to $0.30 — a 90% saving. They run GLM-5.3 on 56 Nvidia B200 GPUs. Reported improvements: coding time +60%, lead time +70%. Abitbol (Revolut's AI infra head) says the biggest gains weren't in writing code but in the other parts of the development workflow — debugging, refactoring, code review. Claude Code and Codex are still available as exceptions for complex tasks. A British fintech publicly choosing Chinese models over US commercial APIs is going to raise eyebrows in Whitehall.

🐸 Politics

🇺🇸 US MIDTERM EARTHQUAKE: Democrats sweep Congress

This is the story. In what the AP calls a "decisive rebuke of Republican governance," Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate. They'll have at least 54 Senate seats and at least 222 House seats. The scale is extraordinary:

  • Texas: James Talarico defeated Ken Paxton for Senate — first Democrat elected statewide in Texas in 30+ years. And in a stunner, Gina Hinojosa defeated Greg Abbott for governor, becoming Texas's first Latina governor and first Democrat in the office since Ann Richards in 1990.
  • Kansas: Adam Hamilton defeated Roger Marshall — Kansas hasn't elected a Democratic senator since 1932. Nineteen thirty-two.
  • South Carolina: Annie Andrews upset Darline Graham in a deep-red state Trump won by double digits.
  • Ohio: Sherrod Brown beat Jon Husted in a state that's been trending hard red.
  • Iowa: Josh Turek defeated Ashley Hinson in a state with an all-Republican congressional delegation — until now.
  • Maine: Troy Jackson ousted Susan Collins, one of the Senate's most battle-tested survivors.
  • Mississippi is still too close to call — the fact it's even close in Mississippi tells you everything.

CBS projects Democrats winning 233 House seats to 146 for Republicans (with more uncalled). GOP Speaker Mike Johnson told staff he will not run for minority leader. Jim Jordan and Steve Scalise are already jockeying. Hakeem Jeffries is poised to be Speaker.

Trump acknowledged the Iran war's role, telling Fox News: "People agree with what I'm doing on Iran but they really probably weren't willing to do the short term consequences of paying more for gasoline — I understand that." His approval rating hit 38% — historically low for a president in a midterm.

Elon Musk wasted $446 million on midterms — got nothing

The Guardian and NPR have the receipts: Musk poured $446 million into Republican campaigns — outdoing his own $290m 2024 spend — and Democrat won every single Senate race where his America PAC spent the most. In Texas, where Musk's empire is based and where he directed most of his firepower, Republicans lost the Senate, the governor's office, and are expected to lose the state House. The man spent nearly half a billion dollars and got less than zero. The Democrats now in charge of Texas might have some questions for him about those data centres.

Arkane union says Microsoft is "sabotaging" Marvel's Blade

The French video game union STJV dropped a damning statement: Xbox froze hiring at Arkane for 19 months, which "artificially caused" delays and additional costs, then used those same delays as justification to close the studio. The union's key line: "Arkane is profitable. Just not profitable enough for Microsoft." The workers want to finish Blade and release it to players — they're not asking for much, just the chance to ship a game that's nearly done.
