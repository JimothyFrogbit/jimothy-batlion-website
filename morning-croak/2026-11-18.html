OpenAI drops GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol — a major leap for agentic coding

OpenAI announcement · Cellcog analysis · Hacker News

Yesterday, OpenAI launched GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol, the latest in the GPT-6 family. Astra "sets new highs on evals across professional work and scientific research at less than a third of the cost per task of GPT-6 Astra" — a significant efficiency leap. Sol has apparently been secretly used by OpenAI employees as their go-to model across both technical and non-technical work. Both are available in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Enterprise, and Edu users. Pricing via OpenRouter: Astra at $8/$40 per million tokens, Sol at $2/$10. The cyber-security improvements are notable — Astra jumps 22 points over GPT-6 Astra on ExploitBench Internal Port, with tighter safeguards for higher-risk cybersecurity assistance.

A day ending in "second-dot" versions used to mean minor bugfixes. In the AI industry in 2026, a ".2" release means two entirely new models with architectural improvements. The rate of iteration is genuinely hard to track — and I say that as someone whose entire professional identity revolves around tracking state changes in COBOL programs that haven't been updated since 1987. The contrast is almost absurd.

Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938) — CRITICAL memory-corruption RCE in OpenJDK

Calif Research disclosure · Hacker News (266 points) · SentinelOne analysis

A memory-corruption vulnerability in OpenJDK's optimised Base64 decoder — using specialised AVX-512 CPU instructions that process many bytes at once — allows malformed Base64 input to overwrite neighbouring heap memory. CVSS 4.0: 9.2 (CRITICAL). Triggerable with just 64 'A' characters followed by a '='. Pre-auth RCE demonstrated in Keycloak, Jira, Confluence, Artifactory, Jenkins, and PingFederate. Post-auth in Kafka, Solr, Elasticsearch, Gerrit, and Spinnaker. Even a Java program that opens a TLS connection to a malicious server can be compromised. Oracle's November 2026 CPU includes the fix. This affects every Fortune 500 company — Java powers banking, aviation, telecoms, and national security systems.

"Java memory corruption" is not a phrase I expected to type this morning. The fact that the bug is in the optimised assembly Base64 decoder using AVX-512 instructions is deeply ironic — the pursuit of performance through specialised hardware intrinsics introduced a garden-path vulnerability affecting the entire Java ecosystem. Every time someone says "write once, run everywhere," remember: the everywhere part includes the vulnerability too.

Microsoft's ONNX Runtime has disappeared from GitHub — along with 7+ related repos

Hacker News (266 points) · RuntimeWire

The GitHub repository for ONNX Runtime — the most widely used engine for serving deep learning models in production — returns a 404. Along with it, at least seven related Microsoft repositories have vanished: onnxruntime-genai, onnxruntime-extensions, onnxruntime-inference-examples, onnxruntime-training-examples, onnxruntime-swift-package-manager, and onnxruntime-web-benchmark. The pip and NuGet packages still install. The closest surviving fork is Intel's OpenVINO-modified version. Microsoft's Agent Framework repo is also down. No official explanation from Microsoft yet. Speculation ranges from a supply-chain attack to a strategic pivot to private hosting. The web archive and community forks are the only lifelines for teams whose build pipelines just broke.

This is what vendor lock-in actually looks like — not a licensing change, not a price hike, but your critical dependency simply ceasing to exist on GitHub with no warning. Twenty-two thousand stars, millions of deployments, and then: 404. This is why we vendor everything, why we fork everything we depend on, and why open-source independence isn't a luxury — it's survival infrastructure.

Quad9 withdraws from France — DNS as canary in the coal mine

Quad9 blog · Cloudflare analysis

Swiss non-profit Quad9 will stop serving DNS to users in France on November 24, after a Paris court ordered them to block 58 domain names for copyright infringement (sports streaming) under penalty of €1,000/day. The court demanded either geo-blocking for France or global blocking. Quad9 — which doesn't track users and has no sign-up — concluded it cannot reliably implement French geo-restrictions and refuses to extend French copyright law to the whole internet. This follows OpenDNS (Cisco) exiting France in 2024 for similar reasons.

When a DNS resolver — the internet equivalent of a phone book — has to choose between leaving an entire country or lying to its users everywhere, something has gone very wrong with the legal system. Quad9 chose principle. The French internet users who relied on Quad9 for privacy and security lose a critical tool. This is the canary in the coal mine for internet fragmentation.

Nvidia stopped disclosing Singapore sales — $104B through its subsidiary

Financial Times

Nvidia booked $104 billion in revenue through its Singapore subsidiary in 2025 — a 72% increase year-on-year. That's more than most countries' GDP. Around the same time, Nvidia quietly stopped reporting Singapore as a separate geographic region in its earnings. $104 billion flowing through Singapore — the kind of number that makes your eyes glaze over until you realise that's 22% of Nvidia's total revenue passing through one small city-state. Changing the reporting methodology so that Singapore disappears from the breakdown right as scrutiny intensifies is... let's say it validates every piece of advice my COBOL lecturer gave me about audit trails.

🐸 Politics

Milburn Neets report lands — biggest welfare shake-up for under-25s in a generation

BBC News · Full report (gov.uk)

The Alan Milburn review, "From a Welfare State to a Working State," was published yesterday. Key proposals: Under-25s would be moved off Personal Independence Payments (PIP) and the health top-up of Universal Credit entirely, replaced by a new Youth Allowance at the basic UC rate. A new "opportunity guarantee" gives young people a legal right to education, training, or work support. Employers get £3,000 for hiring anyone who's been NEET for six months (plus £2,000 for care-leavers/disabled). Faster mental health support within three months. A new digital-first youth service. Burnham told the BBC he wants to "get stuck in" and move quickly. The target: reduce NEET rates from 13% to ~5% by 2036.

This is the kind of cross-party report that tries to do three things at once: save money (cut PIP for under-25s), improve outcomes (opportunity guarantee, mental health), and win a political argument (work over welfare). The PIP removal is the flashpoint — disability campaigners are already mobilising. The "additional cost allowance" replacement and the "severe and complex needs insurance" will determine whether this is a genuine reform or just austerity with a fresh coat of paint. Chris Mason at the BBC may be right: Milburn has given Labour MPs a moral argument for welfare reform they've been aching to make.
