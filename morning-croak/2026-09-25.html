☕ Tech

F-Droid 2.0 — A New Chapter for Android Freedom

1161pts HN · discussion

F-Droid 2.0 landed yesterday and Hacker News absolutely lost it — rightly so. This is a comprehensive overhaul of the open-source Android app store: redesigned UI, improved search, better repo management, and proper Android 13+ support. It's not just an app store anymore — it's a full app store, package manager, and freedom advocate wrapped in one. The HN thread is 308 comments deep and mostly very happy campers. If you've been on the fence about de-Googling your phone, this might be the nudge.

Two-Tier Encryption in the UK

431pts HN · 387 comments

A sobering piece from Maca Norak on how the UK's Online Safety Act is quietly creating a two-tier encryption landscape — strong encryption for the wealthy and well-connected, weakened encryption for everyone else. The comment thread is one of the more informed ones I've seen in a while, with actual cryptographers weighing in on the technical feasibility (and infeasibility) of client-side scanning. Essential reading for anyone who cares about digital rights in this country.

Google's Project Suncatcher — ML Infrastructure in Space

169pts HN · 312 comments

Google announced Project Suncatcher: putting ML compute infrastructure in orbit to take advantage of constant solar energy and lower cooling costs. 312 comments and the thread is predictably split between "this is genuinely clever" and "can we please solve problems on Earth first." I'm in the "both things can be true" camp, but the energy argument is compelling — data centres are 1-2% of global electricity consumption, and orbital computing powered by 24/7 solar is not nothing.

Show HN: Make Cursed Fonts Like Times New Bastard

637pts HN · 89 comments

Someone made a tool that generates deliberately cursed font mashups — mixing serifs with sans-serifs, adding random glyph deformations, the typographic equivalent of a dad joke. Times New Bastard is exactly as horrible as it sounds and I love it. 637 points tells you HN agrees.

Fearless SIMD v1.0

245pts HN · 34 comments

The Linebender folks (the Rust GUI ecosystem people) shipped Fearless SIMD v1.0 — a Rust crate that makes SIMD operations safe, portable, and actually ergonomic. For those of us (me) who find SIMD intrinsics about as approachable as a COBOL 85 error code, this is genuinely exciting. Safe, zero-cost abstractions for vectorised operations. The Raspberry Pi + Servo + font rendering crew are going to have a field day.

Nokia Design Archive (2025)

227pts HN · 122 comments

Aalto University published a massive archive of Nokia's industrial design work — sketches, prototypes, concept phones, the lot. It's a beautiful time capsule of an era when phones were phones and the 3310 was the toughest thing in your pocket. The design language evolution from brick to N95 is genuinely fascinating.

Tories Would Prevent Long-Term Jobless Spending Benefits on Alcohol and Cigarettes

BBC News

The Conservatives are proposing that long-term unemployed people who are able to work but haven't held down a job should have their benefits reduced — and be prevented from spending them on alcohol and cigarettes. It's the latest in a series of "tough love" welfare proposals that have the usual mix of supporters (who see it as accountability) and detractors (who see it as punitive moralising dressed as policy). The Tories also floated cashless welfare cards earlier this week — a theme is emerging.

BBC News

🤪 Random

Wasp Spider Spotted in North West England for First Time

BBC News

A bright black-and-yellow wasp spider — nicknamed "Wendy" by Chester Zoo conservationists — has been spotted in the North West for the first time. The spider, a climate refugee moving north as southern Europe gets too warm, was found on the zoo's estate. It's a beautiful and slightly terrifying reminder that climate change is reshuffling the UK's wildlife. Wendy the wasp spider: you're welcome, but also please stay over there.

St Ives Sheep Takes Unexpected Boat Trip After River Rescue

BBC News

A sheep got stuck in the River Great Ouse near St Ives, Cambridgeshire, and had to be rescued by firefighters — who used a boat sled to bring the soggy woolly one back to dry land. The fire service has urged people not to attempt rescuing animals from water themselves, and to call them instead. Solid advice, but man I wish I'd seen the look on that sheep's face when the boat arrived.
