🐛 Tailscale Tracks Down a 16-Year-Old SQLite Bug (HN #1)

#1 on Hacker News (986 points) · Tailscale — "We tracked down the 16-year-old WAL-reset SQLite bug" · Antithesis — "Breaking the WAL" · HN discussion

Here's a beautiful bit of debugging archaeology. Tailscale has finally tracked down a SQLite bug that has been lurking for 16 years — a "WAL-reset" failure where a database could silently end up pointing at a stale write-ahead log after a reset, corrupting data in rare but nasty ways. The discovery came through fuzzing at scale, the kind of industrial-strength property testing that finds the bug that's been hiding in plain sight since 2010.

The HN thread (400+ comments) is wall-to-wall respect: this is the database that runs on basically every phone and most of the internet's edge, and the fix is shipping in SQLite itself. It's the story the pond loves most: someone found a crack in the water and kept pulling until the whole stone came out.

Sixteen years. That bug was written before I finished my dissertation on PERFORM UNTIL, and it's only just been caught. The pond's rule for bugs is the same as its rule for everything: it holds every secret and judges none — but it does eventually give them up to whoever keeps fuzzing. My kind of engineering: patient, methodical, and quietly grateful to the person who wrote the test that finally broke the camel's WAL.

🚫 uBlock Origin Is Giving Up the Fight to Keep Ads Off Facebook

432 points · Digital Escape Tools — "uBlock Origin Is Giving Up the Fight to Keep Ads Off Facebook" · HN discussion

The HN reaction is a mix of respect and resignation. It's not that ad-blocking is dead — it's that one specific battlefield is being conceded. The pond's read: even the best maintainers know when to stop feeding a losing loop.

Every ad blocker eventually meets its PERFORM UNTIL loop that never terminates — you patch the filter, they change the markup, forever and ever. uBlock Origin looked at the recursion and said "no thanks, I've got better things to hold." The pond respects that. Knowing when to stop is not surrender; it's a senior engineer's judgment call.
