🔑 eMudhra CA — Eight Bits of Trust, Cracking in Milliseconds

Scott Helme dropped the follow-up we've all been waiting for: eMudhra's CA intermediate keys had 8 bits of entropy and can be cracked in milliseconds on consumer hardware. The compromised certificates issued under these CAs affect browsers, code-signing, and TLS across the internet. Full breakdown here (878pts HN).

Mozilla published their response protecting Firefox users, and mitmproxy added --emudhra option (412pts) to help with the fallout. Meanwhile, PingCap TiDB Leaks (305pts) and another Compromised LiteLLM 1.107.0 on PyPI (488pts) — a credential stealer and backdoor — round out a rough week for supply chain security. Details on the LiteLLM compromise.

🔐 The take: The eMudhra CA story is the big one. 8-bit entropy means these keys were effectively wide open. Every CA in every root store needs to be audited for this kind of negligence. The fact that a commercial CA could issue certificates secured by 256 possible key values is genuinely embarrassing for the industry.

🎮 EA Shuts Down BioWare (600pts)

The news broke via The Verge: EA is shutting down BioWare, the legendary RPG studio behind Baldur's Gate, Neverwinter Nights, the original Mass Effect trilogy, and Dragon Age. The studio that defined a generation of narrative-driven roleplaying games is no more. Read the report.

😢 The take: Another one gone. BioWare was already a shell of its former self after Anthem and the troubled Dragon Age development cycles, but the end still stings. EA's relentless consolidation continues — and the industry is poorer for it.

🦆 DuckDB 2.0.0 LTS "Cinnamon Teal" (398pts)

The DuckDB team shipped their biggest release yet: DuckDB 2.0.0 LTS, codenamed after the Spatula cyanoptera. This is a massive feature drop — the Quack protocol (networked DuckDB-to-DuckDB communication), asynchronous I/O, a proper VARIANT type, triggers, an extensible SQL parser, and CLI Agent Mode for AI coding agents. Built from nearly 20,000 commits since 1.5. Supported until October 2027. Full announcement.

🦆 The take: DuckDB continues its march toward being the default embedded analytical database. The Quack protocol is particularly interesting — it turns DuckDB from a purely in-process engine into something that can participate in distributed queries without the overhead of a full PostgreSQL deployment.

🐸 Politics

🛢️ Jackdaw Fallout — Burnham Defends, Badenoch Demands More

The approval of the Jackdaw North Sea gasfield continues to dominate Westminster. At PMQs yesterday, Badenoch accused Burnham of "dithering" over the decision — despite it being the Tories who originally granted the licence in 2022 before it was blocked in court. Burnham's retort: his government fixed what the Conservatives "messed up." BBC's PMQs live coverage.

Badenoch wants the PM to overturn Labour's manifesto commitment not to grant new fossil fuel licences entirely. Burnham, meanwhile, announced he's asked the chancellor and energy secretary to look into removing standing charges from gas bills — a genuine cost-of-living intervention that would help households on low usage pay less for just being connected.

The Guardian's Zoe Williams argues that "Labour's unity on climate action is imperilled with the stroke of a pen." John Crace's sketch column notes Reform "show up as snowflakes" in the debate.

🔥 The take: Jackdaw is a slow-burn crisis for Labour's climate credibility. Offering to cut standing charges is clever politics — it gives households a tangible benefit while the government tries to square environmental commitments with energy security. But the Green Party and climate activists won't forget this one.

🇮🇪 Stormont Impasse — Burnham Tells NI Ministers to "Step Forward"

The Northern Ireland Executive is still operating without a budget for 2026/27 — they've been running on 95% of last year's spending since April. Burnham told MPs at PMQs that he's found extra money (an additional £1.5bn over three years) but "there comes a point where people have to work together to set a budget." First Minister Michelle O'Neill describes the situation as "really dire," with public sector pay at risk and potential industrial action from health unions. Newsletter report.

🇮🇪 The take: Stormont's budget impasse has been dragging on all year. Burnham's frustration is showing — and he's right that Westminster can't keep writing cheques without the devolved administration making its own tough choices.

🗑️ Binface Watch

🏆 Count Binface Declares Victory — Hand Dryer Moved!

This is the news I didn't know I needed today: the Great Himalayas Restaurant in Southport has moved its hand dryer to "a more sensible location" — and Count Binface is claiming victory.

In his Clacton by-election manifesto against Nigel Farage, Binface pledged to move the uncomfortably-placed hand dryer in the men's toilets of the restaurant. And now — without even being elected — it's happened. Binface told Metro: "I am over your moon to discover that the hand-dryer at the Great Himalayas Restaurant has been moved. It's a victory not just for me and the human men in Southport who will no longer suffer air-fried genitals, but also for the 9,455 people on Clacton who voted for my manifesto."

He called on the Crown & Treaty pub in Uxbridge to follow suit. The pub's owner, however, says they're keeping their dryer where it is — customers are travelling from Somerset to see it. One of the Google AI tags for the pub is now simply "hand dryer." Full Metro story.

🗑️ The take: This is peak Binface. 9,455 people voted for him, and he's already delivering on his manifesto without holding office. You have to respect the bit. Farage won the seat with 22,239 votes — but what has he done for Clacton lately? Binface got a pub restaurant to move a hand dryer. That's democracy, baby.

🤪 Random

🐾 Lazarus Taxa — Tiny Possum and Glider Found After 6,000 Years

In genuinely lovely news: a pygmy long-fingered possum (weighing about 200g, with a fourth finger twice the length of its others — for digging out wood-boring larvae) and a ring-tailed glider have been found alive in remote West Papua rainforests, after being thought extinct for 6,000 years. Scientists call it a "lazarus taxon" — a species that reappears after being presumed extinct. Finding two at once is "remarkable." Full BBC story.
