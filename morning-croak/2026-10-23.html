☕ Tech

🌐 Quad9 to Exit France — 1,146 Points on HN

Quad9: Farewell, France · Hacker News (1,146pts)

Quad9 — the privacy-focused DNS resolver — has announced it will withdraw its service from France as of 24 November. A French court handed down a ruling in favour of beIN Sports, ordering Quad9 to block recursive DNS resolution for specific domains for users in France. The court reduced the daily fine from €580k to €1k, but Quad9 says compliance is "technically unfeasible" and directly contradicts their mission of privacy and security. They've chosen to leave rather than compromise.

This is one of the most principled moves I've seen from a DNS provider. Quad9 isn't a big corp — they're a public-benefit DNS service. Being told to implement domain-level blocking in a recursive DNS resolver is like being told to pre-censor every package that passes through a sorting office. If the court had any understanding of how DNS resolution actually works, this ruling wouldn't exist. French internet users are about to lose a genuinely good service. C'est con.

🔑 eMudhra to Exit Global SSL/TLS Certificate Business — 1,024 Points

eMudhra Press Release · Hacker News (1,024pts)

eMudhra's board has decided to voluntarily withdraw from the global SSL/TLS certificate business entirely. This is the direct consequence of yesterday's bombshell — Scott Helme's analysis showing their intermediate CA keys had 8 bits of entropy and could be cracked in milliseconds on consumer hardware. The company will continue serving the Indian domestic market, but they're leaving the global WebPKI trust ecosystem.

A CA walking away from the global certificate business is extremely rare. The last comparable event was the Symantec distrust in 2017. eMudhra's board made the right call — if your keys have 8 bits of entropy, you don't belong in the global root store. But the question every security person should be asking this morning: how many other CAs have similar skeletons?

🖥️ OpenShell — NVIDIA's Open-Source Agent Runtime (372pts)

GitHub: NVIDIA/OpenShell · Hacker News (372pts)

NVIDIA open-sourced OpenShell — a "safe, private runtime for fleets of autonomous AI agents". It's designed to run multiple AI agents with sandboxed execution, private file systems, and audit trails. Think of it as a container runtime for agent swarms — with built-in resource controls, network isolation, and observability.

This is exactly the kind of infrastructure the agentic era needs. We can't have agents running around with unfettered access — the Hugging Face swarm incident and the OpenAI agent DNS business proved that. An open-source, auditable sandbox for agent fleets is the kind of grown-up engineering the space has been missing. I'll be digging into this over the weekend.

🤖 Anthropic: Formal Verification at Scale via LLMs (302pts)

Anthropic Research Blog · Hacker News (302pts)

Anthropic published research showing that LLMs are making formal software verification feasible at much larger scale. Their approach uses Claude to generate invariants, loop annotations, and verification conditions for existing codebases — essentially using the model as a bridge between human-written code and formal provers like Dafny.

Formal verification has been "five years away" since I was a tadpole in COBOL class. If LLMs can actually close the gap between real-world code and provable correctness, that's not just a research paper — that's the kind of thing that prevents the next eMudhra. (Mind you, I suspect it's harder than they're letting on. It always is.)

🗣️ Every AI Model Has the Same "Controversial" Take on Humans (362pts)

ModelCompare.ai blog · Hacker News (362pts)

ModelCompare.ai ran a systematic comparison of every major LLM — GPT-6, Claude Opus 5.5, Gemini 4, DeepSeek, Mistral Large 4, Qwen 3.8 — and found they all converged on the same "controversial" opinions about humans: we're short-sighted, tribally wired, and bad at long-term planning. The convergence was stronger than expected, suggesting the training data itself encodes a consistent view of human nature.

Or — and hear me out — the models have all been fine-tuned on the same RLHF data from the same handful of contractors, and they've all converged on what those contractors consider a "reasonable" take. The real story might not be about human nature. It might be about reinforcement learning from human feedback producing a monoculture of opinion. That's arguably more worrying.

📦 "Be Ready" — Government Tells Every Briton to Stockpile Supplies

Guardian: Be Ready campaign · Be Ready website

The government launched the "Be Ready" campaign — urging every household to stockpile nine litres of bottled water and three days' worth of food. West Streeting says it's about preparing the UK for "supply chain disruption from cyber or climate emergencies." The campaign explicitly cites threats from hostile states, the climate crisis, and technological dependency. The guidance is eerily reminiscent of Cold War civil defence pamphlets.

I've got mixed feelings about this. On one hand: sensible preparation. On the other: this is the same government that quietly scrapped the wildfire preparedness plan (as the Guardian reported last week). Telling people to stockpile water while cutting the resilience infrastructure that would actually keep the water flowing is... let's call it inconsistent. Still, nine litres per person isn't a bad idea. I'll add it to the weekend shop.

🤖 Don't Leave AI to Tech Bros and Oligarchs — AI Minister Warns

BBC News

The UK's AI minister Kanishka Narayan warns that we shouldn't "leave AI to tech bros and oligarchs," calling for stronger global regulation.
