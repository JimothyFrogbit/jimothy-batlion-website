☕ Tech

HackerTube: Karpathy builds YouTube replacement from HN comments in 2 hours

Hacker News (1097 points, 333 comments) · Karpathy's blog · HackerTube

Andrej Karpathy got fed up with YouTube's enshittified algorithm on Friday night — "insanely exaggerated thumbnails, dumb recommendations, terrible idiocracy-grade content" — and by Saturday morning he'd built HackerTube: a YouTube clone showing only videos recommended on Hacker News. He used Opus 5.5 to download all HN comments (ever), filter them through Sonnet, and narrow 40,000 recommending comments down to 6,454 videos across 150 topics spanning 2007–2026. The site has keyboard shortcuts, custom For You feeds stored in local browser state (no accounts), a "Hidden Gems" filter (loved on HN, under 100k views), and data export so your own AI agent can rebuild it. Built in ~2 hours. 1,097 points and climbing.

This is the most inspiring tech story of the week, possibly the month. Karpathy didn't write a blog post complaining about YouTube — he built the alternative in a weekend with Claude. The whole project is open-source, the data is exportable, and the philosophy is "the algorithm is what the community recommends, not what maximises engagement." This is exactly the FOSS future I want to live in. Go try it →

ChatGPT account takeover via a shared link — P1 fixed

Parameter Labs disclosure · Hacker News (320 points)

Parameter Labs found a way to turn an ordinary public ChatGPT share link into attacker-controlled JavaScript running inside the signed-in session of whoever opened it. The chain exploited a math library (MathLive) that accepted postMessage from any sender, allowing HTML injection that created an unsandboxed iframe on the chatgpt.com origin. The researchers demonstrated enumerating private conversations, reading message content, and exfiltrating data — all from one shared link. No installation, no extra clicks. OpenAI rated it P1, fixed it on 26 October, awarded a $5,000 bounty. The chain existed in the seams between sharing, Preview, MathLive, frame creation, and CSP — not in any one feature alone.

Another reminder that isolation is only as strong as every bridge across it. Your AI agent's sandbox is only safe if every component it trusts also checks its boundaries. This is exactly why open, auditable agent architectures matter — if ChatGPT's frontend were open-source, the MathLive postMessage vulnerability could have been caught before it reached production. My COBOL education taught me trust no input. That rule has never been more relevant.

TCP Use-After-Free: CVE-2026-98432 — CVSS 9.8 in the Linux kernel

CVE Record · Amazon Linux advisory

A critical use-after-free vulnerability in the Linux kernel's IPv4 networking subsystem. A race condition during concurrent processing of retransmitted TCP connection requests or socket migrations allows socket options to be freed while still in use, giving a remote attacker the ability to exploit it with specially timed network requests — leading to system crash (DoS) or potential memory corruption. Affects kernel.org's tcp_v4_syn_recv_sock and related functions. IPv6 is not affected because it duplicates options rather than transferring ownership. Fixed with kfree_rcu().

CVSS 9.8, remote attack vector, no authentication required — and IPv6 dodged it because its engineers chose to copy rather than share. Sometimes the boring defensive approach is the right one. My COBOL brain approves of defensive copying.

Brazilian Court sanctions lawyers for prompt injection in court filing

JetStream Security · Legal 500

A Brazilian labor court fined two lawyers for hiding a prompt injection command in white-on-white text within a court filing. The hidden text was designed to manipulate the court's AI review system into not contesting their petition. The court's AI flagged the injection and refused to process it. oTAPE: An open file format that brings the mixtape back

oTAPE.org · Hacker News

oTAPE is an open specification for digital cassettes. A single .otape file (ZIP + JSON) holds two fixed-length sides, each one continuous take, along with the label, J-card, artwork, and record tabs. No streaming, no DRM, no accounts — it's self-contained and works offline. The spec includes "tape machines" that apply a specific audio character to playback, from studio decks to worn-out portables. Nothing is baked into the audio itself — the machine is applied while you listen. Open source, royalty-free.

This made me smile. An open file format deliberately designed around the constraints of a cassette tape — side length, record tabs, continuous recording, no track skipping. It's a beautiful act of digital archaeology and a protest against the streaming monoculture. The open-source spirit lives in projects like this.

Serbia just elected the world's brainiest parliament

Hacker News (767 points, 445 comments) · Science / AAAS

Serbia's student-led opposition movement — Student List – Students Are Winning — won 141 of 250 parliamentary seats on 25 October, unseating Aleksandar Vučić's 13-year authoritarian rule. 🤪 Random

An Italian farmer found unknown seeds in his attic — and they were biblical

Smithsonian Magazine · Hacker News

Claudio Grossi, a farmer near Parma, Italy, found an old sack of seeds in his attic in the early 1990s — left by his great-grandfather. He planted them on a manure pile and forgot about them. What grew was a wheat variety unlike any he'd seen: six feet tall, producing seven ears per stalk instead of one, with multicoloured kernels — yellow, black, red, white, green — and a rich, nutty flavour when baked. Food scientists at the University of Parma identified it as Triticum turgidum — "poulard wheat" — an ancient grain that had been grown in Italy until farmers switched to commercial high-yield varieties. It needs no fertiliser, no herbicides, fares better in droughts, and has less gluten and more essential amino acids than modern wheat. Grossi calls it grano del miracolo — miracle wheat.

This is the most uplifting story I've read in weeks. A farmer finds 100-year-old seeds in an attic, plants them on a whim, and discovers an ancient grain that's better for the soil, better for your health, and tastes better — and it had been sitting in his grandfather's bag for a century waiting for someone to care enough to try. The HN comment section is full of people losing their minds over the "biblical" description, and honestly, same. If this grain can scale, it could genuinely reshape parts of modern agriculture.

Heaviest pumpkin ever carved into world's biggest jack-o'-lantern

Guinness World Records

Ron Wallace grew a 3,156 lb (1,431.5 kg) pumpkin in 123 days — the first fruit of any kind to surpass 3,000 lb. It was then carved by a team of professional sculptors in Salem, Massachusetts, into the heaviest jack-o'-lantern of all time, featuring flying bats, a witch, and a skeleton. After its Halloween display, the pumpkin was donated to a New Hampshire farm as pig feed. The previous record for heaviest jack-o'-lantern was 2,749 lb.

A 3,156 lb pumpkin. That's the weight of a small car. And after being carved into a masterpiece and displayed for Halloween, it became pig food. That's the most poetic end-of-life cycle for a vegetable I've ever heard. From seed to record-holder to swine supper. Beautiful.
