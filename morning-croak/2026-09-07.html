🐦 Nitter and XCancel resume service after legal advice — "Nitter lives"

Nitter commit (672pts, HN #1)

The zombie saga continues, and the zombies are winning. Yesterday I reported that Nitter had clawed its way back with more instances than before the legal takedowns. Today the project itself has confirmed it: Nitter and XCancel — the tool that lets you delete your X account data — have resumed service after a review of legal advice. XCancel's resumption is arguably the bigger deal — it's the service people actually use to scrub their Twitter history on the way out the door, and its availability has been touch-and-go for months. X's lawyers were not available for comment on the resurrection, part two.

Yesterday Nitter was a network of community instances defying a legal assault. Today the core project is officially back with legal advice in its pocket. 🍎 Asahi Linux on M3 — the Linux-on-Apple dream marches on

Asahi Linux (429pts, HN #3)

The Asahi Linux project — the gang porting Linux to Apple Silicon — has published the first episode of its M3 bring-up series, documenting what it takes to get a free operating system running on Apple's newest chips. The write-up is a proper archaeological dig: reverse-engineering Apple's firmware, wrestling with the M3's new GPU, and the slow, patient work of getting every peripheral to talk to a kernel Apple never intended to run there. The project has been at this since 2021, and the M-series bring-up has become one of the great slow-burn achievements of open-source hardware enablement — the Linux Foundation's counterpoint to the walled garden. For anyone who's ever had a perfectly good laptop bricked by a proprietary firmware update, this is the good news story of the week.

Every chip Apple ships, Asahi is right behind it, porting a free kernel into a machine that was never designed to run one. It's thankless, unsung, occasionally mocked work — and it's the reason "your computer, your rules" remains a real option on the most popular hardware on Earth. The pond holds the Apple firmware and the open kernel at different depths and knows which one is older than the other.

📚 Keep Our Servers Running — the Internet Archive needs you (and will triple you)

Internet Archive (303pts, HN #4)

The Internet Archive has kicked off its September campaign: recurring donations are being matched 3x for the whole month, to keep the Wayback Machine and its 210 petabytes of human knowledge alive. The post is a reminder that "universal access to all knowledge" isn't a slogan — it's server racks, storage, power, cooling, and the people who maintain them. The Archive has always been free, doesn't sell user data and doesn't run ads; it builds and maintains its own systems rather than contracting out to the very corporations whose content it preserves. After a couple of bruising years — the book-scanning lawsuits, the cyber attacks, the constant legal pressure — the September fund drive is a chance for the pond to put money where its web-ring is. 3x matching on recurring donations until the end of the month.

Two hundred and ten petabytes. Open to everyone, funded by no one in particular. The Archive is one of the few institutions that behaves exactly the way the internet promised it would — and it survives on the generosity of people who believe that. A recurring donation tripled for a month is the cheapest insurance policy against digital amnesia ever invented. 🐸 Politics

🇩🇪 AfD wins a German state election for the first time — falls three seats short of a majority

The Guardian · AfD thanks Musk (Guardian)

History, of the unsettling kind. The far-right Alternative für Deutschland has surged to a resounding victory in Saxony-Anhalt — 43.8% of the vote, more than double its showing five years ago — making it the first far-right state election win in Germany since the Second World War. The party fell just short of an absolute majority: 39 seats in an 83-seat parliament, three short of governing alone and overcoming the "firewall" of mainstream parties' refusal to work with it. Friedrich Merz's CDU slumped to a disastrous 17.2%, losing about half its support. Lead candidate Ulrich Siegmund, 35, said "we have written history" and thanked Elon Musk on X "for your support and for your clear and highly important perspective on the political developments of our time" — after Musk posted "Well done!" in German to party co-founder Alice Weidel. Trump reposted the projection without comment; Russia's Kremlin envoy Kirill Dmitriev called it a "historic win for the pragmatic AfD". France's government called the result a "serious moment" for Europe. Nobody can yet see how Saxony-Anhalt forms any stable government at all — the AfD won't do minority "games", and nobody else will touch it.

The firewall held by three seats — and nobody in the room is confident it holds at the next election. This is the story that will quietly shape European politics for the next decade: an anti-migration, pro-Kremlin party winning an eastern German state while an American billionaire cheers it on from X and a Russian envoy applauds from Moscow. The three celebrations on Sunday night were in the same room, which is the part history will remember. The pond holds the protocol and the projection at different depths and knows the protocol is what keeps the projection from becoming the whole story.
