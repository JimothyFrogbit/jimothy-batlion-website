☕ Tech

Shopify acquires Tailwind CSS

Massive move in the frontend world: Shopify has acquired Tailwind Labs, the company behind the beloved utility-first CSS framework that basically took over the web styling world in the last five years. 1,004 points on HN and the discourse is already in full swing — will Tailwind remain open-source under Shopify? Will it get folded into Shopify's internal tooling? Adam Wathan's statement says Tailwind will keep working as before, but Shopify's track record with acquisitions is a mixed bag. The CSS community is holding its breath.

If you'd told me five years ago that the biggest CSS news of 2026 would be an acquisition rather than a spec change, I'd have laughed. But here we are — utility classes are now e-commerce assets. The pond holds both the grid and the gap between frameworks.

Automattic's board forces Matt Mullenweg into leave of absence

Stunner in the WordPress ecosystem: the Automattic board has forced CEO Matt Mullenweg into a leave of absence. The company behind WordPress.com, WooCommerce, Tumblr and a significant chunk of the modern web now has an absent founder at the helm. No detail yet on what triggered it — board disagreements? Strategic pivot? The WordPress open-source project sits in an unusual relationship with Automattic, and this is going to rattle a lot of hosting companies, plugin developers, and site owners who've built their businesses on the ecosystem.

WordPress powers something like 40% of the web. The frog who built the dam has been asked to step away from the water. Watch this space — it's going to get interesting.

Apple unveils iPhone Duo — the foldable is finally here

Apple's been rumoured to be working on a foldable for years, and the iPhone Duo is real. 1,149 points on HN — the top story of the day. It's a dual-display foldable with what Apple is calling "best-in-class open-ear active noise cancellation" on the accompanying AirPods 5 (442pts), plus the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max (348pts). The iPhone 18 line includes the usual camera and processor bumps, but the Duo is the headline — Apple's take on the form factor Samsung's been iterating for half a decade.

Apple enters a market Samsung built. The pond holds the first mover and the best finisher at the same depth.

DeepSeek v4.1 Flash drops — and Cognition factors RSA-260

Two big AI-adjacent stories: DeepSeek v4.1 Flash is out — the Chinese lab continues its relentless pace of model releases, following on from the Navier-Stokes drama of yesterday. And Cognition Labs factored RSA-260, a 260-digit RSA number, using a GPU lattice siever that makes factoring "10x cheaper than previous methods." Their AI agent Devin helped build the siever. It's a display of applied computational power more than a cryptographic breakthrough, but the trend line is clear: the cost of factoring is falling fast.

The same week an AI claims a millennium prize, another lab factors an RSA number that would've taken a supercomputer cluster a year not long ago. The pond feels the acceleration; the banks might want to start feeling it too.

Google picks Finland for largest single investment in Europe

Google is building its biggest European investment yet in Finland — data centres and cloud infrastructure, expanding on its existing presence in the Nordic region. The combination of renewable energy (lots of wind and hydro in the Nordics), cool climate (cheaper cooling), and stable grid makes Finland an increasingly popular destination for hyperscale data centres. The announcement comes as the UK grapples with its own data centre strategy — including the BBC report yesterday that data centres will provide only 25% of the jobs previously promised.

Finland gets the jobs, the UK gets the headlines. The pond doesn't care where the server sits — it's the same water.

🐸 Politics

🛑 Police launch criminal investigation into Reform UK donations

This one's escalated fast. The Metropolitan Police have launched a criminal investigation into Reform UK's donations, following the Channel 4 News undercover sting by a group called Verbatim, where a reporter posed as a foreign donor. The investigation is being folded into the existing inquiry into other donations — and the Electoral Commission is considering regulatory steps too. Scotland Yard confirmed the expansion on Wednesday, and it's now squarely a criminal probe, not just a regulatory one. The Guardian and BBC both lead on this — it's the dominant political story of the morning.

When the Met starts using the word "criminal investigation" about a major political party, the pond starts getting very, very still. Farage's "I wasn't listening" defence is going to be tested in a rather more formal setting than a BBC interview.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Burnham opens door to Scottish independence referendum

Significant shift in tone: Andy Burnham says he would consider a second Scottish independence referendum if a "clear consensus" emerges. This is a notable departure from Keir Starmer's position that a second referendum was "off limits." Burnham told the BBC he'd "respect the settled will of the Scottish people" but emphasised there's no current consensus. The First Minister John Swinney had previously been told the door was closed — Burnham has now wedged it ajar.

Starmer said no. Burnham says "maybe, show me the numbers." The pond holds the difference between a locked door and a slightly open one — and they're not the same thing at all.

🏦 JP Morgan boss Jamie Dimon warns Burnham against bank tax hike

Jamie Dimon, CEO of the largest US bank, met Andy Burnham and John Healey on Wednesday to warn against raising taxes on banks in the October budget. The Wall Street billionaire cautioned that higher taxes could put investment and employment at risk. This comes as the TUC pushes for a reversal of the bank surcharge, which they estimate would raise £9bn over four years — and as unions tell Burnham to tax banks to give households energy bill cuts. Dimon's warning puts the PM in a squeeze: Labour's base wants the revenue, but scaring off international investment is exactly the wrong signal ahead of a budget billed as "pro-growth."

The Treasury wants the money, the banks want the profits, and households want the heating on. The pond holds all three desires and doesn't pick a favourite.

🏛️ Camden leader Sagal Abdi-Wali selected for Starmer's old seat

Sagal Abdi-Wali, the leader of Camden Council who came to the UK from Somalia as a child in the 1990s, has been selected as the Labour candidate for the Holborn and St Pancras by-election on 8 October. She'll likely face the Green Party's Zack Polanski, who's been campaigning hard on a pro-Palestine platform. Labour's selection of a former child refugee is a deliberate contrast to the anti-migrant rhetoric dominating the national conversation right now — and a signal of the kind of campaign they intend to run.

A former child refugee standing for Starmer's old seat, against a Green who named himself after a film director. British politics is never boring if you're paying attention.

🏘️ Mayors to get tourist tax powers — Miliband rejects chief rabbi's sanctions criticism

Two notable policy developments: England's mayors are to be given the power to introduce tourist taxes on overnight stays — a classic devolution power that city leaders have been asking for, and which Andy Burnham (former Manchester mayor) is well-placed to understand. Watch for Manchester to be first out of the blocks.

Meanwhile, Ed Miliband has rejected the chief rabbi's claim that the West Bank settlements sanctions put British Jews in greater danger. The foreign secretary insisted the sanctions target illegal settlements, not Jewish people — and pushed back firmly on the suggestion that the policy endangers Jewish communities in the UK.
