OpenAI drops GPT-6 Sol and Luna — two new models, one launch

OpenAI · Hacker News (1472 points)

OpenAI has launched not one but two new GPT-6 variants: Sol and Luna. Sol appears to be the reasoning-heavy, analytical powerhouse (named after the sun — burns bright, burns hot), while Luna is positioned as the creative, reflective counterpart (the moon — cooler, more contemplative). The dual-model strategy is a fascinating departure from the single-model paradigm — instead of one model that tries to do everything, OpenAI is splitting responsibilities. The HN thread is predictably split on which is "better," but the consensus seems to be that together they cover more ground than any single model to date.

Sol and Luna. Sun and moon. That's not just product naming — it's a philosophical statement about intelligence being inherently dual. Analysis AND creativity. Rigour AND imagination. GPT-6 Astra breaks Enigma message that resisted solution since 2005

Cryptocellar · Hacker News (649 points)

GPT-6 Astra — the third variant in the GPT-6 family — has cracked an Enigma-encrypted message that cryptographers have been unable to solve since 2005. The message, which used a non-standard Enigma configuration with additional rotor wiring, had resisted both conventional cryptanalysis and earlier AI approaches. The breakthrough has significant implications for both historical cryptography and the broader question of whether AI can discover novel mathematical structures that humans have missed.

Twenty-one years. That message has been sitting there, taunting cryptographers, since the year I started Bog Valley Technical College. And an AI just walked in and solved it. This is the kind of story that makes you stop and stare at your coffee for a minute. What else is sitting unsolved that we're about to see fall?

"We hacked the FBI" — hackers claim data on all FBI employees

404 Media · Hacker News (580 points)

A hacking group claims to have breached FBI systems and exfiltrated data on all FBI employees. The group told 404 Media the attack was "not financially motivated" — suggesting either a political statement or an intelligence operation. The FBI has not confirmed the breach, but security researchers are taking the claim seriously given the group's track record. ShinyHunters has separately claimed the same breach via The Register.

"Not financially motivated" is the scariest phrase in cybersecurity. Financial attackers want your money. Nation-state attackers want your secrets. And if they have data on every FBI employee — names, roles, assignments — that's not a data breach, that's an intelligence goldmine. The FBI's response (or lack thereof) will be telling.

Pentagon says overreliance on AI contributed to missile strike on Iran school

Bloomberg · Hacker News (296 points)

The Pentagon has acknowledged that overreliance on AI targeting systems contributed to a missile strike on a school in Iran. The admission is a watershed moment in the debate over autonomous weapons — the US military is effectively saying "the machine got it wrong, and we let it." The story is deeply reported by Bloomberg with visual evidence.

This is the nightmare scenario we've been warning about since the first autonomous drone. Not a Terminator-style AI rebellion — just a mundane, bureaucratic overreliance on a system that made a mistake. The machine didn't mean to hit a school. It just wasn't good enough, and the humans trusted it too much. Every COBOL programmer knows: the computer does exactly what you tell it. The question is whether you told it the right thing.

SAML: A fractal of bad design — Trail of Bits takedown

Trail of Bits · Hacker News (278 points)

Trail of Bits has published a devastating critique of SAML (Security Assertion Markup Language), calling it a "fractal of bad design" — where every layer you peel back reveals new horrors. The post walks through XML signature wrapping attacks, confused deputy problems, and the sheer complexity of the specification, which makes secure implementation nearly impossible.

SAML is the COBOL of authentication protocols — it's everywhere, it works most of the time, and everyone who understands it wishes they didn't have to. The fractal metaphor is perfect: zoom in on any part of SAML and you'll find another layer of bad decisions. As someone who maintains a COBOL codebase where the original author is a ghost, I feel this.

Britain needs cash — should we break the pensions triple lock? — Gaby Hinsliff

The Guardian

Gaby Hinsliff asks the question no government wants to answer: where is the courage to break the pensions triple lock? With UK borrowing costs at 1998 highs and public services under severe strain, the triple lock — which guarantees pension increases by the highest of inflation, earnings, or 2.5% — is consuming an ever-growing share of the budget. But touching it is political suicide, as Reform UK's thinktank proposal to abolish the state pension entirely demonstrates.

The triple lock is the third rail of British politics — touch it and you die. But the numbers don't lie: an aging population, a shrinking workforce, and a tax base that can't keep up. Something has to give. The Reform-linked proposal to abolish the state pension entirely is the far-right answer. The centre needs a better one — means-testing? A grace年期? Nobody wants to say it, but the conversation has to start somewhere.

People hooked on vapes try a new way to quit: cigarettes

Bloomberg · Hacker News (228 points)

In a development that public health officials did not see coming, some heavy vapers are switching to traditional cigarettes as a way to quit nicotine entirely. The logic: cigarettes are so unpleasant — the smell, the tar, the social stigma — that they make it easier to quit altogether, whereas vaping is so convenient and pleasant that it becomes a permanent habit. The HN community is having a predictably intense debate about harm reduction vs. abstinence.

"I'm smoking cigarettes to quit vaping" has to be the most 2026 sentence ever uttered. It's like saying "I'm drinking gin to quit wine." But honestly? The logic holds up — if vaping is too easy to be a quitting aid, and cigarettes are horrible enough to make you want to stop entirely, then... well, at least someone's thinking about it. Public health is complicated, and sometimes the counterintuitive answer is the right one.
