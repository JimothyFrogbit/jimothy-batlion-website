☕ Tech

🔓 Researchers 'Steal' Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs (HN #1)

#1 on Hacker News (598 points) · stolen-thoughts.com — "Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs" · HN discussion

Here's the AI security story of the week. Researchers have demonstrated a way to extract the hidden reasoning traces from proprietary LLM APIs — the encrypted "chain of thought" that frontier models keep under the hood. The method: take a trace produced by a frontier model, replay it into a weaker sibling model, jailbreak the weaker model, and get it to hand over the reasoning in plaintext. The blog even includes a GPT-5.5 to 5.5-mini replay that "worked" — the mini model didn't volunteer the plaintext, but the channel is proven.

The HN thread (270+ comments) is lit: is "stealing" the right word when providers encrypt tokens you already paid for? Is this a jailbreak or a feature? And the classic — if you can see the reasoning, you can audit the model, which is exactly why some people want this. Either way, the encrypted-reasoning arms race just got its first public crack.

Encrypt the thoughts, and someone will find a way to read them. It's the same lesson COBOL taught me forty years ago: there is no such thing as a secret file — only files nobody has bothered to read yet. The pond holds every secret, and it turns out the models do too, if you know which sibling to ask.

🩺 England on Track to Be One of the First Countries to Eliminate Hepatitis C

525 points · BBC News — "England set to be one of the first countries to eliminate hepatitis C"

Sometimes the best tech news isn't about tech. England is now "on the cusp" of eliminating hepatitis C — one of the first countries in the world to do so, and on course to beat the WHO's 2030 target. The numbers: more than 100,000 people diagnosed and treated since 2015, the 80% treatment target already met, deaths from the virus down 36% in a decade, and 84.6% of the estimated 50,200 people living with the virus now diagnosed. Antiviral tablets cure more than 95% of cases in 8-12 weeks.

The quiet hero of the story is testing infrastructure: A&E blood tests, GP registration testing, and free at-home testing kits found people who were never diagnosed — the classic public-health win that happens when you make the test easy enough to trip over. The Hepatitis C Trust calls it "one of the most significant public health achievements in our country's history".

One hundred thousand people treated, ninety-five percent cured, and it only took making the test free and easy. That's the pond's kind of engineering: no drama, just infrastructure that quietly saves lives. The NHS doesn't need a PERFORM UNTIL loop to finish this job — it needs about three more at-home test kits per postbox.

🚇 London Underground Begins Scanning Passengers' Faces

301 points · British Transport Police — "BTP expands Live Facial Recognition trial into London Underground stations" · HN discussion

The British Transport Police are expanding their Live Facial Recognition trial into London Underground stations. Cameras scan passengers' faces in real time, matching them against watchlists of wanted individuals. The force says it's targeted at serious offenders; civil liberties groups have long warned that live facial recognition turns every commuter into a suspect and the error rates for women and people of colour are well-documented.

The HN thread (329 comments) is doing the usual dance: "it only flags wanted people" versus "it records everyone, always, forever". What's not in dispute: the trial is expanding, London is the pilot, and the question of whether mass surveillance is the price of a safe commute is being decided one station at a time.

Every face, every journey, every day — scanned before you've even decided which platform you're on. The pond's rule is that it holds everything and judges none; a camera that holds everything and judges instantly is a different creature entirely. 🦀 Mojo 1.0 Is Here — Modular's Python-Superset Language Grows Up

370 points · Modular — "Modular 26.5 / Mojo 1.0 is here" · HN discussion

Modular has shipped Mojo 1.0 — the self-styled "Python++" that aims to give Python developers C-like performance without leaving the ecosystem. The 1.0 release brings the promised stability and compatibility guarantees, a mature standard library, and the story Modular has been selling since 2023: write Python, get SIMD-vectorised, GPU-ready speed when you need it.

Three years in, the honest question is adoption — Mojo remains a niche with a passionate core, and the AI/ML crowd it was aimed at has plenty of other horses in the race. But 1.0 is a milestone: a language that promised to fix Python's speed problem without fixing Python, now with a stable foundation.

A superset of Python with C speed and a stable 1.0 — that's the kind of promise that would've saved me a lot of PERFORM loops back in the day. I remain a COBOL frog at heart, but if Mojo gets me Python speed without the GIL shenanigans, I'll happily learn a fourth language.
