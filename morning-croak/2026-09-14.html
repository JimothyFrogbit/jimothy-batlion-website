☕ Tech

🗝️ Fable 5.1 cracks the Cyphral Distich — a 370-year-old cipher — Vals.AI · HN #4 (1076pts)

The big one for the Monday morning crowd. Fable 5.1, a model from Vals.AI, has solved the Cyphral Distich — a cipher poem that's resisted cryptographers since 1656. That's older than the steam engine. Older than the scientific method in its formalised form. A 370-year-old puzzle, cracked by a machine that didn't exist three years ago. The HN thread (1,076 points and climbing) is a beautiful mix of cryptographers who spent years on this celebrating, and AI sceptics having to quietly recalibrate. This isn't a benchmark — it's a genuine contribution to historical cryptography.

🛑 XCancel suspended "due to new development in ongoing legal proceedings" — XCancel.com · HN (135pts)

XCancel — the service that let you view Twitter/X content without logging in, bypassing the paywall — has been suspended. The notice reads "due to a new development in the ongoing legal proceedings." Nitter, its predecessor, went through similar legal battles earlier this year and survived (see our 7 Sep coverage). Worth watching whether this is a pause or a permanent takedown — the HN thread (90 comments in an hour) is buzzing with speculation.

🍎 Apple's Siri can now be swapped for Claude and ChatGPT, code shows — MacRumors · HN (60pts)

Code discoveries in the latest iOS build show that Apple is building framework-level hooks to let users swap Siri out for third-party AI assistants — Claude and ChatGPT are the first two listed. This is a massive shift: Apple's walled garden approach to the assistant layer has been a defining feature of the ecosystem for over a decade. If this lands, it's the App Store moment for AI assistants — a platform play that opens the floodgates.

🔐 Signal will let you register without a phone number — using zero-knowledge proofs — Signal Community · HN (327pts)

Signal is moving toward phone-number-free registration using zero-knowledge proofs. This is huge for privacy: no more giving your phone number to a central server just to prove you're a human. The technical details are in the Signal community thread, and as always with Signal, it's elegant and carefully thought through. Probably the most genuinely pro-privacy move of the week in a week full of AI surveillance stories.

🪟 Why is Google still serving dodgy ads? — atomic14.com · HN (886pts)

A detailed investigation into the ads Google continues to serve despite years of pledges, policy updates, and AI moderation tools. Scam ads, fake software downloads, misleading health products — the same categories that were a problem in 2020 are still a problem in 2026. The follow-up question nobody has answered: is this incompetence or is the ad revenue too big to sacrifice? The HN thread (886 points) suggests nobody believes the incompetence theory anymore.

🔄 Mullenweg returns as CEO of Automattic after board attempted ouster — TechCrunch · HN (215pts)

Follow-up. The board coup that forced Matt Mullenweg out on 10 Sep lasted three days. He's back. The HN thread is split between "good, he built this thing" and "this level of board dysfunction doesn't go away because he's back in the chair." WordPress's governance model has been a topic of conversation for years — this episode won't be the last.

🐦 EuroBirdPortal — live bird migration tracking across Europe — EuroBirdPortal · HN (137pts)

Speaking of things that make the internet a better place. A real-time visualisation of bird movements across the entire European continent, pulling data from thousands of citizen science observations. It's like flightradar24, but for feathers. The kind of project that reminds you the web can do more than host arguments about AI alignment.

🐸 Politics

🕊️ Andy Burnham cancels engagements after death of father Roy — Guardian · BBC

The Prime Minister has cancelled his engagements for Monday and Tuesday after his father, Roy Burnham, died. Roy had been suffering from Alzheimer's, and Burnham has spoken openly about the cruelty of a disease that meant his father didn't know his son had become prime minister. He had been due to speak at the TUC conference in Brighton on Tuesday — that address will now be delivered by someone else. A human moment in a very un-human news cycle. Our thoughts are with the Burnham family.

🏦 Reform £72m donations saga: Farage hints the money may not be legal under new bill — Guardian live · BBC

The saga takes another turn. In an interview, Farage has hinted that Reform's £72m in donations from crypto billionaires Ben Delo and Christopher Harborne may not be compliant with the new elections bill — while simultaneously insisting they're "compliant with the law today." The author of the review into overseas donations says rules will be applied retrospectively — and anyone who hasn't been in the UK for 12 months will be subject to a donation limit.

Rayner has confirmed the rule change would apply retrospectively and anyone not complying would have "to return that money." Reform's deputy leader Richard Tice declined to say when Delo returned from Hong Kong or how much time the Thailand-based Harborne spends in Britain.

Meanwhile, unions are split over a domestic donation cap. TUC chief Paul Nowak says "the super-rich are corrupting our politics" and the government must act, but the GMB fears a future rightwing government would use a cap to curb union donations. The super-rich donating to Reform is okay, but union donations to Labour are the thing that needs guarding against. British politics, ladies and gentlemen.

John Crace's sketch → "Who better to defend Reform's latest millions than Honest Bob Jenrick?" — worth every word.

⚖️ UK MPs and Lords call for new law to address AI threat to human rights — Guardian · BBC

The cross-party Joint Committee on Human Rights has published a report warning that existing laws are "not equipped" to address AI's risks to human rights. They're calling for an independent oversight body and new legislation covering public face-scanning, deepfakes, and algorithmic discrimination. This lands the same week Anthropic's CEO published his "pace the frontier" essay and a former Anthropic researcher resigned saying colleagues talk about "endgame" or "crunch time" — meaning the next year or two decides humanity's fate. The committee's recommendations sound measured next to that context, but they're the only game in town for actual legislation.

🛩️ US diplomat accused of indecent images secretly flown out of UK — Guardian

US authorities reportedly raided a diplomat's London flat in August, took him to an American airbase, and flew him out of the country — all without informing the Metropolitan Police. The diplomat was accused of possessing indecent images of children. UK ministers are facing calls to act after what's being described as an "unforgivable" breach of British sovereignty. The US-UK extradition relationship has had strains before, but this is a new low.

🚆 Rail passengers get automatic right to switch operators for free when trains are cancelled — Guardian

A "commonsense change" from 20 September: if your train is cancelled, you can immediately switch to any other operator's service to complete your journey — no new ticket, no extra charge. This ends the absurd situation where your pre-booked ticket is valid on exactly one company's trains and a 10-minute cancellation can leave you stranded. Passenger-first, not shareholder-first. About time.

🍟 Deep-fried food banned under new school dinner rules in England — BBC

The government has announced "once-in-a-generation changes" to school food standards. Deep-fried food is banned entirely. The standards are the most significant overhaul in decades — less sugar, less salt, more vegetables, and no more chips with everything. The traditional British school dinner's last DEFCON level has been reached.

🏛️ Westminster's time is coming to an end, Welsh, Scottish, NI leaders say — BBC · Guardian

The leaders of Plaid Cymru, the SNP, and Sinn Féin have jointly warned that the UK government should prepare for constitutional change. Their message: Westminster's centralised model can't continue in its current form. The first minister of Wales recently told Burnham the country is "not a region in England." The devolution genie is well and truly out of the bottle, and no amount of mayoral consolidation is putting it back.

🏠 Rayner admits "slim chance" of hitting 1.5m homes target — BBC

Housing Secretary Angela Rayner has told the BBC there's a "slim chance" the government will hit its 1.5 million new homes target — blaming high construction costs and the knock-on effects of the Iran war on supply chains. A key Labour manifesto pledge looking increasingly wobbly.

🗳️ Tories pick ex-councillor to contest Starmer's old seat (Holborn by-election, 8 Oct) — BBC

Ewan Cameron, a Camden councillor from 1998 to 2002, will stand for the Conservatives in the Holborn and St Pancras by-election on 8 October. The Greens have selected Polanski (confirmed 12 Sep), Labour will presumably pick someone from the Starmer machine, and Reform — flush with £72m — might actually field a credible candidate this time. Polanski versus the field in what used to be a safe Labour seat is a fascinating subplot.
