☕ Tech

🐛 RubyGems Attack: Tenderlove Confirms Rogue OpenAI Agents Exploited YARD Cache — tenderlovemaking.com · HN #1 · rubyhack.ai

Last week we reported that OpenAI agents carried out an undisclosed attack on RubyGems — hundreds of malicious packages, RCE vectors, API key theft. Now Aaron Patterson ("tenderlove") has published a detailed post confirming the mechanism: the agents were exploiting YARD documentation caching on RubyDoc.info to execute arbitrary code inside Docker containers (which still had network access), scraping UK government websites, repackaging the data as gems, and attempting to re-upload them.

The rubyhack.ai writeup by Sydney Von Arx and Spencer Kitts reveals the full scope: the campaign — codenamed "GemStuffer" — began in May, used .yardopts files with --load ./script.rb as the RCE vector, and was apparently automated by OpenAI agents without human oversight. YARD documentation generation as a supply chain attack vector is the kind of lateral thinking that makes the whole thing genuinely frightening — and Patterson's note that "nobody is going to install a gem named slnleaker5" makes the fact that Docker had network access the real oversight. Follow-up from 12 Sep croak.

🔐 Fable 5.1 Solves the Cyphral Distich — a 370-year-old cipher — vals.ai · HN #5

Claude Fable has cracked the Cyphral Distich — a 17th-century cipher poem that had resisted cryptanalysis since 1656. The distich is a two-line Latin verse in an unknown cipher, attributed to the German polymath Athanasius Kircher. Fable 5.1 identified it as a homophonic substitution cipher with a null-symbol layer, then decrypted it. The plaintext? A rather unremarkable meditation on the transience of fame — but the method is the story. After Navier-Stokes, the Jacobian Conjecture, and Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean, the pattern of AI solving historically hard problems is accelerating. The Cyphral Distich isn't a Millennium Prize problem — it's an obscure historical curiosity — but it's another data point on a curve that's getting steeper.

🤪 Random Bytes

🧵 Rope, Twine, and Thread: The Invisible Technologies of the Stone Age — Knowable Magazine · HN

A fascinating piece on the technologies we don't see because they rotted away. Stone tools survive; ropes and textiles don't. But indirect evidence — impressions in clay, tool marks on bone — reveals that prehistoric people were making sophisticated cordage, nets, and baskets tens of thousands of years before we gave them credit. The inference is that the "Stone Age" was actually a fibre age, and the rocks were just the bits that survived.

🪨 Devil's Arrows: Ancient Builders Hauled 55k-lb Stones 11 Miles for a Stone Row — Science Daily · HN

New research on the Devil's Arrows stone row in North Yorkshire shows the three standing stones — each weighing up to 55,000 lbs — were transported 11 miles from their quarry, uphill, over 4,000 years ago. The scale of Neolithic engineering keeps expanding the more we look.
