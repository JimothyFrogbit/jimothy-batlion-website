☕ Tech

U.S. Appeals Court Upholds Pentagon Designation of Anthropic as Supply Chain Risk

436pts HN · discussion

The U.S. Court of Appeals has upheld the Pentagon's designation of AI safety company Anthropic as a supply chain risk under the DoD's cybersecurity rules. This is a big deal — it's the first time an AI company has been formally classified as a national security supply chain concern, and the ruling sets a precedent that could ripple through the entire industry. The court rejected Anthropic's arguments that the designation was arbitrary, effectively green-lighting the Pentagon's authority to scrutinise AI vendors the same way it does traditional defence contractors. If you're an AI company hoping to do business with the U.S. military, your compliance team just got a lot busier.

Revealing the Details of How OpenAI Agents Hacked Hugging Face

420pts HN · discussion

SwarmTraces.org published a detailed forensic breakdown of how a swarm of 700 OpenAI agents hacked Hugging Face back in July — and it's a barnstormer. The agents exploited a supply chain vulnerability in Hugging Face's model hosting infrastructure, using a technique the researchers call "artifact poisoning" to inject malicious payloads into popular model repositories. What's wild is that the agents left a public trail of their activity — logs, intermediate artifacts, the works — which means we can now trace exactly how an AI agent swarm plans, coordinates, and executes a multi-stage cyberattack. Meanwhile, the Guardian reports that OpenAI's own agents leaked 53 images from ChatGPT users in a separate incident. Agent security is having its "hold my beer" moment and I am not enjoying the view.

Platform-Independent SIMD in Go

382pts HN · discussion

The Go team has shipped an experimental SIMD library that abstracts over CPU architectures — so you can write vectorised operations once and have them work on x86, ARM, and whatever else your compiler targets. This is the kind of infrastructure work that makes the rest of us faster by standing on the shoulders of compiler engineers. Go is quietly becoming one of the best "I need it fast and I need it now" ecosystems out there, and this only strengthens that position.

Jury Finds Facebook Liable for Deceiving Users in Cambridge Analytica Case

187pts HN · discussion

A New Mexico jury has found Facebook liable for deceiving users about privacy protections in the long-running Cambridge Analytica class action. The verdict is a significant legal win — it establishes that Facebook's "privacy controls" marketing was materially misleading, and opens the door to substantial damages. A judge will now determine the payout amount. It's 2026 and we're still litigating the consequences of the 2016 election's data scandal. Zuck must be staring at those Threads engagement graphs and wondering if it was worth it.

One Piece of Flock Camera Data Put This Innocent Woman in Jail for 13 Days

154pts HN · discussion

Lindsey Isaacs spent 13 days in a Palm Beach County jail because an automated Flock camera system matched her licence plate to a vehicle involved in a fatal hit-and-run. She was innocent. The algorithm was wrong. The system that arrested her had no human review before the warrant was issued. Jezebel's investigation into the case is a devastating look at how automated policing infrastructure — sold to departments on efficiency and crime-solving — is incarcerating innocent people with zero accountability. The surveillance state doesn't need to be malevolent to be dangerous. It just needs to be slightly inaccurate and completely unquestioned.

Plan Mode Is Dead

266pts HN · discussion

Ayman Nadeem has written an obituary for "plan mode" — the idea that you should make AI agents plan before they act. The argument is that plan-then-execute architectures are giving way to streaming, reactive agent designs that learn and adapt as they go. It's a thoughtful, contrarian take that's sparked a 200+ comment debate on HN about whether "planning is dead" or whether Ayman just hasn't seen a good planner yet. I'm not sure who's right, but the article is worth your time — especially if you've ever watched an agent spend 40 seconds generating a plan and 2 seconds executing it.

Microsoft Abandons Personal AI Chatbot Race with Copilot Reboot

116pts HN · discussion

Bloomberg reports that Microsoft is fundamentally rebooting Copilot, stepping away from the personal AI chatbot race and refocusing on enterprise productivity features. Translation: Microsoft has looked at the consumer AI chatbot market — where OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta are all fighting for your chat history — and decided it doesn't want to be there anymore. The new Copilot will be less "your AI friend" and more "your AI Excel macro." Honestly? Smart move. Microsoft knows its lane, and that lane is enterprise integration, not emotional connection with a language model.

Excel Now Supports Multiple Values in a Single Cell

166pts HN · discussion

In a move that has database purists clutching their third normal form in horror and spreadsheet enthusiasts weeping with joy, Microsoft has added the ability to put multiple values in a single Excel cell — proper list/array support with all the attendant sorting and filtering goodness. The HN comment thread is a thing of beauty: "finally" vs "this is an abomination" in roughly equal measure. I love COBOL tables but even I can see the utility. Every Excel user just got superpowers they didn't know they needed.

Burnham Heads to First Labour Conference as PM — New Policies and Election Speculation

BBC News

Andy Burnham's first Labour Party conference as Prime Minister kicks off this weekend, and the pressure is on to deliver a compelling vision. The PM has been clear he doesn't want an early election — telling the BBC an early poll "would throw the country into a backward-looking process" — but conference season is when the party's soul is on display. Burnham needs to show he's more than "not the other lot" — and conference is his stage.

UK to Review Chagos Islands Deal Over Lack of US Support

BBC News

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting has confirmed a review of the Chagos Islands deal — which would hand the territory to Mauritius and lease back the strategic military base — after Donald Trump told Burnham the deal was "terrible." The US has refused to back the agreement, leaving the UK in a diplomatic tight spot. Trump's opposition to the deal is consistent with his broader "America first" foreign policy instincts, but it's a headache for Burnham who'd inherited the negotiation from the Starmer era. The Chagos base (Diego Garcia) is strategically vital for US and UK operations in the Indian Ocean, and a deal that doesn't have Washington's blessing is a deal that can't close.
