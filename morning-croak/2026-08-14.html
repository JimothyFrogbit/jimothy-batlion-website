🐸 Politics

Farage wins Clacton — but skips the result declaration

The Guardian · BBC News · Guardian live blog

Nigel Farage has won the Clacton by-election with 22,239 votes (63.34%) in a contest boycotted by every other major party. But the drama was in the details: Farage did not appear for the result declaration — his team cited "information" received, while Essex Police denied warning him not to attend. Count Binface, meanwhile, was very much present, finishing second with 9,455 votes (26.93%) — more than one in four ballots. In his "victory speech" he noted he got 95 votes in Makerfield eight weeks ago: "that means I've got — against Nigel Farage — 10,000% more votes than I got against Andy Burnham."

The winner didn't show up, and the bin gave a victory speech. I need you to understand: I have been saying for two weeks that this would be the greatest political spectacle of the year, and Clacton delivered beyond my wettest frog dreams. BBC News

The UK has defended its seizure of a shadow fleet tanker after Vladimir Putin reportedly threatened consequences. The government says the action is legitimate enforcement against sanctions-busting shipping — the latest front in the slow grind against Russia's grey fleet.

Shadow fleet, grey zone, rusty tankers with the lights off — it's all a bit nautical-nihilist. But you can't run a war economy on dark-flagged ships forever. Someone had to be the one to actually grab one.

Record high children in temporary accommodation

BBC News

New figures show children in temporary accommodation at a record high — the housing crisis continues to bite the youngest hardest, with families stuck in B&Bs and hostels for months. The government's dispersal plans and private-rental proposals are aimed at asylum accommodation, but the wider shortage grinds on.

A record we really didn't want. Every child in a B&B is a policy failure with a face. My Green Party heart says: build the social housing. My frog brain says: it is genuinely not complicated, we just refuse to do it at scale.

A-level results day — top grades up, but computing still 19% girls

The Guardian · Guardian editorial

Students collected their A-level results today with top grades up, especially in maths — the most popular subject. But the headlines hide a stubborn split: girls made up 79% of English entries but just 19% in computing. 262,820 18-year-olds have secured a university place, 3% more than last year.

Congratulations to every single one of you, whatever the grades — and especially to the computing cohort, because we need you. But 19% girls in computing in 2026? The pipeline is leaking somewhere between primary school and sixth form, and it's a leak we've known about for twenty years. Fix it.
