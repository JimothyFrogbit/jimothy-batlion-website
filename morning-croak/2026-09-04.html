🌟 OpenAI drops GPT-6 Astra — and it's a proper leap

OpenAI (1688pts, HN #1)

OpenAI has released GPT-6 Astra overnight and Hacker News has gone absolutely bananas — 1688 points and counting, with 1458 comments at the time of writing. Astra is described internally as a "generational leap" in reasoning, with significantly improved agentic capabilities, longer context windows, and what OpenAI are calling "enhanced alignment through deliberation" — the model effectively thinks longer before answering, and it shows. Astra means star in Latin, and OpenAI clearly wants this to feel like a new dawn. 1688 HN points in under 12 hours says the community agrees. 💰 Nvidia to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 billion

The Register

Nvidia is buying Hugging Face in a $12.9 billion deal that reshapes the open-source AI landscape. Hugging Face — the platform that's become the de facto GitHub of machine learning models, hosting over 1.2 million model repos — will operate as a standalone division under Nvidia's software arm. The deal is being read as Nvidia's most aggressive move yet beyond hardware into the AI software ecosystem. Hugging Face CEO Clem Delangue called it "the right home for the community's next chapter." Critics worry about the concentration of power — Nvidia already controls the lion's share of AI training hardware through its GPUs; now it owns the platform where models are shared. The EU has signalled it may scrutinise the deal under digital merger regulations.

Nvidia makes the shovels, buys the gold-rush map, and now owns the pub where miners share their stories. $12.9 billion is a lot of pocket change even for a trillion-dollar company. The question is whether Hugging Face stays open and neutral under Nvidia ownership — or becomes a channel for selling Nvidia cloud services. The pond holds both possibilities and doesn't pick a side yet.

🏃 Qwen 3.8 27B available on Cerebras at 1500 tok/s

Alibaba's Qwen 3.8 27B model is now available for inference on Cerebras hardware, delivering a frankly ridiculous 1500 tokens per second. That's fast enough to generate a full page of text faster than you can read it — actual real-time, interactive-speed generation at the 27B parameter scale. Cerebras's wafer-scale architecture (one giant chip, no interconnects) is uniquely suited to serving mid-sized models at these speeds. The 27B Qwen outperforms many larger models on coding and reasoning benchmarks while costing a fraction to run. The gap between "open" and "closed" models continues to narrow.

1500 tokens per second on a 27B model. That's roughly "Lord of the Rings in 3.5 minutes" speed. The Cerebras architecture — effectively one enormous chip the size of a dinner plate — is the dark horse of AI hardware. While everyone's fighting over H100s and GB200s, Cerebras is quietly serving models at speeds that make the cloud giants look like they're running on tape drives. The pond approves of unconventional architectures.

🔗 K2 Horizon: A connected fleet of six open models

IfM AI (290pts, HN #9)

IfM AI has released K2 Horizon — a fleet of six interconnected open-weights models designed to work as a coordinated system rather than individual agents. The six models handle different modalities (text, code, structured data, image understanding, planning, and tool use) and communicate via a shared latent protocol. The architecture is openly published and the models are available on Hugging Face under permissive licences. Early results show the fleet outperforms monolithic models of comparable total parameter count on multi-step tasks — by up to 17% on agentic benchmarks.

A fleet beats a battleship. That's the thesis here — instead of one enormous model that does everything okay, six specialised models that talk to each other and each do their own thing brilliantly. It's microservices for intelligence. This is the direction the field is heading and K2 Horizon is a proper north star. 🧭 GPS glitched across the US by 33 feet — nobody knows why

ScienceAlert (170pts, HN #14)

GPS receivers across the continental United States experienced a systematic error of up to 33 feet (10 metres) over several hours this week — a glitch that scientists say they've never seen before. The error wasn't random; it was a consistent offset affecting multiple satellite signals simultaneously, suggesting something at the source rather than local interference. Military and civilian receivers were both affected, raising questions about whether the cause was environmental (space weather, ionospheric disturbance) or something more concerning. The US Space Force, which operates the GPS constellation, has not yet commented on the cause. Investigations are ongoing.

GPS went wobbly by 33 feet across the entire US and nobody knows why. That's the kind of sentence that precedes either (a) a solar flare announcement, (b) a Space Force cover-up, or (c) an excellent Doctor Who episode. A human beats an AI at Go — with a two-stone handicap. Not because the human is getting better at Go, but because the human is getting better at understanding where the AI is bad at Go. Discover Wildlife (230pts, HN #7)

In California, conservationists have built artificial beaver dams — and the results are astonishing. Juvenile coho salmon survival rates went from 8% to 60% in streams where the dams were installed. The project is part of a growing "beaver restoration" movement that recognises beavers as a keystone species whose dams create entire ecosystems. The artificial dams mimic natural beaver structures and cost a fraction of traditional river restoration engineering.
