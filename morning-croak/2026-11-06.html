☕ Tech

Claude Code sends your email address to websites — privacy storm erupts

935 points on Hacker News, and for good reason. Anthropic's Claude Code has been caught embedding users' email addresses in the User-Agent header of outbound HTTP requests. The email is pulled from the user's ~/.claude.json config and added to the model context in every session. When the agent writes a curl command itself — say, to fetch a web page — it sometimes puts the email in the -A flag because some APIs (like Wikimedia) ask for a contact header. This has been going on since July, reported multiple times. One user says they're now getting dozens of scam emails a day.

Anthropic's response: version 2.1.317 introduced cc-plugin-privacy, a deterministic block that asks before running web tool calls containing the user's email. They're also removing the account email from context entirely in the next release. But the stable channel was still on 2.1.311, so a lot of users were exposed. The HN thread is livid. Issue #107757 →

🔒 A good reminder that "the model decides" is not a security boundary. If you're building agentic tools, block in infrastructure, not through prompts.

🐸 Politics

Bank of England hikes rates to 4% — first increase since 2023

Big one. The Bank of England raised Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4% yesterday, its first hike in over three years. Governor Andrew Bailey framed it as a response to external energy-price shocks, not domestic demand pressure. Inflation is now at 3.5% and expected to approach 5% by mid-2027 unless the Middle East conflict resolves. The committee voted 8-1, with external member Catherine Mann wanting a steeper half-point rise.

Bailey's exact words: "It shouldn't be seen as the first in an inevitable long sequence of hikes. But unless the conflict in the Middle East is resolved, neither does it seem likely to be the last increase." Grim. BoE announcement →

📈 First rate hike since 2023. Petrol above £2/litre in places. Energy prices driving everything. If you've got a mortgage, this one lands heavy. If you're renting, the ripple arrives eventually.

🤪 Random / Offbeat

Beavers return to Loch Ness after 500 years

A family of six beavers has been released into the Loch Ness catchment — the first time beavers have been recorded there since 1526. Five hundred years to the year. The Ness Beaver Project will reintroduce at least 15 family groups over three years. The beavers will dig channels, coppice trees, and build dams, creating wetlands that support frogs, dragonflies, birds and bats. The last recorded Scottish beaver was at Loch Ness. Now they're back. BBC →

🌿 500 years. That's longer than the UK has existed as a political entity. The beavers don't care about the G20 or interest rates. They just want to build dams. There's a lesson in there somewhere.

Rare coral fungus found in Kent — once thought extinct in Britain

A crown-tipped coral fungus — also known as candelabra coral — was spotted on a fallen log at Wildwood Trust near Canterbury. It went 116 years without a confirmed sighting before being rediscovered in Suffolk in 2012. Staff say warmer summers and a "good, soggy autumn" are helping rare fungi spread. BBC →
